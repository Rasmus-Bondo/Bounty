# SettingsController

Populates and drives the reworked settings panel and owns its open/close. Single-script replacement for the legacy `SettingsGui` (which split this across `SettingsPanelClient` + `SettingsButtonClient`), repointed at the `ReworkedSettingsGui.Canvas.Background.Content` layout.

## What it does
1. **Open/close:** the `ReworkedSettingsGui` ScreenGui stays `Enabled`; the panel is shown/hidden by toggling `Canvas.Visible` + `Backdrop.Visible`. A top-right gear `SettingsButton` (44x44, recreated at the legacy position) toggles it; `Canvas.Background.CloseFrame.Button` closes it. `DisplayOrder` is raised to 10 only while open (to sit above the menu's `DisplayOrder` 1) and dropped back to 0 when closed, so the gear stays in the top-right gap and is covered by full-screen teleport / loading overlays instead of poking through
2. **Intro-gated reveal:** the gear starts hidden (authored `Visible = false`) and only fades in once the intro/loading screen finishes — it waits on the `BountyIntroComplete` attribute on `ReplicatedStorage` (set by LoadingScreen after the camera pan to the lobby), then tweens `Image`/`Background` transparency in over 0.4s so it loads in with the rest of the menu rather than during the sky shot. A 12s `task.delay` fallback reveals it if that flag is ever missed, so settings can't become permanently unreachable
3. **Backdrop:** an oversized full-screen `Active` `Backdrop` frame behind the panel dims the menu and blocks click-through. Its `InputBegan` dismisses settings **only when the press lands outside the panel's absolute rect** (`pointInPanel` guard against `Canvas.Background`). This is deliberate: non-`Active` panel frames — notably the sliders — let a mouse-down fall through to the backdrop underneath, so an unconditional close would let a slider drag dismiss the panel. `Canvas.Background` is also flagged `Active` as a second line of defense
4. Generic slider driver (`bindSlider`) reused for mouse sensitivity, crosshair size, and all four volume sliders — one drag/release model. `setSliderVisual` insets the fill's leading edge by half the handle width so the golden bar never overhangs the track ends, then places the round handle a few px left of that tip (`HANDLE_LEFT_NUDGE`) so it reads as sitting on the end of the bar rather than poking a half handle-width past it
5. **Camera:** mouse sensitivity slider maps `[0.05, 1]` to `UserInputService.MouseDeltaSensitivity`; loads saved value via `MouseSensitivityRequest`/`Response`, saves on release via `SetMouseSensitivity`
6. **Crosshair color:** 8 preset swatches (colors + `SwatchIndex` attributes baked into the GUI); clicking sets `Player:SetAttribute("CrosshairColor", Color3)` so it carries into Redwater Canyon. Selection ring toggled via `UIStroke.Thickness`. Swatches are sized as a scale fraction of the row (not fixed px) so all 8 always fit within the panel on any screen
7. **Crosshair size:** slider maps 0-1 fraction to `[0.5, 2.5]` multiplier; sets `Player:SetAttribute("CrosshairSize", number)`
8. **HUD Show Ping / FPS:** creates a `PingFPSGui` `ScreenGui` with a bottom-left `TextLabel`; `RunService.Heartbeat` sampler averages FPS over 0.5s windows. The chip **measures** its text rather than predicting it: `AutomaticSize.X` plus symmetric `UIPadding`, so the dark plate is always exactly as wide as the current string (the old fixed 140×22 box was a guess at the widest `PING nnn | FPS nnn`, and the readout overran its own backing). Text size scales off a 720p baseline clamped to `[12, 20]`, with the plate height and side padding derived from it, and re-fits on `Camera.ViewportSize` changes. One `buildPingFpsLabel` / `sizePingFpsLabel` pair serves both the startup path and `ensurePingFpsOverlay`'s rebuild
8a. **Touch devices take a further ×0.65 cut with a lower floor of 7** (`PING_FPS_MOBILE_TEXT_SCALE` / `PING_FPS_MOBILE_MIN_TEXT_SIZE`, added 2026-07-30). The 720p baseline alone never reached any phone — `12 × 400/720` is 6.7, so every handset clamped back up to the floor of 12 and a 360-tall Galaxy drew the identical chip to a 720p monitor, the same "floor sets phone sizes" bug VersionTagController was tuned out of twice. Measured effect: every phone goes text 12 → 7, plate 113×22 → 68×13 (a 64% cut in area), tablets scale genuinely (iPad 10in 14 → 9, 13in 17 → 11), and desktop is unchanged at **every** height tested (480/529/614/720/768/1080/1440) because the gate is `TouchEnabled`, not viewport size
8b. **Tuned down twice — and the floor, not the multiplier, is the knob that moves phones.** The first pass (×0.8, floor 9) was a real reduction on paper — 12 → 9, plate 22 → 17 — and still came back from a device test as "did not get smaller", which is the honest result: 3px off a 12px stamp is a change you can see in a diff and not in the hand. Because every phone clamps to the floor (`12 × 400/720` = 6.7 is far below it), the multiplier only ever shapes **tablets** — dropping 0.8 to 0.65 without also lowering the floor would have changed nothing at all on a phone. 7 matches what the version tag settled on for the same reason; below it glyphs stop resolving cleanly at phone pixel densities. Raise this floor first if the readout comes back unreadable
8c. **`TouchEnabled` is read inside `pingFpsTextSize`, and a late flip is explicitly re-run.** Studio's device emulator flips the property a beat *after* client scripts start, and the mobile branch is gated on it — so without a re-run the chip is built at the desktop size and simply stays there, which looks exactly like the mobile cut never having been applied. The existing resizes only fire on viewport / probe changes and a late flip is neither, so a `GetPropertyChangedSignal("TouchEnabled")` connection (in a `pcall`) plus one `task.defer` pass cover it
8d. **The 8px left gap is conditional on the screen actually having a cutout to clear.** `PingFPSGui` sets `IgnoreGuiInset = true`, which is the legacy spelling of `ScreenInsets.DeviceSafeInsets` (verified by read-back, not assumed). On a notched handset in landscape Roblox reserves a device-safe band on *both* short edges even though only one carries the cutout — measured at 62px on an iPhone 16 Pro Max — so the chip rendered 62 + 8 = 70px in from the physical edge; on a phone with no cutout that reserve is 0 and the same 8px was a plain margin against bare glass. `sizePingFpsLabel` now measures the band live from two throwaway probe ScreenGuis (one `None`, one `DeviceSafeInsets`) and drops the gap to 0 when it reads 0, since there is no API that answers "is this screen notched". Verified: iPhone 7 → 0px from the physical edge, iPhone 16 Pro Max → 70px (unchanged, still clear of the cutout)
8e. **X only, and touch only.** The Y delta between those same two probes is the topbar offset, and reading it is the mistake that shipped twice in VersionTagController — it measures 0 in Studio, so a wrong value looks correct until it is on a handset; the chip is anchored to the bottom edge, which no inset here moves. The flush behaviour is also gated on `TouchEnabled`, because a monitor likewise reports no inset but none of the edge-space reasoning applies to it — desktop keeps its 8px. The probe reading is sticky (only a `> 0` delta overwrites it): a disabled `ScreenGui` stops laying out and its descendants' `AbsolutePosition` collapses to 0, which is indistinguishable from a genuine "no notch". Failing to 0 is the safe direction — flush is what an un-notched phone wants anyway, and it is off by at most 8px on a notched one for the frame or two before layout lands
8f. The `ViewportSize` listener is **rebound, not stacked** — `CurrentCamera` is replaced outright by the menu / skin-preview cameras and by respawns, so the previous connection is dropped before a new one is made rather than leaving a live listener on every camera the client has ever had. Redwater's copy carries the same guard, where it matters more (kill cams, spectating, the intermission orbit)
8g. **HUD Ability Descriptions:** a three-mode segmented row (`ON` / `DEFAULT` / `OFF`) under the HUD header, persisted as the `AbilityDescriptions` setting. It controls **Redwater's** hunter ability tags — the lobby has no ability HUD, so nothing here reacts to the value; it exists so a player can configure it before queueing, and Redwater's own SettingsController publishes it to its intro client as a player attribute. The row is **two lines** — label above, three full-width buttons below — because a fixed-width three-button control does not fit: at a 705x338 viewport the row is 165px wide and a 176px control drove the label to a negative width and made it vanish. The active button uses the same `TOGGLE_ON_COLOR` the boolean toggles use, and the row is cloned from `ShowPingFPSRow` at build time so its styling cannot drift
8h. Unlike every other control, this one is **not** given a safe default before the server responds — Redwater treats a missing attribute as "show nothing", and an optimistic guess would flash the tags at a player who turned them **Off**. The fallback runs only after the request retries are exhausted
8i. **INPUT Auto Fire (2026-08-07):** a boolean toggle row (`AutoFireRow`, cloned in Studio from `ShowPingFPSRow`) under its own `INPUTHeader` between the CROSSHAIR and HUD groups, persisted as the `AutoFireEnabled` setting (default **true**). It controls **Redwater's** mobile auto-fire — nothing in the lobby consumes it; the `FireServer` write is what matters, since the profile is shared and a player who flips the toggle here must arrive in a match with it honoured (the attribute is still set for symmetry with Redwater's controller). The row and its header are hidden on non-touch devices (`UIS.TouchEnabled`, re-polled for ~12s because Studio's device emulator flips the property late); the `UIListLayout` collapses them so no gap is left. Seeded `applyAutoFire(true)` alongside the other defaults and re-applied from the `PlayerSettingsResponse` payload
9. **Graphics Shadows:** toggles `Lighting.GlobalShadows`; default PC = on, mobile = off (detected via `TouchEnabled`, used only if server returns nil)
10. **Audio:** four sliders drive `SoundService.SFX.Volume`, `SoundService.Music.Volume`, `SoundService.HitSound.Volume` with a master multiplier applied on top of each group
11. **Codes:** a `CodeTextBox` + `RedeemButton` fire `RedeemCodeRequest`; `FeedbackLabel` shows the success/failure message from `RedeemCodeResponse`
12. **Load flow:** applies safe defaults immediately, then fires `PlayerSettingsRequest` (retry x5, 1s apart) until `PlayerSettingsResponse` arrives — server also pushes proactively on `PlayerAdded`
13. **Save flow:** every control fires `SetPlayerSetting(key, value)` on release/click
14. **Responsive scaling:** a `PanelScale` `UIScale` (created under `Canvas` at startup) scales the whole panel uniformly toward a 1280×720 baseline — `math.min(viewport.X/1280, viewport.Y/720)` clamped to `[0.9, 1]`, re-applied on `Camera.ViewportSize` changes — so the panel's fixed-pixel controls (text, sliders, swatches, padding) stay proportional and don't overflow on small / mobile viewports. Desktop (≥ baseline) is unchanged at scale 1.0; the mobile floor is `0.9` (raised from an earlier `0.6`) so the panel renders ~50% larger and stays legible on phones instead of shrinking to a hard-to-read size. The panel's decorative `UIStroke`s (outer outline, header box, and the inner content-border rectangle) render proportional to this `UIScale`, so each stroke's authored thickness is divided by the current scale on every update — keeping its on-screen weight constant so the inner rectangle stays fully drawn instead of thinning to just its top edge on phones
15. **Responsive height**, set in the same pass and for the opposite reason to the scale above: the `Canvas`'s Y size is interpolated from the authored `0.6` at the 720p baseline up to `0.78` on a ~420px-tall viewport (`0.88` was the first attempt and overshot — it read as filling the screen rather than sitting on it). The rows are fixed-pixel and the `UIScale` floor stops them shrinking past `0.9`, so at a flat `0.6` a phone showed two or three settings at a time. Width is left as authored — a full-width vertical list is only ever short of vertical room. Interpolated on viewport height rather than branched on `TouchEnabled` (the device emulator flips that flag after client scripts start, and a small windowed desktop wants the room too); at `0.78` the `Background` (which is `1.038` of the canvas and hangs slightly low of centre) spans about 9%-81% of the screen at the `0.9` scale floor, leaving a visible margin top and bottom
