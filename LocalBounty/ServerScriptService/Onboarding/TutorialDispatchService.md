# TutorialDispatchService

Decides who gets OFFERED the onboarding tutorial, and dispatches the ones who accept to a private reserved Redwater server (design 5.1).

## What it does

1. On profile load (polled up to 30s after join), evaluates the dispatch conditions — `Tutorial.Completed == false`, `Tutorial.Attempts < 2`, profile present, and **no** TeleportData at all on arrival (which excludes returns from Redwater, parties, and forwarded friend/follow joins in one test) — then fires `TutorialInvite` with `{ cash, tokens, chest }` and **waits for an answer**. Nothing is written to the profile at this point: a player shown the card who quits before answering has neither accepted nor declined, and the offer is waiting on their next join.
1b. **The offer is the trigger again as of 2026-08-17, and the tutorial is no longer mandatory.** It was server-driven and unskippable from 2026-08-11 until then, for a reason that was real: routing a teleport through the client makes it depend on a LocalScript having connected its listener before the server fired, and a RemoteEvent with no listener is silently dropped — so losing that race meant the tutorial simply never happened, with nothing anywhere saying so. The fix is the **`"__request"` pull** in item 3a, not the removal of the choice. The other objection, that the old 4s auto-advance sliver was a window in which a first-timer could decline by accident, is answered by the card having no timer at all: only its two buttons close it.
1f. **What the invite carries.** `REWARD_CASH` (250) and `REWARD_TOKENS` (50) at the top of this module are the lobby's copy of Redwater's `RoundSystem.Config.TUTORIAL_COMPLETE_CASH` / `TUTORIAL_COMPLETE_TOKENS`, which is where the payout actually happens. A place cannot require another place's `Config` and two integers do not earn a nineteenth universe-wide mirrored module, so this is a deliberate second home — **nothing detects drift between the two**, the card just oversells or undersells the lesson. Change them in the same pass. The card itself hardcodes neither number; it renders what arrives.
1g. **The chest half of the invite is computed per account, not constant** (2026-09-07). `offerPayload(player)` adds `chest`, true only while `profile.Lootboxes.TutorialChestPaid` is unset — the tutorial super chest is one per account and TutorialDirector burns that flag in Redwater when it banks. So a replaying test account, or anyone whose earlier run reached the outro, is offered the two currencies alone rather than a chest that will not be paid. Unlike the two currency figures this needs no second home: the flag it reads is on the profile, which both places share. An absent profile reads as "not yet paid", which is the right answer for the player this card is for and unreachable in practice, since the invite waits the profile out first.
1g. **`dispatch` is still forward-declared** (`local dispatch`, defined below `evaluatePlayer`) even though its only caller, `onResponse`, is now defined *after* it. Without the declaration `function dispatch` further down declares a **global**, and the next caller added above that line resolves to nil and errors at the exact moment a first-time player joins — which it has already done once on live.
1a. **The test gate that sat in front of all of that is now OFF** (opened up 2026-08-11, after the whole flow was driven end to end on live — on PC and on a phone — from the listed account). While `TEST_ACCOUNTS_ONLY` was `true`, only accounts in the `TEST_ACCOUNTS` table (keyed on UserId, `4284873730` = VorFader) were invited and **a genuinely new player got nothing**, dropping into the normal lobby instead; it existed so the flow could be driven on live without putting a first-timer through a tutorial that had never been signed off. With it `false`, who gets dispatched is decided by the profile conditions in item 1 and by nothing else. Two asymmetries worth knowing before it is ever switched back on: a listed account is **exempt from the once-only pair** (`Completed` / `Attempts`), so the invite comes back on every fresh join and the lesson can be re-run without wiping a profile; but it is **not** exempt from the TeleportData rule, because the return leg carries `tutorialComplete` and re-inviting on it would stack the invite card over the completion card and loop straight back in. `Attempts` still increments, so telemetry stays honest. The gate is re-checked in `dispatch` as well as `shouldInvite`, since `onResponse` only consults `invited[]`.
1e. **The once-only exemption is now its own switch, `TEST_ACCOUNTS_REPLAY`** (2026-08-16, `true`). It exempts the listed accounts from the `Completed` / `Attempts` pair while leaving dispatch open to everyone else, so a listed account re-runs the lesson on every fresh join without a profile wipe. Both were previously welded to `TEST_ACCOUNTS_ONLY`, which meant the only way to replay the lesson was to stop inviting every real first-time player — you could not verify onboarding without switching it off for the people it exists for. The two restriction sites (`shouldInvite`, `dispatch`) test `TEST_ACCOUNTS_ONLY` **directly** rather than through `isTestAccount`, which is what makes the replay switch safe to ship enabled: a true from `isTestAccount` can never narrow dispatch on its own. It does **not** exempt anyone from the TeleportData rule, so the return leg from Redwater still lands in the lobby — a rejoin from the app is what re-triggers. **Its old cost is gone as of 2026-08-17**: it used to mean a listed account could not reach the normal lobby on a fresh join, because it was dispatched into the lesson instead, and now the account simply declines the card and carries on into the menu. Leaving it on costs only a card to dismiss on every join, which is still not something to ship — it is `true` again for verifying the offer card and belongs on the pre-publish checklist. **What holds the live player base out of the tutorial now is DataService's grandfather rule, not this switch:** `Reconcile` added `Tutorial` to every existing profile with `Completed = false`, and `onPlayerAdded` silently marks any profile carrying MatchesPlayed / TotalPlaytime / Level > 1 / XP as completed — and does it *before* `profiles[player]` is assigned, so `getPlayerData`, which is what `evaluatePlayer` polls, can never see an ungrandfathered veteran. An existing account that has genuinely never played is dispatched, which is the intent.
1c. **Following a friend defers the decision** (`FollowUserId ~= 0`, 2026-08-11; changed from a flat refusal to a wait on 2026-08-13). A follow-join is a fresh join with `Player.FollowUserId` set, **not** a teleport, so it carries no TeleportData and the arrival rule in item 1 cannot see it. Two services want the player at once: [[FollowJoinService]] forwards them to the friend's match while this one would reserve a tutorial server. Whichever teleport loses, the attempt is already spent — `dispatch` increments `Attempts` before teleporting and `refundAttempt` only rolls back failures this server observed, never a player who left — so two invitations from friends could disqualify an account at `Attempts >= 2` without it ever seeing a step; the other branch pulls them out of the match they were invited to. `shouldInvite` therefore calls `FollowJoinService.waitForOutcome(player, 20s)` and returns false unless the answer is `"failed"`. When the forward succeeds nothing is written, so the lesson is still waiting on their next ordinary join — which only works because DataService's grandfather screen is one-time (see its item 13a), since the match they are about to play would otherwise mark them a veteran.
1d. **Why it waits rather than refuses.** The flat refusal was right whenever the forward worked and quietly awful when it did not. A brand-new player who taps Join on a friend who is *themselves* mid-tutorial got neither path: FollowJoinService cannot forward into a tutorial server (it publishes no directory entry), and this module had already disqualified them — so they landed cold in the full lobby menu with a card saying their friend's match had ended, which is the exact drop the onboarding flow exists to prevent, and two new friends inviting each other is an ordinary way to start this game. The collision in 1c is still impossible because `waitForOutcome` answers `"failed"` only once FollowJoinService has genuinely given up, by which point its `pending` entry is cleared and no retry of its own can fire; a **timeout is not a failure** and leaves the player alone. On claiming them `evaluatePlayer` calls `FollowJoinService.dismissOverlay(player)` before firing the invite — kept out of `shouldInvite`, which stays a pure predicate — so the "couldn't join" card does not sit for five seconds saying they are staying in the lobby immediately before teleporting them out of it. The 20s wait is matched to FollowJoinClient's own `SAFETY_TIMEOUT`.
2. `dispatch` re-verifies against the profile, increments `Tutorial.Attempts` and stamps `StartedAt` **before** `TeleportAsync` (two crash-outs stop the auto-send), reserves a private server via `MatchmakingService.reserveTutorialServer()`, mints the tutorial funnel session id (`Telemetry.newSessionId`), and teleports with `ReservedServerAccessCode` plus TeleportData `{ tutorial = true, redwaterAccessCode, tutorialFunnelId, characterSelections = Dust default }`. No `groupId`, no `members` — a tutorial player is not a party.
3. `onResponse` is the whole decision point again (2026-08-17). It handles three actions, all gated on `invited[player]`: `"start"` dispatches, `"skip"` declines, and `"__request"` re-sends the invite. `awaitingChoice[player]` is set beside `invited[player]` when the offer goes out and **cleared before either branch acts**, so a double-tap cannot reserve two private servers or write the profile twice, and a stale client answering after the fact is a no-op rather than an error.
3a. **`"__request"` is the pull half of a join-state handshake, and it is what made the choice safe to restore.** A `FireClient` at a client whose listener has not connected yet is dropped silently — the failure that got the choice removed in the first place — so TutorialWelcomeController connects its listener and *then* asks for its invite, rather than us assuming the push landed. Answered only while `awaitingChoice` is true, which is what stops the pull re-raising a card over a player already mid-teleport or resurrecting one they declined. Because the client connects before it asks, either ordering of push and pull works and the card appears exactly once.
3b. **The decline ladder: "skip for now" means exactly that, once.** `decline` stamps `Tutorial.Skipped` on the FIRST skip, which leaves the profile eligible, so the offer and the reward it names come back on the next join; the SECOND skip stamps `Completed` / `CompletedAt`, the same terminal every other route out of onboarding uses, and `shouldInvite` refuses from then on. Two showings, then never again. Deliberately **not** a new `Declines` counter: the existing `Skipped` bool already tells a first decline from a second, and a new key in that sub-table would have to be reconciled into three places' profile templates for a number nothing reads. **`Attempts` is untouched** — an attempt is a *dispatch*, and a decliner was never sent anywhere, so charging one would spend the retry ladder's budget on a decision and would make DataService's grandfather rule read them as "already in the flow". In practice the cap rarely bites: a decliner who plays one real match is grandfathered `Completed` on their next join anyway, and since 2026-08-17 the reward is still reachable afterwards through TutorialReplayService's CHANGE MODE entry, so a permanent decline no longer strands it.
4. Every failure (ReserveServer, teleport) fires `TutorialInvite("failed")` back so the card closes to the normal lobby. Failing into the lobby is fine; failing into a real match is not.
5. **Retry ladder** (2026-08-11). This was the only teleport in Bounty without one, and it had three failure shapes. `reserveWithRetry` gives `MatchmakingService.reserveTutorialServer` 3 tries with a growing delay — it is a web call and the likeliest transient failure. `attemptTeleport` gives `TeleportAsync` `MAX_TELEPORT_ATTEMPTS` (3), building a **fresh** `TeleportOptions` each time (one handed to `TeleportAsync` must not be reused) and reusing the **same access code and funnel id** — the reserved server is already made, and re-reserving would strand it and mint a second funnel session for what is one run. Constants copied from FriendJoinService / FollowJoinService so all four teleport paths in this place behave the same under the same Roblox failure.
5a. **The third failure shape reached nothing at all before this.** `TeleportAsync` returning cleanly is not proof the player left; the teleport can still die during initiation, which surfaces on `TeleportService.TeleportInitFailed` — and this module never connected it. The three services that do all filter to their own senders (PartyService on `teleportRetryAttempts`, FriendJoin/FollowJoin on their own `pending` tables), so a tutorial teleport that died at init produced **no retry, no `"failed"` to the client, and no warn**: the player simply stayed in the lobby with an attempt spent. The handler now gates on this module's own `pending` table for the same reason. Retryable set is `Failure` / `Flooded` / `GameFull` / `GameEnded` / `GameNotFound` / `IsTeleporting`; `Unauthorized` is terminal because no retry fixes a permissions problem. `IsTeleporting` has to be in the *set* and not only in the `BACKOFF_DELAY` branch — left out, that branch is unreachable and the most transient of the eight results gives up immediately.
5b. **`Attempts` is refunded on a failure this server observed** (`refundAttempt`). The increment stays before `TeleportAsync` — a crash mid-lesson is unobservable from here, so the count has to assume the worst — but counting failures that happened *in Bounty* turned two Roblox hiccups into a permanent disqualification, since `dispatch` refuses at `Attempts >= 2` and the grandfather rule reads `Attempts > 0` as "already in the flow". `giveUp` restores the pre-dispatch `Attempts` and `StartedAt`, guarded by a `refunded` flag and by a re-read (it only rolls back if the stored value is still `prior + 1`, so a future writer cannot be silently overwritten into handing out extra runs). Deliberately **not** called from `PlayerRemoving`: leaving mid-ladder is either a teleport that worked or a quit, and neither is an observed failure. `dispatchFailed[player]` makes a give-up terminal for the session so a client re-firing `"start"` cannot hit ReserveServer again on every press.
6. **Fires `NewPlayer` funnel step 4, "Returned To Lobby"** (2026-08-15). `noteTutorialReturn` runs on every `PlayerAdded` alongside `evaluatePlayer` and fires the step when the arrival's TeleportData carries `tutorialComplete`. That arrival is the only server-side evidence the round trip actually closed: TutorialDirector pays and stamps the profile in Redwater, but the player still has to *land* in Bounty for the completion card and the lobby to mean anything, and a quit at the reward card is a real drop. It is deliberately **not** inside `shouldInvite`, which returns false at `tut.Completed` long before it ever reads the join data. Gated on step 3 having genuinely fired in Redwater, like every step on that funnel, so an arrival from somewhere else carrying stray TeleportData cannot advance it.
5c. **The test gate hid all of this while it was on.** A listed account is exempt from the `Attempts` check, so on a test account a failed dispatch silently came back on the next join and looked fine — the ladder only starts mattering once `TEST_ACCOUNTS_ONLY` goes false, which it now is. Verified at the time by sandboxing the real module source (one line changed: the gate) against stubbed services and driving nine scenarios — reserve exhaustion, `TeleportAsync` throwing, init failure then success, `Unauthorized`, `IsTeleporting`, `GameEnded` exhaustion, a clean run, and `Attempts == 2` refusing to dispatch at all.
