# TitleStyles

Visual style presets for cosmetic titles — the data half of the title styling system that TitleRenderer turns into instances.

## What it does
1. `Presets` — twelve named style presets forming a ladder from cheapest to flashiest: `plain` (flat accent text), `tint` (accent text + soft aura), `gradient` (static vertical two-tone), `shine` (gradient + a highlight band sweeping across the word), `glow` (aura breathing in size and brightness), `smoke` (ashen two-tone + a plume rising off the top of the word), `fire` (heat ramp + flame tongues licking off the letters + a rising ember shower + flickering aura), `holo` (rainbow scrolling along the word, **no aura**), and the four 800-Token apex styles `chrome` (polished gunmetal, hard specular rake + a bright glint), `bloodmoon` (crimson heartbeat + an eclipse band crossing the word + heavy embers), `lightning` (dim storm blue that *strikes* — white-out, aura punch and sparks) and `sandstorm` (ochre ramp + a pale scouring band + dense blowing grit)
1b. **The apex tier is defined by having an EVENT, not just a colour.** The 600-Token bar is `fire` (moving flame tongues) and `holo` (a full hue cycle); "coloured text plus a glow" reads as cheaper than both no matter how good the glow is. So each 800-Token style owns a periodic moment: chrome's glint, bloodmoon's eclipse sweep, lightning's strike, sandstorm's scour. Judge a new apex style by whether something *happens*, not by its palette
2. A preset describes **behaviour only**. The per-title accent colour comes from the TitlesConfig entry's `color` field (read by TitleRenderer, which owns both requires — this module requires nothing), so one preset serves every hunter's palette. The six presets carrying their own fixed palette (`fire`, `holo`, `chrome`, `bloodmoon`, `lightning`, `sandstorm`) ignore the accent for the glyph fill
3. `animated = true` marks the presets that register with TitleRenderer's shared stepper — everything except `plain`, `tint` and `gradient`. The static three are one-shot setups that do no per-frame work. The animated set is kept cheap deliberately: these render on overhead nameplates during a firefight
4. **Every `thickness` is a fraction of the rendered TEXT HEIGHT, not a pixel count**, resolved live by the renderer and clamped between `MinStrokePx` and `MaxStrokePx`. A fixed thickness is wrong at both ends — the 1.2px this module once authored measured 3% on a 40px card label (invisible beneath the authored 1px black TextStroke) and 7.5% on a 16px nameplate (a muddy merged smear). Fractions keep a style looking identical at every size it ships at
5. **The glow is a stack of dilated ghost copies of the word (`bloom.layers`), not a rectangle.** This reverses an earlier conclusion in the module: a UIStroke past ~5% of text height does merge neighbouring glyph outlines into a solid slab, which was read as "a wide soft glow is unreachable", and presets instead grew a `haze` — a rounded lozenge behind the text. It looked exactly like what it was, a grey box parked behind the word, on every apex title. A merged silhouette **is** a glow provided transparency climbs with thickness, so big thicknesses are fine and `MaxStrokePx` is now 22. No preset authors `haze` any more (the renderer still supports the field)
5b. **THE CALIBRATION WAS REVISED 2026-07-30 and every bloom preset repainted, because the original numbers drew a coloured LOZENGE behind each word.** Old: tight rim `0.07–0.11` @ `0.50+`, mid `0.18–0.26` @ `0.78+`, outer `0.38–0.46` @ `0.90+` — which composites to roughly **65% opacity at the glyph edge**, with an outer layer thick enough to bridge the gaps between neighbouring glyphs, so at every size a title actually ships at (16px podium plate, 24px shop card, 40px match-summary line) the three dilations merged into one near-solid mass hugging the word; at 52px the outer layer also clamped near `MaxStrokePx` and rendered a visible cross-hatch. New: tight rim `0.035–0.045` @ `0.50–0.62`, mid `0.10–0.13` @ `0.85–0.90`, outer `0.22–0.32` @ `0.94–0.96`, measuring about 51% → 16% → 5% across the falloff. Same mechanism, same claim — a merged silhouette *is* a glow — but only once transparency genuinely climbs with thickness, which at `0.50` on the inner layer it did not
5b-i. **`pulse` and `flash.punch` MULTIPLY the resolved thickness**, so a generous `maxScale`/`punch` re-creates the lozenge on the frames it fires — `lightning`'s `punch = 1.7` inflated its halo 2.7× on a strike. Keep `maxScale` ≤ ~1.2 and `punch` ≤ ~1.0. Screenshot any new or retuned preset at **16px, 24px and 40px**; every defect fixed in this pass was invisible in the source and obvious in a picture
5c. **A UIGradient is fitted to the glyph BOX (`TextBounds.Y`), which is much taller than the letters.** The title font spends roughly its top fifth and bottom fifth on ascender/descender room no capital reaches — caps measure about 57% of `TextBounds.Y` — so a ramp authored across a full `0..1` shows the word only its middle. `fire`'s white-hot crown and ember base both landed in that padding and the word rendered flat mid-orange: the ramp existed and was never seen. **Every `rotation = 90` preset now authors its stops remapped into `0.20..0.80` with the end colours repeated at `0.00`/`1.00`** so the full range lands on the caps and holds flat outside them; TitleRenderer's `baseStops` does the same for the two-tone it derives from `lightScale`/`darkScale`. Ramps at `rotation = 0` run *along* the word and are already fitted to its width, so they are left alone
6. Preset fields map to renderer behaviours: `gradient` (stops, or lightScale/darkScale derived from the accent, plus rotation and an optional `pad` so an animated offset never drags the ramp's clamped ends onto the glyphs), `stroke`, `bloom` (aura layers), `haze`, `pulse`, `shine`, `specular`, `heat`, `flicker`, `hue`, `scroll`, `flash` and `particles`. Two of those are more general than their names suggest: **`specular` is any coloured band raked across the glyphs**, so near-black at low transparency is bloodmoon's eclipse and pale sand is sandstorm's scour, not just chrome's highlight; and **`flash` takes a `punch`** that swells the aura on the same frame the word whitens, which is what turns lightning's strike into a burst of light rather than the text blinking
6b. **`flash.threshold` is measured against `math.noise() + 0.5`, which in practice occupies roughly `0.2–0.8`, not the full `0–1`.** A threshold of `0.80` therefore fires essentially never — chrome's glint sampled at 1% peak strength over 5 seconds before this was caught. Keep thresholds at or below ~0.66, and verify a new event actually fires by sampling the ghost's `TextTransparency` over several seconds rather than trusting a screenshot
7. `particles` is either one system or a **list** of systems, each with a `kind` of `flame`, `smoke`, `ember` or `grit` plus an optional `bed`. Dimensions are fractions of the rendered text height on **both** axes, so a tongue keeps its tall-narrow shape on a wide short nameplate instead of stretching into a disc. `fire` is the reason the list form exists: tongues and embers differ in where they are born, how fast they rise and how they fade, so one emitter cannot do both
7b. **Budget a particle system's total width against the WORD's width, and keep it translucent.** Fire's tongues were 13 bars at `transparency = 0.10` (i.e. 90% opaque) and `width = 0.15` of text height — about 85% of the word's own width in total — born 0.62 deep in the glyphs and travelling 1.35 of glyph height past them, which read as a picket fence standing over the title. Particles always draw *above* the host label (a child cannot go behind its parent), so an opaque particle over a glyph is a hole in the word. Now `count 13` × `width 0.10` @ `transparency 0.54`, born at `0.42` and dead within `0.60`, so they are a burning fringe on the top edge
7c. Every other emitter was softened in the same 2026-07-30 pass. The two worst: `sandstorm` blew **24 motes × `width 0.24`** across the word at 68% opacity — 2.5× the word's own width in total coverage, which obliterated the letters (now 18 × `0.18` @ `0.55`); and `bloodmoon` rose 12 embers *through* the glyphs from the baseline at 74% opacity (now 10, smaller, @ `0.44`). `smoke` 0.62→0.68, `chrome` 0.35→0.48, `lightning` 0.24→0.40
8. `flicker` and `hue` drive the colour of the tight `stroke` **and** every bloom layer, so a preset cannot have a fixed dark rim under a colour-cycling aura. `fire` and `lightning` therefore omit `stroke` and let the host label's own authored TextStroke supply the rim
9. `get(styleName)` returns the preset, falling back to `plain` so an unknown or missing style renders flat rather than erroring

Mirrored verbatim in Redwater Canyon.
10. **`gilded`** (2026-09-06) — the BOUNTY+ apex, reserved to GOLDEN OUTLAW alone, taking the preset count 12 → 13. Fixed gold glyphs under a slowly cycling prismatic aura: nothing else in the catalog moves colour through the HALO. Built entirely from `hue`, which was already in the schema and already implemented in TitleRenderer but used by no preset, so the style is DATA-ONLY and the renderer stayed byte-identical. Carries a `flash` glint so an apex owns an event, not just a palette. Deliberately has NO `stroke`: `hue` drives both the tight stroke and every bloom layer, so a fixed dark rim is not expressible — the host label's own authored TextStroke supplies it
