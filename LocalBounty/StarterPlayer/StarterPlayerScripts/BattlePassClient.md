# BattlePassClient

Drives the authored `StarterGui.BattlepassGui` panel (no longer builds any UI in code). Binds the two-track reward view to live server state, sets reward text **and art** from [[BattlePassConfig]], and handles claiming + the Upgrade Pass purchase.

## What it does
1. Resolves `PlayerGui.BattlepassGui` (sets `Enabled=false`, `ResetOnSpawn=false`). The panel **fades in only once the camera has landed** on the shop location — it listens to the main menu's `MenuCameraArrived` BindableEvent and opens on `4BattlePassFrame` (NOT on the raw tab click, which just starts the ~1.8s camera flight). It closes immediately when the menu moves to any other page, on the panel's own `CloseFrame` button, or when the main menu is disabled. **The close is driven by the `SelectedMenuFrame` attribute** on `MainMenuGui` (published by MenuHandlerScript from `selectMenuFrame`, the one function every navigation route passes through), not by the other tabs' buttons as before — those only fire for navigation that *starts* on the bottom bar, so a hand-off left the pass on screen until a `MenuCameraArrived` happened to fire, and the pass and the Trading Post share `CameraPartShop`, so a move between them need not produce one. Only the close moved: opening deliberately stays on `MenuCameraArrived` so the panel fades in on arrival rather than on the click. The `CloseFrame` (X) button additionally fires `MenuNavigateRequested("1HomeFrame")` so the player is returned to the Home tab (camera flies home) — this is wired only on the X button, not on the shared `close()` used by the other close paths
1b. **Fade-in reveal** (`playReveal`): a panel-coloured `BPFadeCover` Frame (runtime, ZIndex 500) dissolves over the body while a runtime `BPRevealScale` UIScale "pops" the Canvas in (0.93→1) and the hunter `ViewportFrame.ImageTransparency` fades 1→0 — so the whole panel + hunter materialise together on arrival
2. Layout is 6 `Section`s of 5 levels. For each: `PaidRewardTrack.<i>Reward` → global level `(S-1)*5+i` (bound to `Config.PAID`), the `FreeRewardTrack` cells (see 2b), and a `CompletionBackground` with 5 chips. Cards are built **once per join** from the season live at that moment (`Config.PAID`/`FREE` resolve by time), so a player sitting in the lobby across a season boundary keeps the old cards until they rejoin; the server refuses claims on them
2a. **A level with no reward row is captioned `COMING SOON`** (`markPending`) and its placeholder art hidden — the authored card underneath reads `LOREM IPSUM` over a boost icon. Applies to a paid card with no `PAID` row and to a section's `5Free` card with no `FREE` row; no ref is built, so it is never claimable. This is how BattlePassConfig's ART PENDING rows render
2b. **The free track builds its own cells.** It is authored as five grid cells — `1Blank`..`4Blank` spacers plus one real card in `5Free` — from when the free track was one reward per section at level `S*5`. `Config.FREE` may now list **any** level, so the loop walks all five cells and, for a mid-section free level, clones that section's own `5Free` card into the matching blank (named `<i>Free`, the blank destroyed). Nothing is authored per level: adding a free level is a config-only change. Two things are load-bearing — cells are filled **in order 1..5** because cell 5 *is* the template and must be last or the clones inherit its text and art; and `LayoutOrder` is set explicitly on every cell (authored cells all sit at 0 and order by name today, which a clone named `2Free` happens to satisfy, but only by luck of the alphabet). Every free cell now captions in its own `TextLabel` and wears a FREE plate (3e), so the cells are interchangeable and a clone needs no per-section special-casing
3. **Reward text is data-bound** from the config through `rewardName(reward)`: paid cards caption in `RewardName`, free cards in `TextLabel`. Level chips show the level number. Free cards used to read `FREE` and push the reward name into a `RewardImage.AmountText` overlay drawn on top of the icon; both authored overlays (the level-1 paid card and section 1's `5Free`) were **deleted on 2026-07-26** because they repeated the caption sitting right underneath. Every card now states its reward once, and the word FREE moved onto a plate (3e)
3a. **A title card is captioned with its HUNTER, not its label** (`rewardName`). Every title belongs to exactly one hunter, and the card already renders the title itself as its art — so "TITLE: DRIFTER" underneath said the same word twice while omitting the one fact that decides whether the player can ever wear it. The caption is `"<HUNTER> TITLE"` (`DUST TITLE`, `FORTUNA TITLE`), resolved through [[TitlesConfig]] `HunterOf` rather than duplicated into [[BattlePassConfig]]. Same call the Trading Post's title cards make
3a-i. **A caption gets exactly one line, and that is a hard budget.** The box is 0.268 of the card tall — room for two `TextScaled` lines — but it is positioned near the card's bottom edge, so a second line falls into the track's clip rect and is lost: `PaidRewardTrack` clips it directly, and on the free row the `ScrollingFrame` edge does. "HUNTER SKIN: SUMMER DUST" wrapped and lost "DUST" on both rows, worst on the free one. So `rewardName` strips a leading `Hunter Skin: ` / `Gun Skin: ` / `Emote: ` prefix (`PREFIXED_KINDS`) — the art is already a hunter, a revolver or a dancing dummy, so the prefix spent half the line naming what the picture said. Every S3 caption now measures as one line, the longest being `CASH BOOST — 60 MIN` at 89 of 89.3 px. Two authored fixes went with it, both applied to all 78 caption labels: the box moved from y-scale 0.99 (half outside the card, where the free row's copy sat under the horizontal scrollbar) to **0.87**, seating it inside the card on both rows; and `ZIndex` went to **3**, because the caption is a *sibling* of `RewardFrame` and on free cards it is authored **before** it — so a 1.6× skin viewport (ZIndex 2) drew straight over `SUMMER DUST` and hid it completely. Paid cards only ever worked by being authored last
3b. **Reward ART is data-bound too** (`applyRewardArt`, called from `buildRewardRef`). Every authored card ships with the same placeholder coin icon, so each is repainted from its config row: **currency / chest** → a flat icon from `Config.ICONS`; **boost** → the per-boost `icon` off [[BoostConfig]], so 15/30/60-minute boosts are tellable apart; **title** → no title icon art exists and a generic one would say nothing, so the title is drawn as *itself* in a runtime `BPTitleArt` TextLabel styled by [[TitleRenderer]] (identical to a nameplate), sized 1.7× the `RewardFrame` so `TextScaled` doesn't shrink a long label, and taking its `FontFace` from the card's own caption label — `RewardName` **or** `TextLabel`, the same either/or `buildRewardRef` makes. Reading only `RewardName` left every *free* title card on the engine default font, so `gunhand`, which sits on both tracks at L7, rendered as two visibly different titles depending on which row you looked at; **hunterSkin/gunSkin** → the real model live in a runtime `BPRewardViewport` ViewportFrame. Rig framing is head-and-shoulders (viewport 1.6×, FOV 28) matching the Collection hunter tiles; gun framing is bounding-box based but grown to 1.6× with the camera pulled to 0.58× distance, because a long thin gun otherwise reads far smaller in frame than a rig. The gun's pose comes from [[WeaponDisplayConfig]] per hunter, exactly as the Collection weapon tile's does (it used to be a HellFire-only half turn, which put every other Dust gun upside down), plus Bram's card-only diagonal roll. Level 9's card is authored **without** a `RewardImage`, so the image is created on demand rather than assumed; a missing model falls back to a flat icon so a bad config row leaves a plain card, not a blank one
3b-i. **Viewport mesh quality is authored on the SOURCE models, not set here.** Default `RenderFidelity.Automatic` picks a mesh's LOD from its size **on screen**, and a reward card's viewport is ~55px square on a phone — so Automatic parts dropped to their coarsest hull and the Summer hunter skins rendered as faceted blobs while the flat-icon rewards beside them looked fine. `RenderFidelity` carries **PluginSecurity**, so a LocalScript cannot write it: assigning it inside `stripForDisplay` throws *"the current thread cannot write 'RenderFidelity'"*, and thrown mid-loop it aborts the strip halfway, leaving the rig unanchored and the card broken. The 20 straggler MeshParts were converted to `Precise` on the Bounty-side templates instead — `Characters.{Dust,Vesper,Fortuna}.Skin.Summer*` (8 / 5 / 3 of ~22 each; the rest were already Precise, so this restores the authored intent) and `WeaponSkins.Dust.HellFire` (4 of 4), the one gun skin on this track with the same defect. Clones inherit it, and the Collection menu's viewports get the same benefit. HellFire is mostly **UnionOperations**, which carry their own `RenderFidelity` — a MeshPart-only sweep missed all 6 of them; both classes are covered now. Deliberately **not** mirrored into Redwater: Precise on gameplay character meshes has a real mobile cost, and Redwater has no pass panel
3b-ii. **Card art is resolution-bound, and fidelity settings cannot fix that.** `Background` is sized `RelativeYY` — off the reward *track's* height — so on a 392px-tall screen `RewardFrame` lands at ~34px and its ViewportFrame at ~55px, against ~94px / ~151px at 1080p. A ViewportFrame renders at its own pixel size, and widening the card supplies nothing because the card never took its size from its width. `rewardViewportScale()` is the one lever that adds pixels without moving the panel: it interpolates the viewport multiplier from the authored **1.6** at the 720p baseline up to **2.2** on a ~420px viewport, so the rig simply draws bigger (the authored 1.6 already overflows `RewardFrame` on purpose). Rig and gun share the one function so the two card kinds cannot disagree. The ceiling is the reward **cell** (~96×82 on that phone) — past it neighbouring cards' art collides — so this reaches ~75px and is still short of desktop; closing the rest needs the pass showing fewer levels at once, which is a layout change rather than a tuning one
3c. **An emote card gets the Trading Post's animated showcase** (`renderEmoteArt`) — a dummy looping that emote in a `BPRewardViewport`, instead of one shared emote glyph on every emote card. The rig is **borrowed from the shop's emote-card template** (`StarterGui.Shop.Templates.EmoteCard.Canvas.ViewportFrame.WorldModel.Rig` since 2026-08-23 — the template ShopExtrasTab clones its cards from) rather than duplicated here, so the two menus cannot drift into showing different dummies. That is an instance dependency on another ScreenGui, so it is a **soft** one: the card is painted with the flat `Config.ICONS.emote` first, queued in `emoteQueue`, and upgraded to a viewport off the main build by a `task.spawn` at boot; every `WaitForChild` in the lookup carries a timeout, so a missing or slow shop leaves flat icons and never stalls the panel. The upgrade re-points `ref.rewardImage` at the new viewport so the claimed dim still lands on the art that is showing, then calls `refreshAll()`
3d. **Emote framing is fixed, not measured** (`EMOTE_CAMERA_DISTANCE` 10.6, `EMOTE_FOCUS_RISE` -0.41, FOV 36, viewport 1.5× at y-scale 0.4). The shop rig is already looping an emote of its own when the pass clones it, so a `GetBoundingBox` taken at clone time is whatever pose it was caught in — a crouch pulls the camera in and every standing frame after it renders with the head cut off. The camera aims at the **anchored HumanoidRootPart** instead, which is identical on every clone, offset down to the rig's centre (the root sits at the hips). A standing R15 dummy fills ~75% of frame; sampling the pass emotes across their loops reaches 0.80 of the viewport half-height at worst, so no pose clips. The viewport is lifted off centre because this is a full-body shot, not the head-and-shoulders crop a hunter skin gets, and at centre the boots ran into the card's caption. `stripForDisplay(rig, false)` anchors **only** the root — anchoring the rest freezes the animation. Playback follows `gui.Enabled`, so four rigs never animate behind a closed panel
3e. **Free cards wear a FREE plate** (`addFreeTag`). With the reward name occupying the caption line, nothing else on screen said which row was which — and the panel scrolls one whole section at a time, so a header before level 1 would scroll out of view, while the hunter viewport occupies the only margin a pinned label could sit in. The plate has to live on the card. It is the paid row's own `RewardLevel` gold chip **cloned**, which inherits the authored plate, stroke, shadow, font and text outline for free — worth doing because `UIStroke.Thickness` in this GUI is an authored fraction (0.010–0.050), not pixels, so a hand-built stroke is an easy way to get an invisible or an enormous one. Only the gradient and the text change: pewter instead of brass, so the two rows read as two tiers of the same thing. It sits top-**right** rather than centred like the level chip, because the claimed ✓ badge occupies the top-left and a plate wide enough for a word would run underneath it
4. **Claim eligibility** mirrors the server (`getReward` + `isClaimable`): reached, tier-eligible (paid ⇒ needs `ownsFullPass`), unclaimed. `claimed` is keyed `p<level>`/`f<level>`
5. **Ready-to-claim glow:** when a reward is claimable, clones the `ReadyToClaim` UIShadow (from the `ReadyToClaimTemplate` folder) into that reward's `Background` and destroys it when no longer claimable. The authored template values are overridden at clone time (`BlurRadius` 0.30, `ZIndex` -1) and the glow **pulses** between transparency 0.45 and 0.12 on a looping reversing tween — at the authored 0.2 blur / 0.5 transparency it was effectively invisible at card size. The card's own black `UIShadow` is pushed to `ZIndex` -2 while glowing (both sat at -1) so the gold reads on top, and restored when the glow is removed
5b. **Claimed stamp:** a reward already in `claimed` dims its art and gets a runtime gold `BPClaimedBadge` circle with a ✓ in the card's own font. Whatever `applyRewardArt` installed takes the dim — an ImageLabel or ViewportFrame through `ImageColor3`, a styled title through `TextTransparency` instead (it is text, not an image) — alongside `BackgroundGlow` and the label at transparency 0.45. Authored colours are cached per reward in `buildRewardRef` so the dim is reversible. Without this a claimed reward looked identical to one that was never reached
6. **Tap-to-claim:** a transparent overlay `TextButton` is added over each reward `Background`; active only when claimable, it fires `ClaimBattlePassRewardEvent(track, level)` and optimistically applies the claimed stamp — **to the sibling card at the same level too** when `state.ownsFullPass`, because the server claims both tracks in one go ([[BattlePassService]] `tryGrantBoth`). Without that the other card sat glowing "claimable" until the authoritative payload landed a beat later
7. **CLAIM ALL** button fires `ClaimAllBattlePassRewardEvent`; enabled only when something is claimable
8. **UPGRADE PASS** button shows the price (`fullPassPrice`) and flips to "PASS OWNED" (disabled) when owned. It no longer fires the purchase on the first tap — it opens a confirmation card (8a) and `UpgradeBattlePassEvent` is fired from that card's CONFIRM
8a. **The confirmation card** (`openConfirm` / `buildConfirmPopup`). 800 Tokens is the largest single spend in the lobby and the three bottom-row buttons sit 0.09 apart, so a mis-tap beside CLAIM ALL emptied the wallet with nothing to take it back. `BPConfirmOverlay` is built once on first use and parented to the **ScreenGui, as a sibling of `Canvas`** — the scrim has to cover the whole screen, the lobby's bottom tab bar included, and `Canvas` is only 0.6 × 0.64 of it; `ZIndexBehavior` here is `Sibling`, so the overlay's 400+ sorts above every authored element on the panel (it cannot reach ScreenGuis of a higher `DisplayOrder` — the friends-in-match handle at 100 stays lit and clickable, which is harmless). Everything carrying the panel's look is **cloned from a live authored part** rather than hand-built, for the same reason `addFreeTag` clones the level chip (3e — `UIStroke.Thickness` in this GUI is an authored fraction, not pixels): the card wears `Canvas`'s own `UIStroke` + `InnerStroke` + `UIShadow`, its title is a cloned `Header`, its price is the upgrade button's own `TokenPriceLabel` (which brings the token icon, the purple and the text outline with it), and both buttons are cloned `ClaimRewardButton`s — CANCEL repainted with `FREE_TAG_GRADIENT`, the same pewter the free track already means "the lesser of these two" with, so the gold button is unambiguously the one that commits. A clone arrives carrying whatever state its source was in, so three things are reset on it: the `BattlePassSfxHooked` attribute (`hookButtonSfx` early-returns on it, and the buttons would get no hover sound or scale), the hover `UIScale`, and a `ReadyToClaim` glow if one happened to be sitting on CLAIM ALL. CANCEL and a tap on the full-screen backdrop both dismiss — cancel is the harmless outcome, so a stray tap costs the player nothing, unlike the GiftPopupClient backdrop this otherwise follows, which deliberately does *not* dismiss because closing is that popup's only action. Both handlers are wrapped closures rather than `Connect(closeConfirm)`, since `MouseButton1Click` passes the click's x/y through and a number landing in the `instant` parameter would skip the fade every time
8b. **The card is locked to 1.6:1 and floored in PIXELS.** Every row inside it is positioned in scale, so a card sized purely in screen scale ran from 1.53:1 on a phone to 2.34:1 in a wide window and the title, price and buttons stretched with it. `Size` is `UDim2.new(0.9, 0, 0.14, 120)` under a `UIAspectRatioConstraint` — height comes mostly from the 120px offset rather than from scale, which holds the card at 172px on an iPhone 7 and puts CANCEL/CONFIRM at ~45px, clear of the 44px minimum touch target and nearly double the 24px the panel's own bottom-row buttons measure there; the 0.14 scale term still grows it to 271px at 1080p. `AspectType` must be **`FitWithinMaxSize`**: `ScaleWithParentSize` derives the element's size from the parent alone and ignores `Size` offsets, which would throw the pixel floor away. The 0.9 width term is only a guard for a viewport too narrow to fit 1.6× the height across it. Sizing the card off the *panel* was rejected for the same reason — it would have inherited that 24px button height
8c. **A player who cannot afford it never sees the card.** The click handler pre-checks `state.tokens` against `fullPassPrice` and goes straight to the red price flash, because a confirmation that could only ever end in a refusal is worse than no confirmation; `tokens` is nil only before the first payload lands, and then the server is left to decide as before. That flash is now a shared `flashPrice()` used by both the pre-check and the `insufficient` reply, and it reads the authored colour **once at startup** rather than at flash time — a second flash landing mid-flash captured the RED as the colour to restore and left the price red permanently, which the pre-check makes far easier to hit. The card is also dropped instantly (no fade) whenever the panel closes and whenever `updateUpgradeButton` sees the pass owned, so it can neither outlive the panel nor sit over an already-bought pass
8d. **The price badge is squashed on a tablet, and only there.** `TokenPriceLabel` pairs a `TextScaled` “800” — sized by the label’s HEIGHT — with a `RelativeXX` `TokenIcon` whose size *and* 0.275 x-offset both come off its WIDTH, so the two only read correctly while the box keeps the shape it was drawn at. `BattlepassGui.Canvas` carries no aspect lock (unlike the shop’s `Content`, which ShopController clamps to 16:9, the pass chips’ `StackHolder` at 1.6174, or the confirmation card at 1.6), so a 4:3 tablet hands this label a box 25 % narrower for the same height: the digits keep their size, the coin shrinks, and the coin lands under the 8. `fitPriceBadge` multiplies the authored Y scale by `min(1, aspect / (16/9))` on every `ViewportSize` change, putting both halves back on the width axis at the proportions it was authored with. It is a `min()`, so at 16:9 and wider — every phone, and any desktop not windowed narrower than 16:9 — the size written is the authored one and nothing moves. The confirmation card’s clone of this label needs none of it: 8b locks that card to 1.6, so it keeps one shape on every device
9. **Completion spine + chips:** the 6 gold `CompletionBackground.RewardTrack` bars form one continuous XP track — section *s* fills its bar left→right for `xp` in `[(s-1)*SECTION_XP, s*SECTION_XP]` where `SECTION_XP = XP_PER_LEVEL * 5` (12500), tweened from the payload `xp` (starts empty on build). The fill is offset back by **half a level** (`HALF_LEVEL_XP`): both the chip grid and the card grid use `CellSize` 0.2, so a level's diamond sits at the *centre* of its cell (0.1/0.3/0.5/0.7/0.9 across the section), not at `i/5`. Without the offset the bar ran half a level ahead of the diamonds and a reward read as unlocked a full card early. **Below level 1 that offset needs its own ramp** (`spinePosition`): `xp - HALF_LEVEL_XP` is negative there, and clamping it per section pinned all six bars at exactly 0 for the first 1250 XP — half a level, which is ~6 matches. Nothing else on the panel carries a number, so an account with a real 562 XP was pixel-identical to a fresh one and every match read as having paid out nothing. The pre-level-1 stretch now gets its own linear ramp onto level 1's diamond — `0 → 0`, `XP_PER_LEVEL → FIRST_DIAMOND` (0.1, derived as `HALF_LEVEL_XP / SECTION_XP` rather than hand-tuned as a second constant) — which is exactly where the offset formula picks up, so the two join at level 1 without a step and every diamond still lands on its own bar edge (2500 → 0.1, 12500 → 0.9, 40000 → section 4 at 0.1). At max XP every bar is forced to 1 so the last diamond isn't left 10% short of the right edge. The 5 chips per section dim (BackgroundTransparency 0.7) for un-reached levels and brighten for reached ones
9b. **`WeeklyQuestsButton` is the link to the Quests menu's WEEKLY tab** — the third button in the bottom row, right of CLAIM ALL. Weekly quests are one of the two ways to fill this track, but they live in a different panel and nothing on either screen said the two were connected — the pass stopped drawing quest cards when they moved to the Quests menu and no link replaced them. Clicking it fires `QuestPanelRequested("weekly")`. **This needed no new remote:** `state.quests` and `state.week` were always in the payload and were simply going unread once the cards were removed. It replaced a full-width `WeeklyQuestBanner` across the empty band under the header plate (deleted 2026-07-30), so every action the panel offers now sits in one place. The row was **rebalanced from two buttons to three** for it — `UpgradeBountyPass` 0.35→0.20 and `ClaimRewardButton` 0.65→0.50, new button at 0.80 — because a third at the authored 0.2100857 width overflowed the canvas otherwise; all three keep that width and the authored 0.09 gap, so the row stays symmetric (edges 0.095/0.905) and still clears the hunter viewport at 0.015. The button is **cloned from `ClaimRewardButton`** so it inherits the gradient, `UICorner` and UIStroke thickness (an authored *fraction*, 0.05, not pixels — a hand-built stroke is an easy way to get an invisible or an enormous one, same trap as the free-track plates in 3e). Its label box is `0.8 × 1.0`, copied from UPGRADE PASS rather than CLAIM ALL's `0.8 × 0.8`, because both are two-word labels — at the shorter box `TextScaled` settled several px smaller than either neighbour
9b-i. **The banner's four-state status line is gone and could not come back.** A 0.21-wide button cannot hold `CLAIM 4,500 XP FROM 3 WEEKLY QUEST S`, so the label is fixed and the claimable state is carried by two signals instead of text: the pulsing `ReadyToClaim` glow, and a red corner dot. Both are driven by `anyWeeklyQuestClaimable()`, which replaced `weeklyQuestSummary()` — the old four-way text needed totals and XP sums, the two visual signals need only a boolean, so it early-returns on the first hit. It still counts **every unlocked week** (a quest stays claimable for the rest of the season) and clamps `state.week` to `Config.TOTAL_WEEKS`, matching `BattlePassService.onClaimQuest`'s own `entry.week > Config.currentWeek()` gate. Because the button is gold like its neighbours it needs no idle/active colour swap, so `BANNER_IDLE`/`BANNER_ACTIVE` and `commaNumber` are gone too
9b-ii. **The glow needed a `Spread`, and that is a per-ref override.** The authored `ReadyToClaim` template has `Spread = 0`, which halos an 87×100 reward card fine — a blurred gold silhouette behind a near-square card bleeds past all four edges. A bottom-row button is 209×40, so at `Spread = 0` the blur has nowhere to escape to and the glow is **invisible**; the same clone that halos a card shows nothing on a button. `setGlow` therefore honours an optional `ref.glowSpread`, set only on `weeklyBtnRef` (`UDim2.new(0.055, 0, 0.29, 0)`) so the reward cards keep their shipped tuning untouched. Scale, not offset, because the button is ~209×40 on desktop and ~97×23 on a phone; the X scale is ~1/5.2 of the Y because the button is 5.2:1, which is what keeps the halo even in pixels rather than a wide flat smear
9b-iii. **The dot is `WeeklyNotifyDot`, built at runtime, and two `UIAspectRatioConstraint` defaults break it silently.** Two nested circles — a dark `Color3.fromRGB(40, 26, 14)` ring with a red `224, 44, 44` `Dot` at 0.74 of it — rather than a Frame with a UIStroke, for the same fractional-thickness reason as above. It is sized off the button's **height** (`UDim2.fromScale(0, 0.50)`, ~20px desktop / ~9px phone) with the aspect constraint holding it round, so **both** constraint properties are off their defaults: `AspectType` must be `ScaleWithParentSize` (the default `FitWithinMaxSize` fits the element inside the `Size` box and so resolves to 0 against the 0 width scale) and `DominantAxis` must be `Height` (the default `Width` would drive height from that same 0). Either default alone collapses the dot to 0×0, and a zero-sized Frame throws nothing — it just never appears. `ZIndexBehavior` is `Sibling`, so ZIndex sorts globally and a badge left at the button's own ZIndex of 1 can draw *under* it — the same trap that put the old banner's label behind its parent's fill — so the ring and dot sit at 6 and 7
10. Refreshes on every `BattlePassDataEvent`; eager-fetches state on join via `RequestBattlePassDataEvent`. Individual quest **cards** are not shown here (they live in the Quests menu WEEKLY tab); only the button in 9b. That refresh is what clears the dot and glow when a quest is claimed in the *other* panel — [[BattlePassService]] `onClaimQuest` calls `sendState`, so no cross-panel notification is needed and the signals cannot go stale. The equipped hunter shown in `Canvas.AnimatedFrame` is driven by the sibling `BattlePassRigAnimationClient` viewport script (no code connection to this one). That frame was **scaled to 0.74 and shifted left on 2026-07-26**: at its authored size it reached 13.5% into the panel and covered almost all of the level-1 card. Its right edge now parks at 1.5% of the canvas width, chosen rather than "just clear of card 1" because the cards are `RelativeYY` — they grow toward the cell edge on a taller-relative screen, so a margin tuned at one aspect ratio does not hold at another. The frame's bottom edge was pinned while it shrank, so the hunter stays standing on the ground

## Connections
### RemoteEvents
| Remote | Direction | Other script |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| `BattlePassDataEvent` | ← Server | [[BattlePassService]] pushes full pass state on join and after each claim/purchase |
| `ClaimBattlePassRewardEvent` | → Server | [[BattlePassService]] validates and grants one `(track, level)` reward |
| `ClaimAllBattlePassRewardEvent` | → Server | [[BattlePassService]] claims all available rewards |
| `UpgradeBattlePassEvent` | → Server | [[BattlePassService]] sells the Full Pass for 800 Tokens |
| `BattlePassPurchaseResultEvent` | ← Server | [[BattlePassService]] reports purchase success/failure |
| `RequestBattlePassDataEvent` | → Server | [[BattlePassService]] replies with current state |

### BindableEvents
| Bindable | Direction | Other script |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| `MenuCameraArrived` | ← | [[MenuHandlerScript]] fires when the camera lands on a frame; opens/fades the pass on `4BattlePassFrame` |
| `MenuNavigateRequested` | → | [[MenuHandlerScript]] X (`CloseFrame`) button fires `"1HomeFrame"` to close the pass and return to the Home tab |
| `QuestPanelRequested` | → | [[QuestClient]] `WeeklyQuestsButton` fires `"weekly"` to open the Quests menu on the WEEKLY tab. The pass is deliberately left **open** underneath (QuestGui is DisplayOrder 10 against this panel's 8, and its close-overlay swallows clicks), so closing the quests returns the player to the pass rather than to the lobby |

| Target | Function | Purpose |
