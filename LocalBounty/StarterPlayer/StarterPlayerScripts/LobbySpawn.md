# LobbySpawn

Manages the lobby camera and spawns visual dummy characters at lobby slots based on party composition.

## What it does
0. Resolves `Workspace.LobbySpawns`, `Workspace.CameraFolder.CameraPart` and the four `Player1`–`Player4` slot parts through a `requireChild` helper with a **bounded 30 s `WaitForChild`**, warning and returning if any never arrives. `LobbySpawns` was a plain Folder until 2026-08-01 and so streamed by distance like ordinary geometry; Bounty has no `SpawnLocation`, so the character spawns at the origin far from the slots. That combination produced infinite-yield warnings on `LobbySpawns:WaitForChild("Player1")` — and because the chain sits at the top of the script with no timeout, a slow stream-in stalled not just the slots but all ~900 lines below it, taking out the lobby camera and every dummy in silence. `LobbySpawns` is now a Model with `ModelStreamingMode = Persistent`, the same treatment `CameraFolder` and `InviteButtons` already had, so it replicates on join regardless of where the player is standing; the timeouts remain as a diagnostic rather than a hang
1. Locks camera to a fixed scriptable position using CameraFolder.CameraPart
2. Maintains a folder of lobby dummy models cloned from ReplicatedStorage.Characters
3. Places the local player's dummy at slot 1, party members at slots 2-4. `placeDummyAtSlot` does **not** pivot the rig straight onto the slot part — it first measures the clone's real foot plane with `getFootLift` and raises the placement by however far the feet hang below the model's pivot. The slot parts were positioned assuming every rig's pivot sits at foot level, which was true until Bram's rig was scaled up 1.1x: that pushes his feet 0.3 studs below his pivot, so he stood buried to the ankles in the lobby floor while the other hunters looked fine. The lift is clamped to zero so it can only raise — Vesper's and Fortuna's feet sit 0.15 studs *above* their pivot, and pulling them down would change how they already stand. It is measured on the fresh clone, before the rig is unanchored and the idle starts moving joints, because these rigs are posed by AnimationConstraints relative to the anchored HumanoidRootPart and the stored pose is exactly the constraint rest pose
4. Strips scripts from cloned dummies, anchors root parts, disables collision
4b. **Attaches the hunter's world weapon to each dummy's right hand, using a grip offset measured live off a reference rig rather than a hardcoded CFrame.** `attachLobbyWeapon` clones the model from `ReplicatedStorage.WeaponSkinsWorld.<Hunter>` (resolved through an alias table, so the equipped skin name `DefaultRevolver` maps to the folder's `Default`; non-default skins like `HellFire`, `PrestigeCard`, Vesper's `Frostpiercer` and the 2026-08-21 ice guns `BetaRevolver` / `Icefang` / `FrozenHand` / `FrostbiteGrudgekeeper` map to models of their own name). The alias table is not what makes a skin resolve — an unlisted name already falls through to itself — but `warmWeaponOffsetCache` iterates it, so a skin missing from it measures its grip on first display instead of at boot. Fortuna's cards are the exception to "guns arrive posed": both card skins take a `LOBBY_CARD_ROTATION_SKINS` turn plus their own `LOBBY_WEAPON_OFFSET_ADJUSTMENTS` entry, and `FrozenHand` shares `PrestigeCard`'s numbers because its fan is posed onto Prestige's first card in the match places too, then asks `getWeaponOffset` where to put it. **A new gun skin therefore needs its model added to `WeaponSkinsWorld.<Hunter>` SAVED POSED AT THE WIRE RIG** — the grip is measured from the model's stored pivot against the rig's hand, so a model pasted at an arbitrary CFrame measures a garbage offset and the dummy draws the gun hundreds of studs away (how Frostpiercer first shipped: pivot 277 studs from `VesperWire`). Pose it by `PivotTo`-ing the sibling `Default`'s pivot and scale it to the same longest-axis length, since nothing downstream normalizes size. That offset is *computed*, not stored: `computeWeaponOffsetFromReferences` finds the hunter's poser rig in the workspace (`DustWire` / `FortunaWire` / `VesperWire` / `BramWire`), takes its `RightHand` CFrame, and measures the weapon pivot against it — which is what lets a grip be re-tuned by dragging the wire rig in Studio instead of editing numbers. The per-hunter constants in `LOBBY_WEAPON_OFFSET_ADJUSTMENTS` only nudge that measurement. **The wire rigs are therefore load-bearing at runtime and must be `ModelStreamingMode = Persistent`** — they sit ~460-480 studs from the lobby slots, far outside any guaranteed streaming radius, and under `StreamingEnabled` a Model replicates as an empty container while its parts are still streamed out. `workspace:FindFirstChild("DustWire")` then succeeds while `FindFirstChild("RightHand", true)` returns nil, the offset comes back nil, and the dummy spawns **holding nothing** behind a single `Lobby weapon missing Dust DefaultRevolver offset` warning. The failure is never cached — a nil offset stores nothing, so any later rebuild retries — and the `task.delay(1.5, rebuildLobbyDummies)` on the `CharacterAdded` path re-runs late enough to succeed, which is why the dummy self-heals and only the warning survives. **Persistent alone did not silence it, and making the rigs Persistent is not the whole fix** — see item 4d for the second, independent half
4c. `warmWeaponOffsetCache` precomputes all 10 hunter/skin offset pairs once at init, before the first `rebuildLobbyDummies()`. It existed as dead code — defined and never called — from whenever the cache was introduced until 2026-08-03; wiring it up means the offsets are resolved in one pass at a known-good moment instead of lazily during the first dummy build. It never poisons the cache, since it skips keys that already hold a value and a failed measurement writes nothing — but that same property made it a **complete no-op** from the day it was wired up (2026-08-03) until 2026-08-05, because it ran at the exact instant the wire rigs had not replicated yet, resolved nothing for any of the ten pairs, and stored none of the failures. It only does its job now that item 4d gates it behind the rigs actually arriving
4d. **`resolveWireRigs` waits for the four poser rigs before any grip is measured, because `Persistent` is a promise about *eviction*, not about *arrival order*.** A Persistent Model is sent at join and never streamed out again — it is not guaranteed to be present when this script's first line runs. The `requireChild` gates in item 0 release the instant `LobbySpawns` and `CameraFolder` replicate, and the final `rebuildLobbyDummies()` at the bottom of the file then runs **synchronously**, measuring grips against wire rigs that are still in flight later in the same batch. Making the rigs Persistent removed the distance-based eviction that used to make this race flip both ways, so from 2026-08-03 the ordering was stable and the race lost **every** join — an intermittent warning became exactly one per login, which is how it was caught. `resolveWireRigs` does a bounded `WaitForChild` for each rig and its `RightHand`, then sets `wireRigsResolved`; it runs inside a `task.spawn` that **does not yield at all** when the rigs are already there, so the common case warms the cache before the first build and nothing is rebuilt twice. Until that flag is set, `attachLobbyWeapon` treats a missing offset as a deferral rather than an error: it sets `weaponAttachDeferred` and stays silent, and the resolver rebuilds once the rigs land instead of waiting for the 1.5 s respawn retry. The warning is *suppressed, not deleted* — the flag is set even when a rig times out, so a rig that is genuinely renamed or deleted still warns, once, naming the rig. **Nothing here is sequenced against the loading screen**, and never was: the final `rebuildLobbyDummies()` is called flat at the bottom of the file, and only `startCamera` waits on `BountyIntroComplete`. `ReplicatedFirst.LoadingScreen` happens to cover the window anyway — a `DisplayOrder = 1000` full-screen GUI held for a `MIN_HOLD_TIME` 1.5 s floor measured from before StarterPlayerScripts run, plus a `MIN_SKY_TIME` 1.2 s floor and the camera pan, over a camera it owns via a RenderStep bind — which is why this was log noise rather than a visible empty hand in the normal case. The exception is item 8's missed `CharacterAdded`, which is what let it outlive the cover
5. Plays randomized idle animations on each dummy with slight speed/time variation
6. Adds a BillboardGui name tag above each dummy's head (a vertical `UIListLayout` stack) showing the player's name, and — when that player has a title equipped — their equipped title beneath the name. The title is read from the replicated `EquippedTitle` attribute (set by CollectionService to the **currently-equipped hunter's** per-hunter title label — titles are chosen per hunter now) via `titleForUserId`, which also returns the companion `EquippedTitleId`, so it works for the local player and any party member in the lobby. The line is then rendered through [[TitleRenderer]], which applies that title's cosmetic style (gradient, halo, flames) on top of the plain gold text — effects attach as children of the label, so the billboard's `UIListLayout` is left undisturbed
6b. **Nametag lines are never `TextScaled`.** `addLine` sizes each line through `fitTextSize`, which measures with `TextService:GetTextSize` and returns the largest **whole-number** `TextSize` (capped at that line's height) whose rendered width still fits `TAG_WIDTH`. TextScaled rasterises the glyphs at one size and rescales them to fit the box, and that resample leaves a faint ghost row of the font atlas a few pixels above the line — plainly visible as two hairline dashed rows above the dummy's name against bright sky. Measuring and rasterising at a whole number draws the glyphs natively, so the ghost disappears while long display names still shrink to fit exactly as before ("VorFader" → 22, an 18-character name → 13, 20 W's → 5). `TAG_WIDTH` is shared with `billboard.Size` so the two can't drift apart. `fitTextSize` returns the rendered width alongside the size, which is what lets the level badge be placed against the name's real left edge
6c. **The level badge (a clone of StarterGui.LevelDiamondTemplate) sits in a reserved gutter, not on top of the name.** The billboard is `TAG_WIDTH + DIAMOND_GUTTER` wide and the `TextStack` is inset by the gutter, so the text region is exactly `TAG_WIDTH`; `billboard.SizeOffset` shifts the whole box half a gutter left so the *text* stays centred over the head rather than the box. The badge then sits `DIAMOND_GAP` left of the name's measured left edge, clamped so it can never leave the box — a short name lets it tuck in beside the text, a name that fills the region pushes it back into the gutter, and either way the 3px gap survives. It used to be pinned at a flat `Position.X = -4` against the billboard edge, which silently assumed a long name would leave slack there; it does not, so the badge landed on the first letters ("8ndtaglutdinso" on a 14-character name). The gutter reserves the template's **diagonal**, because the template frame carries `Rotation = 45` to make the diamond shape and so draws `Size * sqrt(2)` wide — a 24px badge reaches 17px from its centre, not 12, and that gap between `Size` and drawn extent is what made the flat offset look safe. Hugging the name also pulls the badge *inward* on short names (`Ky` puts it at head-33 where the flat pin held it at head-69), so it moves away from the neighbouring dummy's tag rather than toward it; on desktop this fixed a second overlap the flat pin caused, slot 4's badge landing on slot 3's title line
6d. **Two metric sets, chosen by `UserInputService.TouchEnabled`.** Desktop keeps the numbers it always had (`TAG_WIDTH` 130, line heights 15/22/16, badge 24, gap 4); touch gets a set about a third smaller (100, 11/16/12, badge 17, gap 3), because the desktop tag was wider than the 9.15 studs between lobby slots and neighbouring tags piled up on a phone. Why the tag crowds worse on a phone: a BillboardGui's offset `Size` is **absolute screen pixels** — it does not shrink with camera distance and it does not shrink with the viewport (verified in Redwater with two identical plates at 12 and 24 studs, which render the same pixel size). The gap between lobby dummies on screen is the opposite, being world geometry projected through the camera, so it is proportional to viewport height. A fixed-pixel tag therefore takes a bigger share of the gap between dummies the shorter the viewport is. The badge overlap in 6c is the other half and is **not** device-dependent — badge and name are both in the tag's own pixel space, so it reproduces identically on PC, which is why 6c applies to both metric sets
7. When there is a real party (2+ visible dummies), stacks a "★ Party Owner" badge above the owner's name
8. Rebuilds all dummies when party state updates or character respawns. The `CharacterAdded` work is a **named function invoked on both edges** — connected for future lives, and called directly when a character already exists as the script reaches that line. `CharacterAdded` does not fire retroactively, and the bounded `WaitForChild` gates in item 0 can let the character win that race; the initial life then gets no camera lock and **neither** of the two rebuilds, so a dummy built before its grip rig replicated (item 4d) stayed empty-handed for the entire session, long after the loading screen lifted. That is the *visible* half of the missing-weapon reports, as against the log-only half that the retry always healed. The pass is deduped on the character instance, so the fallback racing a real `CharacterAdded` cannot run it twice for one life; a respawn is a new instance and still runs
9. Listens to PartyInviteEvent "PartyUpdated" to refresh lobby visuals; also rebuilds when the local player's `EquippedCharacter` / `EquippedSkin` / `EquippedTitle` attribute changes, and on every `EquippedWeaponSkin_<Hunter>` attribute, so the dummy + name tag + held gun update live
