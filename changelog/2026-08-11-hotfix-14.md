# Hotfix 14 — 2026-08-11

**Summary:** Onboarding opens to every new player — the training run now ends on a kill and hands the canyon over for two minutes, a player who joins a friend is never diverted into it, and the veteran check that would have locked those players out for good is now a one-time decision.

---

- **Published:** `Bounty r2354 · Redwater r2487` — published 2026-08-11.
- **Rollback target:** `Bounty r2342 · Redwater r2458` (Hotfix 13) — revert **both** places. Reverting restores code only: the 250 Cash paid to players who completed training, and the `Tutorial.Screened` field written to every profile that has loaded since, both survive a revert. The field is harmless to older code, which ignores it — but a reverted build resumes re-running the veteran check on every load, so any account screened as a newcomer and since played would be re-classified as a veteran.
- **Shared modules:** `GameEnvironment` (version bump only) — mirror verified ✅
- **Profile schema:** v2 (unchanged — `Tutorial.Screened` is a template key added by `Reconcile`, **not** a `DATA_VERSION` bump, so a rollback is safe and older code simply ignores the field)
- **Data-affecting:** **yes** — the tutorial's 250 Cash award now reaches real players, and the profile gains one field
- **Note:** most of this release was published to live mid-development on 2026-08-11, ahead of the roll, under the Hotfix 13 version string. The publish that carries this entry is what brings the rollout gate, the follow-join fix and the version tag live.

## Added

- **Listed test accounts re-earn the training bonus on every run** (Redwater,
  `ServerScriptService.Onboarding.TutorialDirector`). The 250 Cash is once per account, so an
  account replaying the lesson to check it was paid nothing from its second run on — and because
  the completion card refuses to claim a payout that did not happen, its reward line went blank
  too. The accounts that replay the lesson are exactly the ones that need to see what a real
  first-timer sees, so for listed accounts the once-only rule is lifted. **Players are unaffected:**
  a genuine first completion still pays 250 and any replay still pays nothing. Checked against the
  UserId server-side in Redwater rather than carried in the join payload, which arrives with the
  client and would have made this a repeatable Cash farm. `BonusPaid` is still written, so clearing
  the switch restores the once-only rule without touching any stored profile.
  *Data-affecting: awards real Cash to the listed accounts, once per completed run.*

- **The training run ends with two minutes of the canyon to yourself.** The last step is the only
  real fight in the lesson, and it used to be a thirty-second stopwatch that ended whether or not
  the player had hit anything — taking the map away at the exact moment the fight got going, and
  leaving the one step with a fight in it with nothing to actually win. Now it ends the moment the
  player takes a bot down, and the run does not end with it: a card thanks them, a second says the
  match closes in two minutes and that they can keep playing or leave whenever they like, and then
  the canyon stays open with the bots still respawning. Leaving early and staying the full two
  minutes are the same run — the reward is paid when the lesson finishes, not when the player
  eventually goes, so EXIT TRAINING during those two minutes pays the same 250 and shows the same
  card back in the lobby. A player who never lands a kill is not stuck either: the step gives up
  after 30 seconds and hands them the same cards, the same reward and the same two minutes to keep
  trying in — nobody waits longer than that on the tutorial before it lets them finish. The closing card reads
  its own duration from the server, so the promise and the timer cannot drift apart. Both cards sit
  on their own authored label rather than borrowing the welcome card's — smaller text, on screen
  two seconds longer, and **each on a single line at every screen size**, which needed the label to
  stop auto-scaling its text: auto-scaling *is* wrapping in Roblox and cannot be told otherwise, so
  the text size is now solved from the width of the screen it is actually on. That also makes the
  two cards the same size as each other, which auto-scaling did not — a line that fits on one row
  is scaled by width and a wrapped one by height, so the shorter card came out half again as big.
  Their dark plate is padded either side of the line to match the instruction banner's, rather than
  being cropped to the exact width of the text. Checked against the banner side by side on a phone,
  a tablet and a laptop, plus a narrow desktop window. The end of the two minutes is also silent
  now: it used to play the step-completed chime and the green screen flash on the way out, which
  was correct back when the run ended the instant the last step passed, and became an achievement
  announced over nothing once two minutes of free play sat in between. The screen just clears and
  the player is sent back.

## Changed

- **Onboarding is open to real first-time players.** The training run has been gated to a single
  listed test account for as long as it has existed; that gate is now off (Bounty,
  `ServerScriptService.Onboarding.TutorialDispatchService`), so a genuinely new player is dispatched
  into it on their first lobby join and who gets it is decided by the profile conditions alone.
  Flipped only after the whole flow was driven end to end on live, on PC and on a phone. **Existing
  players are not swept into it:** the grandfather rule silently marks any profile carrying matches
  played, playtime, a level above 1 or any XP as already completed, and does so before the
  dispatcher can see that profile at all — so the only accounts this reaches are ones that have
  genuinely never played. The gate is a single switch and narrows back to the listed account the
  same way if the flow ever needs re-driving on live.

- **The listed test account stops re-earning the training bonus** (Redwater,
  `ServerScriptService.Onboarding.TutorialDirector`) — this supersedes the entry under Added above.
  The two switches only ever worked as a pair: with dispatch back on the stock rules the listed
  account is no longer exempt from the once-only condition either, so the only route to a second run
  is a wiped profile, and a wiped profile earns the 250 by the ordinary rule. Left on it would have
  bought nothing and stood as a repeat live payout keyed to a hardcoded account id.
  *Data-affecting: removes a repeat award. No stored profile is touched — the switch was always read
  live, and `BonusPaid` was written on every run regardless.*

- **The training run no longer asks.** A first-time player used to get a welcome card with START
  TRAINING, "skip for now", and a 4-second auto-advance — a four second window in which they could
  decline onboarding outright. The card is now a non-interactive "Saddling up..." hold and the
  teleport is issued the moment the profile loads. The dispatch also moved off the client while it
  was being changed: the teleport used to depend on a LocalScript having connected its listener
  before the server fired the invite, and a RemoteEvent with no listener is silently dropped, so
  losing that race meant the tutorial never happened with nothing anywhere reporting it. The server
  now dispatches itself and nothing waits on an answer. The `"skip"` handler was removed with it —
  with no way left to decline, leaving it connected meant a stray response could stamp
  `Skipped`/`Completed` onto the profile of a player already mid-teleport, permanently.
  *(Still behind the test-account gate; no player reaches this yet.)*

- **The training-complete card reads its reward instead of describing it.** Coming back from the
  training run the card now says TRAINING COMPLETE over a REWARD line carrying the cash icon and
  the amount, in place of a `+250 CASH` text line — and it is the same icon the currency chip in
  the corner uses, so the card names the thing the player then watches go up. It also closes on a
  click anywhere outside it rather than only on its own six-second timer.

- **The five tutorial scripts were stripped back to working comments.** Onboarding is finished, so
  the running commentary it was built under — dated "it used to do X", playtest-by-playtest
  archaeology, design-doc section references — came out of `TutorialClient`,
  `TutorialDirector`, `TutorialDispatchService`, `TutorialWelcomeController` and
  `TutorialCompleteController`. 3376 lines to 2968, 152KB to 119KB, comment-only lines 1180 to 772.
  What stayed is the part that stops a regression: the measured icon aspect/fill table, the
  `STEP_BEAT` keep-in-sync pair across the two halves of the step machine, why the HUD lookup goes
  through `findHosts` rather than a ScreenGui name, why the approach bots carry no leash, and the
  test-account switch. **No behaviour changed, and that is verified rather than asserted** — the
  code lines of each file were checksummed before and after, and every one matched, the two
  exceptions being trailing `(design 4.7)` suffixes inside comments on two `return` guards.

- **The card shown while a first-time player is sent to training says what is happening.** It read
  "Saddling up...", which named a mood rather than the fact that the game is about to pull them out
  of the lobby; it now reads "Teleporting to tutorial match...". The card was also reshaped around
  the buttons it lost when the choice was removed — it had stayed a tall panel with an empty bottom
  half, 58% of the screen height on a phone, and is now wide and short at 28% with the line centred
  under the title. Checked on an 800x360 phone, an iPhone 13 and an iPad.

## Fixed

- **A new player who joins a friend now plays with their friend — and still gets the tutorial
  later.** Opening onboarding to everyone made this reachable for the first time. Clicking Join on a
  friend drops you in the lobby with a "follow" marker rather than a teleport, which the dispatcher's
  arrival check could not see, so a brand-new player was claimed by two things at once: forwarded to
  the friend's match *and* sent a private training server. Whichever teleport lost, the training run
  was already counted as attempted — and two invitations from friends could have disqualified an
  account from onboarding without it ever seeing a step. The other branch was worse: being pulled
  out of the match you were just invited to.

  Friends now always win. A follow-join is never diverted into training, nothing is written to the
  profile, and the lesson is waiting the next time that player opens the game on their own — where
  they can still skip it in seconds if they would rather not.

  That second half needed a second fix. The rule that keeps existing players out of the tutorial
  judges by matches played, playtime, level and XP — and it re-ran on **every** join, so one match
  with a friend was enough to classify a day-old account as a veteran and close onboarding to them
  for good. It is now what it was always meant to be: a one-time decision, made the first time an
  account is ever seen and never revisited, answering "was this account here before onboarding
  existed?" — which cannot change afterwards. Existing players are screened exactly once and are
  unaffected. It also repairs something already broken: a training run that failed to start returns
  the attempt so the player gets another go later, and that return was worthless the moment they
  played a round in between.
  *Data-affecting: adds one field to the stored profile. Reconcile-safe, no schema version bump,
  and a rollback is still safe — the field is simply ignored by older code.*

- **Every teleport out of Redwater now retries.** Four paths send a player back to Bounty and two
  of them had no ladder of their own. The **tutorial return** was one `TeleportAsync` and a warning,
  leaning on the server's shutdown drain to try again — and that drain rebuilds a solo player's
  payload and kept only the character selection, so every retried tutorial return arrived with no
  record of having finished, on a run that had already been paid for: the player got their Cash and
  nothing on screen ever said so. It now retries three times on a growing delay and handles the
  failure shape a clean `TeleportAsync` return cannot report — the teleport dying *during*
  initiation, which reached nothing at all here because this module never listened for it. The drain
  carries the completion through as well now, so the last resort promises the same thing the ladder
  does. **Forwarding a late joiner** was also a single attempt; it retries three times too. It
  should be unreachable — Redwater is reserved and Bounty refuses to route into a closing match —
  but when it does run, the alternative to a retry is parking someone in a match they are not in
  until the podium finishes. `LeaveToLobbyService` and the soft-shutdown evacuation already had
  ladders and are unchanged; all four now use the same constants and behave alike under the same
  Roblox failure. `Unauthorized` stays terminal everywhere: no retry fixes a permissions problem.

- **The ultimate step could strand a player with no way to finish it** (Redwater,
  `ServerScriptService.Onboarding.TutorialDirector`). High Noon spends the entire charge on the F
  press and then ends on its own five seconds later whether or not a shot was taken, but the step
  only advances on the shot. A player who opened it and then hesitated — or who mashed fire inside
  the 1.5s minimum, which the client drops before it reaches the server — had spent the ultimate
  with no way to earn another in a lesson that has no combat pressure, and sat out the rest of the
  step's timeout. The step now watches the charge and refills it, twice at most, whenever High Noon
  is not running and the bar is short, giving each retry a fresh timer. It reads the caster's own
  `IsUsingHighNoon` state rather than a delay, so a refill cannot land mid-ultimate and hand out a
  second free cast.

- **…and it advanced without teaching the ability.** The same step accepted any trigger pull during
  Dead Eye, locked on or not — the client sends the shot request once the 1.5s minimum has passed
  whether or not a skull ever appeared — so aiming at empty air and firing passed a step whose
  entire subject is locking on. It now requires both halves of what its own line asks for: a target
  actually locked, then the shot. Dust's ultimate publishes a `HighNoonHasLock` state off its live
  target scan for the step to read (written only when it changes, since that scan runs ~12×/second;
  nothing in normal combat branches on it, so live play is unaffected). **This ships with the
  refill above and not separately** — a strict gate on its own would have turned the commonest ult
  mistake into a hard stall, and the refill on its own left the step passable without learning
  anything. A miss now costs the attempt, not the step.

- **A first-time player could permanently lose their training run to a transient teleport failure**
  (`ServerScriptService.Onboarding.TutorialDispatchService`). The dispatch was the only teleport in
  Bounty with no retry ladder, and it had three failure shapes: `ReserveServer` returning nothing,
  `TeleportAsync` throwing, and — the silent one — `TeleportAsync` returning cleanly and the
  teleport dying during initiation. That last case reached **nothing**: this module never connected
  `TeleportInitFailed`, and the three services that do all filter to their own senders, so it
  produced no retry, no failure message to the client, and no warning in the log. The player just
  stayed in the lobby.

  Worse, it was permanent. `Attempts` is incremented *before* the teleport — deliberately, since a
  crash mid-lesson can't be observed from the lobby — and dispatch refuses at `Attempts >= 2`, so
  two Roblox hiccups disqualified the account from onboarding for good and the grandfather rule then
  read `Attempts > 0` as "already in the flow".

  Now: the reserve retries 3× with a growing delay; `TeleportAsync` retries 3× building fresh
  `TeleportOptions` each time while reusing the same access code and funnel id (the reserved server
  already exists, and re-reserving would strand it and split one run across two funnel sessions);
  `TeleportInitFailed` is handled, gated on this module's own pending table so the four ladders in
  this place can't steal each other's teleports; and a failure the server actually *observed* rolls
  the attempt back, so only a real abandoned run costs one. Constants match FriendJoinService and
  FollowJoinService so all four paths behave alike under the same Roblox failure.

  **The test-account gate was hiding it** — a listed account skips the `Attempts` check, so a failed
  dispatch quietly worked on the next join. Verified instead by sandboxing the module against
  stubbed services and driving nine scenarios end to end.

- **Alt frees the cursor on the first hold — the mechanism was replaced, not repaired again**
  (Redwater, `StarterGui.ReworkedSettingsGui.SettingsController` + a new authored `AltUnlockModal`
  button in the same ScreenGui). Third attempt at open item **#26**: after Hotfix 8 and its
  hardening, the owner still reported (2026-08-11, live, Hotfix 13 build) that the first Alt hold
  of a live session does nothing. Both shipped fixes fought the camera scripts for cursor
  ownership — snapshot four properties, drop `CameraMode` to `Classic`, take the camera
  `Scriptable`, re-assert `Default` every render step, then restore or re-derive it all on release,
  with dedicated death/respawn hand-off paths — and both kept losing the first live hold to the
  engine's own first-person mouse lock, which no `MouseBehavior` write can override.

  The hold now uses the engine's sanctioned escape instead: a 1×1 transparent `TextButton` with
  `Modal = true` (authored, sibling of the gear) is made `Visible` while Alt is down. A visible
  Modal button is an engine rule — *"the mouse will not be locked unless the right mouse button is
  down"* — so the cursor comes free without touching `CameraType`, `CameraMode` or `MouseBehavior`
  at all, identically in Studio and live. The hunter clients' gameplay lock stays asserted the
  whole hold, merely suppressed; hiding the button on release is the re-lock. All snapshot and
  life-boundary hand-off machinery is deleted (~300 lines → ~115), which also makes **#28**'s
  stranded-Scriptable-camera failure unrepresentable. The only state still managed is the cursor
  icon: re-shown per frame during the hold (a respawning hunter client can write it false
  mid-hold), and set by rule on release — hidden only if alive in a live round. Side-behavior
  change: camera rotation during a hold now stops because the unlocked mouse feeds no deltas, and
  right-drag rotates it (same engine rule) instead of the old hand-built head-follow freeze.

  **Needs live verification (Studio has never reproduced #26):** first hold of a fresh session,
  first hold after each of several deaths, Alt-tab mid-hold, and no camera strand or jump on
  release. `OPEN-ITEMS.md` #26 carries the checklist.

- **"Couldn't join" was being drawn underneath the loading screen.** Trying to join a friend the
  game cannot reach put the explanation on screen immediately — behind the loading veil, which sits
  above it — and its own dismiss timer ran the whole time it was covered, so by the time the lobby
  appeared there was nothing left to read. The message now waits for the loading screen to hand off,
  and holds five seconds instead of three. The "Joining…" overlay still comes up instantly, because
  covering the lobby during a *successful* hop is the only reason it exists. Most visible when the
  friend is in a training run: that server is unlisted by design, so the join fails at the lookup
  almost immediately, which is exactly the case that was covered longest.

- **A large black box hung in the sky over the whole training run.** The victory showcase's backdrop
  (a 150x58x150 black box parked 226 studs above the canyon) is authored visible and hidden by the
  round loop at the top of every wait phase. The tutorial session replaces that loop outright, so
  the hide had never run on a training server: four of the box's six faces were in clear line of
  sight from the lesson spawn, and the first step of the lesson tells the player to look around.
  The session now hides it — and the finish pillars — on the way in, and never restores them.
