# Hotfix 1.1 — 2026-08-16

**Summary:** Players are no longer matchmade into a match on another continent, the onboarding tutorial moves into a purpose-built walled bowl, and the map vote folds into the match-summary screen — plus mobile touch buttons re-placed and re-sized from a real device, a seven-step first-time-player funnel replacing the retired `MatchLoop` one, Dead Eye no longer locking through wooden buildings, Vesper visibly holding her rifle on other players' screens, and bots no longer stranding on rocks.

## Added

- **Region-based matchmaking — players are no longer routed into a match on another continent**
  (Bounty: new `RegionService`, `MatchmakingService`, `PartyService`; both match places:
  `MatchDirectoryService`, `RoundController`; `Telemetry` in all three). A player matchmade into a
  distant datacenter played the whole round at 250-300ms. The lobby now refuses that: a group is
  only offered servers in its own latency region, and when none is open it reserves a fresh one
  rather than falling back to a distant match.

  Buckets are datacenters, not continents — `NA`/`SA`/`EU`/`AS`/`OC` over 101 country codes — so
  there is no latency table and no threshold to tune. Friend and follow joins are exempt and needed
  no code to be: the filter lives in `chooseDestination`, which neither path calls. Region rides its
  own MemoryStore map (`RedwaterServerRegions_v1`, 3600s TTL); why it is not a field on
  `RedwaterAccessCodes_v1` is commented at both writers. New `MatchPing` event ships with it —
  one row per player per match, the only instrument that can tell us whether this works.

  **This is the one rule in `MatchmakingService` that refuses to pack players together, and it will
  cost matches.** In a thin region the first player to press Play seeds a server and fights bots.
  That is the owner's explicit call (2026-08-15): a bot-padded local match beats a full one at
  300ms. Accepted, not overlooked. Two failure paths are permissive on purpose — see `isRegionMatch`
  before tightening either, because failing closed on a country-lookup outage turns the whole game
  into bot matches.

  **Reverting** restores cross-region matchmaking: the pool re-merges and fills faster, at the cost
  of the 300ms matches. Nothing persists — the MemoryStore keys expire on their own and old code
  ignores the extra entry field. Not data-affecting.

  **Verified only as far as Studio allows.** All seven scripts recompile; the three mirrors are
  byte-identical across places (`Telemetry` 26887 in all three, `MatchDirectoryService` 31425 and
  `RoundController` 97638 across both match places); the country table has no duplicates (101 codes)
  and was spot-checked on 39, including the two that look like bucket names (`SA` Saudi Arabia →
  `EU`, `AS` American Samoa → `OC`). **Every routing decision is unverified and only verifiable
  live** — MemoryStore needs API access, teleports do not run in playtests, and the gate needs
  concurrent players in two regions. It also rests on an undocumented assumption Studio cannot test:
  that a reserved server is allocated near its first joiner. This **builds open item #92** (owner
  request, 2026-08-05), which stays open for the live verification and records the two places the
  build deliberately departed from how that item was written.

- **`NewPlayer` funnel — the first-time arc, end to end** (all three places, `Telemetry`,
  `DataService`, `PartyService`, `TutorialDispatchService`, `TutorialDirector`, `RoundController`).
  Seven steps: First Join → Tutorial Started → Tutorial Completed → Returned To Lobby → Queued For
  Match → Joined Match → Finished Match. One funnel per **account**, not per session — the id lives on
  the profile, so it spans both places, rejoins and days with nothing carried in `TeleportData`.
  Minted on the grandfather screen's un-grandfathered branch in **both** Bounty and Redwater, since a
  follow-joiner's first profile load happens in a match; an empty id means "not a new player" and
  drops every step, which is what keeps the existing player base out.

  Its steps are deliberately **not** implied by each other — playing a match does not imply finishing
  the lesson — so `Telemetry.logJourneyStep` enforces the order against `Tutorial.JourneyStep`
  instead of relying on it. That is the whole reason the funnel is trustworthy and the one thing not
  to "simplify" later: without it Roblox's skipped-step backfill makes the chart read better the more
  players skip the tutorial. Rationale is in the source, above `JOURNEY_STEPS`.

  **Reverting** loses the chart and stops the profile writes; nothing player-facing changes. **Gate
  verified** 2026-08-15 by sandboxing the shipped `Telemetry` source with three lines stubbed (Studio
  gate, `AnalyticsService`, `DataService`) and driving eight cases — empty id, out-of-order, repeat,
  the full 1→7 walk, and a 3→6 skip. Call-site timing needs live data to confirm (open item #117,
  which carries the four checks that close it); first read is
  whether step 1 is materially larger than step 2.

## Changed

- **The map vote now happens on the match-summary screen, and the whole post-game is 13s instead of
  21.5s** (both match places). The ballot tiles sit in the band under the podium and appear the
  moment the stats card and podium land; `MAP_VOTE_TIME` and `SUMMARY_COUNTDOWN_TIME` are both 6 and
  are now **one window** — the "NEXT MATCH IN" counter times the ballot, which is why the vote panel
  no longer draws a timer of its own. `MAP_VOTE_RESULT_TIME` 2.5 → 0.5, now the tail of
  `POST_GAME_VICTORY_TIME` rather than time after it. Tiles went square → 1.6 landscape, and gained
  a vote-gated glow (the gold `UIShadow` is on only while that map has votes) and a press bounce.

  Revert: `Config`, `RoundController` and `MapVoteClient` must revert **together**, both places.
  `runMapVote()` was replaced by `runMapVoteDuringSummary()`, which now *is* the round loop's
  post-game wait — an old `RoundController` against a new `Config` waits the summary out twice, and
  a new `RoundController` against an old `Config` breaks the equal-window invariant. The GUI
  geometry does not revert with a script.

  Playtested on phone, tablet and PC.

- **RETURN TO BOUNTY moved to the top-middle of the screen and lost its 44px touch floor.** It is
  pinned 10px from the physical top edge exactly the way the settings gear and mobile Stats button
  are (`AnchorPoint` Y 0, flat pixel offset rather than a scale), and is `{0.44, 0.364}` — 65x24 on
  an iPhone 7 where it was 83x44.

  The floor is **deleted, instance and code** (`CanvasReturnToBounty.TouchFloor`,
  `MIN_TOUCH_TARGET_PX`). `UI_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §4.6 states there is no blanket 44x44 rule in this
  game and that adding one is not a fix; this constant predated that measurement by a week and then
  outlived it while still citing §4.6 as its authority. All it achieved here was holding the button
  at 44px tall on precisely the devices it was being shrunk for.

  Revert: restoring the constant alone does nothing, because the constraint instance is gone and GUI
  instances do not revert with a script. Playtested on phone, tablet and PC.

- **The stats panel is ~13% wider** — `Canvas` 0.30 at 0.15 → 0.34 at 0.17, which is anchored at its
  centre so the centre had to move with the width or the left edge walks off screen. On a phone it
  had visible empty space beside it.

  The podium band that keeps the 3D shot clear of the card now reads the panel's geometry off the
  authored `Canvas` rather than the literal `0.15 + 0.15 * scale` it had hardcoded, so widening the
  card walks the podium right on its own. Revert those two together: a widened card against the old
  band stands the podium on the scoreboard, and the framing solver reports success either way
  because it solves whichever problem it is handed. Playtested on phone, tablet and PC.

- **Mobile combat buttons re-placed and re-sized** (Redwater + Frostbite,
  `HunterMobileClusterLayout`, the four `HunterMobile*Button` modules, `VesperScopeController`, and
  the five authored `StarterGui` buttons). The owner dragged and resized all five by hand in a
  Frostbite playtest on a Galaxy A06; those pixel rects are now re-expressed in the layout module's
  own units so every touch device reproduces the same shape. FIRE and JUMP grew to 0.200H, SPRINT
  shrank to 0.140H, SCOPE holds at 0.184H. SCOPE now owns the outer column alone with JUMP one notch
  inboard, JUMP is the lowest button and the base every vertical offset is measured from, and the
  cluster sits 40pt off the bottom edge instead of hugging it — which is what lets the ability row
  pass underneath. FIRE and JUMP no longer touch at the corner.

  RELOAD is the one size that was not simply read off the screen: its authored 0.100H had been
  silently overridden by its own 44pt `MinSize` on the reference device, so 0.100 was never the size
  anyone signed off. It is now 0.1226H — identical there, proportional everywhere else.

  The five authored `MaxSize` ceilings moved with it (135/135/124/95/83). **Reverting the scripts
  without also reverting those five instances leaves the cluster deformed on tablets** — the ceiling
  engages per button, so maxima that are not in the same ratio as the sizes cross at different screen
  heights and some buttons clamp while others keep growing. Nothing persists; not data-affecting.

  **Verified** — the layout resolves to the hand-placed rects to 0.0px on all four edges of all five
  buttons, holds its shape within ~1px across all 19 device presets, and was checked live on an
  iPhone 7, an iPad 6th gen and a 13in iPad Pro. All six scripts recompile and are byte-identical
  across both match places.

- **Replaying the tutorial no longer costs the live onboarding funnel** (Bounty,
  `TutorialDispatchService`). `TEST_ACCOUNTS_ONLY` did two unrelated jobs: narrow dispatch to the
  listed accounts, and exempt those accounts from the once-only `Completed` / `Attempts` pair. They
  were welded together through `isTestAccount`, so the only way to re-run the lesson on a real
  server was to stop inviting every genuine first-time player for the duration — you could not
  verify onboarding without switching it off for the people it exists for. The exemption is now its
  own switch, `TEST_ACCOUNTS_REPLAY`.

  Safe to ship enabled because both restriction sites — `shouldInvite` and `dispatch` — test
  `TEST_ACCOUNTS_ONLY` **directly** rather than through `isTestAccount`, so a listed account can
  never narrow dispatch on its own; `TEST_ACCOUNTS_ONLY` stays `false` and unlisted players are
  routed exactly as before. Neither switch exempts anyone from the TeleportData rule, so a return
  leg from Redwater still lands in the lobby. `Attempts` still increments under either, so the
  funnel stays honest.

  **It ships `false`** — it was `true` through the tutorial-relocation verification run, because
  while it is on a listed account cannot reach the normal lobby on a fresh join and is dispatched
  into the lesson instead. **Reverting** re-welds the two behaviours and nothing else; no
  persistence, not data-affecting, no mirror involved (`TutorialDispatchService` is Bounty-only and
  not one of the 18). Verified by read-back: the module compiles and both restriction sites still
  read `TEST_ACCOUNTS_ONLY` directly.

  300 → 330; the `TutorialRun` aggregate gains a `|W<travelTeleports>` field.

  Revert: a place-version revert restores geometry and code together and nothing data-side moved —
  but code and geometry cannot be reverted separately, the lookup sites and the folder ship as one,

  Verified by read-back only: all five scripts loadstring-compile, no stale references remain, the

- **The lesson's first step is now the walk, and every walk has a guide beam** (2026-08-16, after

## Fixed

- **A mobile combat button could build a second copy of itself, printing a stray text label under
  the real button's icon.** Reported live on a phone joining straight into a Frostbite match: the
  jump button read `JMP`, which is a half-occluded `JUMP` from the legacy runtime fallback firing
  while the authored button was merely late to replicate. All five cluster builders
  (`HunterMobileJumpButton`, `HunterMobileFireButton`, `HunterMobileSprintButton`,
  `HunterMobileReloadButton`, and Vesper's scope button in `VesperScopeController`) now wait for
  the authored ScreenGui **and** its button before falling back, and the fallback draws the same
  icon the authored button carries instead of a label. Rationale is commented at
  `HunterMobileJumpButton._awaitAuthored`.

  Reverting restores the duplicate on any slow mobile join, and with it `JUMP` / `FIRE` / `SPRINT` /
  `RLD` / `SCOPE` printed over the HUD. Jump also changed order: Roblox's own touch jump button is
  now suppressed at client boot rather than after our button is built, and the touch build runs
  detached so it cannot block `ManuelJumpClient`'s character wiring.

  Unit-checked against three replication timings — late button, late gui, genuinely-absent gui —
  each now yielding exactly one gui and one button, and the old code reproduced as two. **Not yet
  playtested on a device** — open item #118; the repro is a mobile join straight into a match.

- **The "NEXT MATCH IN" countdown flickered between one and two lines on every tick** (reported on
  iPad). `TextScaled` and `TextWrapped` are coupled in the engine — setting either forces the other
  — and `TextScaled` + `TextWrapped` does not pick a size then wrap, it picks whichever of the two
  the box affords the larger text on. The label sat on that threshold, so single glyphs decided the
  line count and the tick pop crossed it twice a second. Measured at 1080x810: ": 6" and ": 4"
  wrapped to two lines, ": 5" ": 3" ": 2" ": 1" did not.

  This label and RETURN TO BOUNTY now both run `TextScaled = false` with the drawn size governed
  entirely by a `UITextSizeConstraint` whose cap is derived per viewport from the label's own width.
  That is the only combination giving one line **and** per-device sizing — turning wrapping off
  turns scaling off with it, which silently pins a label to its authored `TextSize` on every screen.
  The countdown's cap had been a flat authored 48, correct only at 1080p (48px of that copy is 232px
  wide against an iPad's 194px label). Desktop is unchanged at 48; phones and tablets resolve
  21-35px.

  Revert: GUI properties and script constants must revert together, both places. Swept all six
  countdown values against both pop states at 1080p with zero multi-line or overflow cases;
  playtested on phone, tablet and PC.

- **Dust could lock High Noon through wooden buildings.** The sight test walked the same penetrable
  cover the bullet does, and every wooden building in both maps counts as penetrable by material
  alone — so in Frostbite Claim one cast from the street outside the central house locked everyone
  inside it, on every floor. Acquisition is now two-tier: cover explicitly tagged or attributed
  `Penetrable` stays see-through as before, cover that qualifies only by material has to leave part
  of the rig visible. `BulletPenetration` gains `GetAuthoredPenetrable` / `IsAuthoredPenetrable` and
  an optional `overrides.IsPenetrable` for `Pierce`; the rule itself is in
  `HighNoonTargeting:HasLineOfSight`.

  Revert: code-only, both places, and the two files must revert **as a pair** — `HighNoonTargeting`
  calls a `BulletPenetration` export the old copy does not have. Damage is untouched: every other
  `Pierce` caller passes no `IsPenetrable` override, so bullets cross cover exactly as before and
  only what the ultimate can *acquire* changed. Bot Dust was never affected — `BotCombat` has always
  used a plain raycast with no penetration.

  Verified: both modules loadstring-compile and are byte-identical across the mirror, and the rule
  was measured against live map geometry before and after — Frostbite's central house drops from 147
  interior positions lockable in a single cone to 67, while Redwater's cacti, fences, wagons, barrels
  and crates stay lockable at 96–100%, unchanged. Playtested in Redwater and Frostbite.

- **Touch players got no auto-fire on the tutorial's range dummies.** Aim assist only considers
  enemy players and instances tagged `AimAssistTarget`, and auto-fire reads the assist's lock rather
  than casting its own ray — so an untagged target is invisible to both. The two rigs authored in
  the bowl carried no tag. Fixed as data: they are tagged now. Revert is removing the tag; nothing
  in code changed and no mirror is involved.

  This was a regression from removing the dummy-spawn path: the `ServerStorage.TutorialDummies`
  templates were the tagged instances, and when the lesson stopped cloning them the tag stayed on
  copies that no longer spawn. Line of sight was verified from the spawn pad (61/52 studs) and
  checkpoint 1 (25/18) using the assist's own exclusion set — the invisible tutorial walls are not
  excluded from it, so that check was load-bearing.

- **The HUD ability row reaches the touch cluster on tablets** (Redwater + Frostbite,
  `HunterMobileSprintButton`). On a 4:3 tablet the three ability tiles stranded ~137pt left of where
  the phone layout puts them, reading as a separate pad rather than part of the cluster. The row is
  clamped against whichever cluster button shares its vertical band, and on a tablet the tiles — and
  the intro tags, whose text size is a fraction of the tile — ran oversized enough that the band
  swallowed SPRINT, two buttons further left than the one that binds it on a phone.

  The row is now scaled against the jump button's diameter before it is measured. The ratio is the
  phone reference, so **every phone resolves to exactly 1.00 and cannot be moved by this**; 4:3 and
  16:10 tablets resolve to ~0.82, and a 1.6-aspect tablet is already in proportion and also resolves
  to 1.00. **Reverting** re-strands the row on tablets and changes nothing on a phone. Not
