# Hotfix 11 — 2026-08-07

**Summary:** Mobile auto-fire now takes the shots the server would actually score, sized to each
hunter's own hit rules instead of one conservative value — plus the stats menu rebuilt in the game's
visual language, and its counters starting each round from zero instead of accumulating.

## Added

- **The stats menu now dims the scene behind it, and clicking or tapping anywhere outside the panel
  closes it.** It also gained a close button — it previously had none, so the only ways out were the
  M key and the mobile Stats button. The dim is a full-screen backdrop that also *swallows the click*,
  which fixes a quieter problem: with the menu open, clicks fell straight through the panel into the
  world, so checking your stats mid-fight could discharge your revolver. On touch the Stats button
  deliberately stays above the backdrop and works as a second way out. Clicking the `STATS` header
  plaque does **not** dismiss the panel, even though the plaque overhangs the panel's top edge and is
  therefore geometrically "outside" it. Same contract the settings panel uses, including the reason
  the dismiss test cannot simply trust that the backdrop only ever sees outside clicks — non-Active
  frames inside the panel let a press fall through to it, so the press position is tested against the
  panel's rect instead.

## Changed

- **The stats menu has been rebuilt in the game's own visual language.** It was the last grey
  `(20,20,20)` panel in Redwater and the only one still setting `GothamSSm` at a fixed text size — a
  plain list of `Kills: 7` lines with no styling at all. It is now a weathered saloon panel: brown
  body with sharp corners, gold-rope outline, a `STATS` plaque straddling the top edge and an
  overhanging close button. **My Stats** leads with a hero strip carrying your level, an amber account-XP
  bar and your cash, then reads out **This Match** and **Lifetime** as tiles — the number large and
  amber, its caption small and muted — with time alive and dead on a line of their own. **Scoreboard**
  is now ranked by kills (ties broken by fewer deaths) with a rank column, and your own row is called
  out twice over, by an amber ring and an amber name. The panel is sized off viewport *height* with a
  locked aspect ratio, so it holds its proportions from a 360px-tall phone in landscape to 1080p, and
  it pops rather than snaps when opened.
- **The scoreboard shows every player at once instead of scrolling, and its rows scale with the
  panel.** Rows were a fixed 32px with a capped text size, which is right on a phone and strands the
  type at 17px inside a 990px-wide panel on desktop. Redwater seats ten humans at most and bots never
  appear on the board, so ten rows and a header now simply fit at every size.
- **The mobile Stats button is now part of the authored interface** rather than being constructed at
  runtime, and has been restyled to match. **Its size and position are unchanged and must stay that
  way** — the quest toast and the version tag both measure their own placement against this button's
  60×44 footprint at 62px from the right edge.
- Removed `PostGamePanel`, an empty leftover inside the stats menu that nothing referenced.

- **Mobile auto-fire now pulls the trigger whenever the shot would actually register, instead of
  demanding roughly twice the accuracy the hit rules ask for.** Each hunter's lock is sized to its
  *own* server hit radius rather than to one shared conservative value: Dust and Vesper 3.5 studs
  (`HitForgiveness.Radius`), Fortuna 2.4 (her lag-comp torso spheres — the only near-miss generosity
  her cards get, so the shared 3.5 would have thrown cards that cannot land), Bram 2.0 with a 7.6°
  floor, because his body damage is a fixed cone rather than a sphere and so must not shrink with
  distance. The old shared 1.6 was under half the revolver's own forgiveness, so auto-fire was
  *declining* shots the server would have scored — players hovered on an enemy and the gun stayed
  silent. **Bram gained by far the most:** he was firing only within 1.5° at 60 studs while his cone
  damages everything inside 7.6°. The sizing rule is now "match, per hunter"; it used to be "stay
  below every hunter's radius", which forced the smallest common denominator and produced the exact
  opposite of its intent. A 3.5 sphere at the root also spans a whole R15 rig (head top +2.19, feet
  −3.00), so hovering over a head or a pair of legs now locks — the old 1.6 sphere sat on the upper
  chest and reached neither.
- **Auto-fire's lock range raised from 200 to 350 studs,** matching the furthest the server will
  score a forgiven hit. This is an *acquisition* cut-off, so at 200 a mobile Vesper had no auto-fire
  whatsoever past that distance however well aimed — her rifle reaches 500 and her damage falloff
  only ends at 300. Per-hunter distance caps still apply on top (Bram 60, Fortuna 150). Note this
  also extends Dust's envelope to 350, where he deals his falloff floor of 20 body / 40 headshot;
  his "deliberately uncapped" note predates this and was written when 200 was the effective ceiling.
- **Vesper's scoped auto-fire no longer waits out the 3-second Deadeye Focus ramp** — scoped and
  hipfire now behave identically. Three seconds of dead trigger with the crosshair sitting on an
  enemy read as auto-fire simply being broken, and the bonus it was protecting is *headshot-only*
  while the lock aims at the body centre, so it was spending every scoped shot to preserve something
  that mostly did not apply. A player who wants to charge Deadeye holds fire themselves with
  auto-fire off in settings.
- **Vesper alone now waits for the crosshair to sit well inside the lock tolerance before taking a
  shot** (new `FireQuality`, 0.55). The tolerance is sized to the server's hit radius, so a shot
  taken at the very edge only lands if the client's view of the target agrees with the server's —
  and those diverge by roughly (ping × target speed) studs, which eats the entire margin against a
  strafing target. She has the scarcest shots in the roster (4 rounds, a 3s reload, 1.8s between),
  so a marginal miss costs her far more than a few frames' delay. The other three fire often enough
  that taking every chance is correct and are unchanged at 1.0. If she reads as too hesitant in
  play, this is the single number to raise.

## Fixed

- **Mobile auto-fire could fire with no target locked at all.** The lock-loss grace window — meant
  only to carry the acquire dwell across a brief dropout, so strafing behind a thin post does not
  reset a burst — also let the *shot* through, so a weapon cooldown maturing anywhere inside that
  0.12s window fired at wherever the crosshair happened to be. Reported from a mobile playtest as
  auto-fire "firing for no reason and then missing", and it was overwhelmingly a **Vesper** symptom:
  her 1.8s fire interval means her cooldown almost never matures on the frame she acquires, making
  it far likelier to mature during a dropout than Dust's 0.4s — and at her ranges the sub-degree
  tolerance flickers constantly. Grace now preserves the dwell only, never permission to shoot.
- **Enemies standing on rooftops or platforms could not be auto-fired at when viewed from below.**
  Line of sight was tested only against the target's root — hip height, and the first thing a
  parapet hides when you look up — so an enemy whose head and chest were plainly visible was
  rejected outright, which read as enemies going untargetable whenever you had to aim up at them.
  It now tests chest, head and root and passes on the first that is clear, matching what the server
  does anyway since its primary hit path strikes whichever body part the ray actually meets. A
  fully-hidden target is still correctly refused.
- **One enemy behind cover could suppress the lock on a second enemy standing in the clear.** Only
  the single closest-to-crosshair candidate was line-of-sight tested, so when it failed the whole
  acquisition returned nothing. Up to three candidates are now walked outward until one is visible.
- **The lock tuning in `RevolverConfig.AimAssist` was inert for three of the four hunters.** Dust,
  Bram, Fortuna and the shared first-person fallback never passed the lock keys into the controller,
  so they silently ran its module defaults — which happened to equal the config values, making the
  config look authoritative while editing it in fact moved Vesper alone. All five construction sites
  now pass them explicitly.
- **A target sitting exactly on the edge of the lock tolerance chattered in and out,** re-paying the
  acquire dwell on every flicker. An already-locked target now keeps a slightly wider tolerance.
- **The 4° acquire cone silently capped the lock tolerance** at every range where the lock sphere
  subtends more than that — inside roughly 50 studs — regardless of the configured radius, because
  the candidate search seeded its best-angle with the cone. The scan is now widened to whichever of
  the two is larger, and the cone gates camera magnetism only.

- **The stats menu (M on PC, the Stats button on mobile) now starts each round from zero instead of
  adding every round the server has played together.** Its middle block is per-match — the header now
  says `THIS MATCH` rather than `SESSION` — and both halves of the panel reset: the My Stats figures
  and the Scoreboard tab, which reads a separate set of player attributes and so had to be rewritten
  as well as the table behind it. A round-start push means a panel left **open** across the boundary
  repaints itself rather than sitting on stale numbers until it is closed and reopened. Launch issue
  #32. **This is a regression from Hotfix 9's round loop, not a launch defect**: these counters were
  correct while a Redwater server played one match and closed, because "session" and "match" were the
  same span. The match summary was never affected — it reads a different table — which is why the
  drift showed up in one panel only. One non-obvious coupling came with it: the alive-time reward
  measures against a running total of time alive, so zeroing that total per round required clearing
  the reward's high-water mark in the same step, or the reward would have stopped paying out
  altogether for the rest of a server's life. Side effect worth knowing: partial progress toward the
  next alive-time reward is now discarded at a round boundary rather than carried across it.
