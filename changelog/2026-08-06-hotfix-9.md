## Added

### The ultimate icon now lights up when the ultimate is ready

Nothing about the tile changed at full charge except the label reading "READY" — and Dust's meter
**hides its label entirely at 100%**, so a charged High Noon looked identical to an empty one. All
four hunters now get a gold light that breathes out from behind the ult tile.

New shared `ReplicatedStorage.Hunters.Shared.UltimateReadyFX` (`.new` / `:SetReady` / `:Destroy`),
driven from each hunter's existing charge handler. One module covers every hunter because all four
HUDs are clones of `StarterGui.ReworkedHunterGui` — the `StarterGui.HunterGui.*` trees still carry
the pre-rework `Ultimate > FrameBorder` shape but nothing clones them any more.

- **Roblox UI has no blur primitive, and establishing that cost three rejected attempts.**
  `UIStroke` is a hard-edged line — widening it makes a slab, not a glow. A single `UIShadow` looks
  like the answer and is not: its `Spread` dilates a hard silhouette with essentially no falloff, so
  it renders as a solid band hugging the tile, rejected twice (spread 0.22, then 0.16). A radial
  glow **image** behind the tile is structurally wrong here — the texture's bright centre is occluded
  by the tile itself, so only its dim outer tail is ever visible; it could be contained or visible,
  never both.
- **The shipped glow is 24 stacked `UIShadow`s** under a new authored `ReadyGlow` frame. Each is a
  *filled* dilation, so graded layers accumulate into real falloff. That is the opposite of
  `UIStroke`, which is an *outline* and stacks into concentric rings — conflating the two is what
  wrongly ruled stacking out on the first pass.
- **Stacked transparencies multiply, they do not add.** Normalising by dividing each layer's alpha
  by the layer count — the obvious way to stop "more layers" meaning "brighter" — caps achievable
  opacity near `1/count` and made the glow *impossible* to brighten. Each layer now gets
  `total ^ share` with the shares summing to 1, so the product telescopes back to the requested
  figure: `GlowTransparency` means accumulated transparency at the tile edge, and the authored layer
  count becomes a pure smoothness dial. Verified numerically — 24 layers composite to 0.1000 against
  a 0.10 target.
- **Hosted at `ZIndex 0`, beneath the charge fill**, so the light sits behind the *entire* tile: the
  interior keeps its authored dark and the ability glyph is untouched. Hosting it inside `Background`
  instead lights the tile face and is **measurably more visible**, and was rejected by decision — the
  light must sit behind the icon, not tint the tile. The quieter option is the intended one.
- **Tuned against the map, not a preview card.** Redwater's `Ambient` is (0.60, 0.43, 0.23) over sand
  at (202, 113, 58) at `Brightness 3.5`, so a warm glow on warm bright rock has very little contrast.
  A version signed off against a dark probe background was invisible in play and read as "the glow
  did not load" — it was working and simply could not be seen.
- **Cleared in three places on Vesper and Bram, not one.** Their `setUltimateCharge` returns early
  while the ultimate is active, so the charge push that would extinguish the glow never reaches the
  render path and it would stay lit for the whole window. Fortuna has no active guard and drives it
  from the charge branch alone.

Also carries a defensive guard for a defect seen once and never reproduced: a live client showed the
glow lit while the charge label read 88%, i.e. `SetReady(false)` running every tick and doing
nothing. `SetReady` no longer trusts its idempotent early return on the false path — a live tween
while the object believes it is dark is always a bug state, so it re-extinguishes. Root cause
unknown; tracked in `OPEN-ITEMS.md`, and an owner playtest showing no recurrence is not evidence for
a timing-dependent symptom.

**Verified by owner playtest 2026-08-06.** ⚠️ **Not measured on a phone** — 24 shadow draws and 24
tweens per lit HUD, and mobile is most of the player base. The layer count can be lowered purely for
performance without re-tuning, because brightness is solved against it.

Touched: new `UltimateReadyFX`; hooks in `VesperHunterGuiClient`, `BramHunterGuiClient`,
`CardsHunterGuiClient`, `HighNoonChargeUI`; authored `ReadyGlow` + 24 shadow layers under
`UltimateFrame.MainFrame` in **both** `ReworkedHunterGui.ScreenGui` and `HunterGuiMobile`. Redwater
only — none of these is a mirrored module.

### Ability and ultimate icons now pop the moment they come up

Neither existing readiness cue **moves**. The cooldown bar simply stops draining, and the ready glow
above fades in behind the tile — both easy to miss in peripheral vision at exactly the moment
readiness matters. Every ability tile now scales up and settles back the instant it becomes
available: off cooldown for the two abilities, crossing to full charge for the ultimate.

New shared `ReplicatedStorage.Hunters.Shared.HunterHudBump` (`.new` / `:Play` / `:Destroy`), same
shape as `UltimateReadyFX` beside it. It drives an authored `BumpScale` `UIScale` — six of them, one
per tile across **both** `ReworkedHunterGui.ScreenGui` and `HunterGuiMobile`.

- **The whole tile scales, not just the glyph.** `BumpScale` sits on `MainFrame` rather than the
  `Ability` ImageLabel, so the background, strokes, cooldown bar, keybind badge and the
  `UltimateReadyFX` glow all travel with the artwork. Chosen over the glyph-only option for
  legibility mid-fight.
- **Nothing reflows.** The `UIGridLayout` in `AbilityFrame` lays out the `1Ability` / `2Ability`
  **holders**, not `MainFrame`, so a scaled tile cannot nudge its neighbour. Measured at the shipped
  peak of 1.18: a desktop ability tile goes 86px → 101px inside a 122px holder cell, leaving 28px of
  clearance. Past roughly 1.28 that stops being true.
- **Fast rise, settling fall** — 0.09s `Quad`/`Out` up, 0.24s `Back`/`Out` back. The `Back` easing
  overshoots, which on the way *down* means dipping slightly under rest before landing; that
  undershoot is what makes it read as a bump rather than a zoom. A retrigger mid-bump restarts from
  rest instead of stacking.
- **The ultimate is rising-edge only.** `setUltimateCharge` runs on every charge tick, so a
  level-triggered pop would restart the tween continuously for as long as the ultimate sat charged.
  The edge is re-armed everywhere the glow is cleared — Bram's `isLastCallActive` return and
  `startLastCallCountdown`, Vesper's `ultimateActive` return and activation handler — so a recharge
  after the window still pops.
- **Cooldowns fire only on natural expiry.** `setSkillReady` gained a `bump` argument passed from
  the countdown-completion site alone; its other callers (initial wiring, respawn, the zero-length
  cooldown path) are restoring a resting HUD, and popping there would fire on every spawn.

Dust's ultimate is wired through `HighNoonChargeUI` rather than its HUD client, because that is where
its charge state lives. **Verified by owner playtest 2026-08-06.**

⚠️ **The wiring is duplicated four times, and that is the real finding here.** The animation is one
shared module, but the trigger point exists once per hunter — Fortuna's and Vesper's cooldown blocks
are character-identical, Bram's is the same plus one branch, Dust's is an older variant. Both bugs
below came out of that split and each had to be fixed four times. Specced for a later pass in
`HUNTER_HUD_COOLDOWN_REFACTOR.md`; deliberately **not** attempted in this change.

Touched: new `HunterHudBump`; hooks in `HunterGuiClient`, `CardsHunterGuiClient`,
`VesperHunterGuiClient`, `BramHunterGuiClient`, `HighNoonChargeUI`; authored `BumpScale` under
`1Ability` / `2Ability` / `UltimateFrame` `MainFrame` in both GUI variants. Redwater only —
`Hunters.Shared` is not mirrored and Bounty has no copy.

### Bram's Last Call now pops both ability tiles when it refreshes them

Last Call resets both of Bram's cooldowns on activation, and the HUD showed that only by silently
snapping the bars back to full. Nothing moved, so the refresh — one of the two things the ultimate
actually does — was easy to miss at exactly the moment it mattered. Both tiles now play the same
readiness pop they play when a cooldown expires normally.

Owner request. This is the second intentional behaviour change of the cooldown refactor below, and
the reason it is safe to make in one place instead of four.

- **The pop is guarded on the tile having actually been cooling**, and that guard is load-bearing
  rather than tidiness. Bram fires zeros down **two** paths: Last Call activation
  (`GrudgekeeperClient:1250`, one per slot) and `AbilityCooldownsReduced` (`:1265`), which fires on
  **every landed blast** for the rest of the window with both remaining times clamped by
  `math.max(..., 0)`. Once an ability is fully shaved, every subsequent hit re-fires `0` into an
  already-ready tile — so an unguarded pop would strobe both tiles for the whole ultimate.
- **Consequences of the guard, both intended.** Ulting with an ability already off cooldown does not
  pop it, because nothing was refreshed. And a blast that shaves the last of a cooldown away pops
  once, at the hit that actually finishes it, which is a genuine "it just came up" event.
- **Only Bram is affected.** Every other cooldown bindable in the place fires a real config value —
  Dust 4/8, Fortuna 3/5, Vesper 8/12 — so the zero branch stays unreachable for the other three.

Verified against the live module source (**not** `require`, which serves the plugin's cached
pre-edit copy and reported the new pop as absent): refresh while cooling pops once; refresh on an
already-ready tile does not pop; six consecutive zeros after one cooling tile produce exactly one
pop; a blast shaving to a real remaining time still counts down and pops at the end. The three
pre-existing cases — respawn mid-cooldown silent, natural expiry one pop, retrigger one pop — all
still hold.

⚠️ **Owner-confirmed on mobile, not yet on desktop.** The desktop playtest pass ran *before* this
pop was requested, so it verified Last Call resetting the tiles but not the new animation. Harness
plus the mobile pass cover it; one deliberate desktop look would close it out. Nothing else in the
cooldown work carries this caveat.

Touched: `HunterHudCooldown`, `BramHunterGuiClient` (comment only). Redwater only.

## Changed

### Hunter HUD skill cooldowns now run through one shared controller

No player-visible change on its own — this is the refactor specced in
`HUNTER_HUD_COOLDOWN_REFACTOR.md`, done because the readiness pop above cost four wirings and the
phantom-countdown fix below cost four identical edits on the same day.

New `ReplicatedStorage.Hunters.Shared.HunterHudCooldown` (`.new(canvas, names)` / `:Start` /
`:SetReady` / `:Destroy`), sitting beside `HunterHudBump` and `UltimateReadyFX`. Each HUD client now
holds one controller instead of its own copy of `setSkillReady`, `startSkillCooldown`,
`cooldownVisuals`, `activeSkillTweens` and `skillCooldownTokens`. Fortuna −98 lines, Vesper −98,
Bram −99, Dust −86.

- **The duplication was literal.** Normalising the skill names away, Fortuna's and Vesper's blocks
  were **character-identical** (1480 chars, same hash); Bram's was the same plus one branch; Dust's
  was an older variant taking the frames as explicit arguments. The module resolves both tiles off
  the canvas itself, so the hunter only supplies two opaque names in HUD order.
- **Token retirement is now structural.** `Start` and `SetReady` both retire the previous countdown
  through one private path, so the phantom-countdown bug cannot be reintroduced by a caller
  forgetting to. That defect already shipped fixed (see Fixed below) — this does not re-fix it, it
  removes the four places it could come back.
- **The pop is internal and is no longer a parameter.** `HunterHudBump` fires from inside the module
  only. The `bump` argument threaded through all four scripts is gone, and with it the chance of a
  restore path passing it.
- **Bram's zero-length branch now applies to all four**, and only Bram ever fires a zero. A cooldown
  cleared to 0 rests the tile immediately instead of building a zero-duration tween that would flip
  on the next 0.05s tick.
- **Deliberately not unified: the ultimate.** Three genuinely different `setUltimateCharge`
  implementations with three different early-return guards, and Dust has none at all. `getChargeSize`
  and `setFrameSize` stayed behind in the three clients that still need them for the ult, which is a
  real trap — only the skill half of their call sites went away. Dust is the one hunter that now
  requires no `HunterHudBump` at all, since its ult tile belongs to `HighNoonChargeUI`.
- **Teardown isolates the two slots.** `Destroy` takes one `pcall` per tile and drops its tile table
  before the loop, and the three clients that own an ultimate bump got their shared `pcall` split in
  two. It runs inside each HUD's own `pcall` during a live swap, so a throw on slot 1 previously
  abandoned slot 2 — countdown still running, `BumpScale` off its authored value — with the error
  swallowed upstream and nothing on screen to show for it. Pre-existing shape, carried over from the
  four copies and fixed here rather than preserved. Fault-injected with **both** bumps throwing:
  both countdowns still retire and the controller still clears.

Verified in an edit-mode harness (`start_playtest` hangs the plugin): all five scripts compile, and
eight behavioural cases pass — respawn mid-cooldown silent, natural completion one pop, retrigger one
pop, Last Call refresh while cooling one pop, refresh on an already-ready tile silent, six
consecutive zeros no strobe, a blast shaved to a real remaining time counts down and pops, and
`Destroy` mid-cooldown stops the label with `BumpScale` back at its authored 1. Sampled side by side
against the pre-refactor code over a 3s cooldown, bar `Size` and label `Text` matched **exactly** —
max delta 0.00000 over 21 samples. Both GUI variants were cloned into `CoreGui` and
screenshot-checked resting and mid-cooldown.

⚠️ **Harness trap worth knowing:** `require()` inside `execute_luau` returns the plugin's cached
**pre-edit** module, which reported a freshly added behaviour as absent and produced two convincing
false failures. Compile the live source instead —
`loadstring(src.Source:gsub("script%.Parent", "<absolute path>"))`, since `script` does not exist in
a `loadstring` chunk.

✅ **Owner playtest 2026-08-06, all cases pass, desktop and mobile:** respawn mid-cooldown, natural
expiry, instant retrigger, hunter switch mid-cooldown, Bram's Last Call refresh, and the mobile
ability buttons.

Touched: new `HunterHudCooldown`; `HunterGuiClient`, `CardsHunterGuiClient`, `VesperHunterGuiClient`,
`BramHunterGuiClient`. Redwater only — `Hunters.Shared` is not mirrored and Bounty has no copy.

### Fortuna's cards now land the shots that look like hits

Reported as her basic attack being hard to hit with. The throw is **hitscan** — `raycastCard` resolves
it the instant you click and the flying card is cosmetic — so travel time was never the accuracy
problem. The real cause is that `CardsConfig.LagCompensation.PartRadii` doubles as Fortuna's *only* hit
forgiveness, because she has no `RevolverHitForgiveness` equivalent, and those radii were set at
roughly the true size of the body parts. Dust gets a flat 3.5-stud bubble; Fortuna got spheres the size
of her target.

- **Forgiveness widened.** Torso/root 1.35 → 2.4, Head 0.9 → 1.3, and the eight arm/leg parts added at
  1.1 / 1.15 — 5 tracked parts to 13, mirroring the revolver's set. A graze on a strafing target's limb
  now compensates at all; before there was no sphere there to hit. Roughly 3.2× the original near-miss
  area, still under Dust's 3.5. **Head is deliberately the most conservative of the set** — a headshot
  is 42 vs 24, so it is the one radius where over-tuning inflates *damage* rather than just hit rate.
- **It now covers bots.** `findLagCompensatedHit` iterated `Players:GetPlayers()`, so not one value in
  that table ever reached a bot; it walks `HunterCharacterUtil.GetHumanoidModels()` now. Bots are
  sampled at **live** positions rather than rewound — a bot is server-authoritative with no ping to
  compensate for — and carry no `PartSnapshotPosition`, which is what keeps the card-landing correction
  on their live CFrame. **Expect little difference against bots in practice:** they already carry a
  global 4.5 × 3.0 × 3.4 `BotBodyHitbox` (2.25 half-width) that the direct ray hits first, so 2.4
  clears it by 0.15 studs. This is a player-facing buff almost entirely.
- **A cover test had to come first.** The radii could not safely exceed ~2.0 while
  `distance <= wallDistance + 0.25` was the only cover check: that rejects a shot *through* geometry the
  ray struck, but a ray sailing past a corner has `wallDistance == range` and nothing to compare
  against. New `hasClearLineTo` fires a blocking ray at the winning candidate — a plain ray, not
  `BulletPenetration.Pierce`, because cards do not penetrate. It runs at most once per candidate
  character and only on shots that already missed.

Verified: both scripts compile, and the radii were read back from a freshly compiled config.
`hasClearLineTo` was checked by extracting its **shipped** text out of `CardsServer.Source` and running
it against real temporary geometry — open line passes, solid wall rejects, cover slid across the target
rejects, another character in the way rejects. **Not** exercised under real latency: Studio ping is ~0,
so only the forgiveness spheres were confirmed locally, not the rewind.

⚠️ **Do not surface the cover test in any player-facing note.** Described publicly it reads as an
admission that shots used to register around cover, and invites probing the sphere pass for the next
one. "Backend improvements" covers it.

Touched: `CardsConfig`, `CardsServer` (Redwater only — neither is a mirrored module).

### Fortuna's cards fly at a constant speed instead of one that scales with range

`Basic.Speed` 220 → 1400 and `Ability1.Speed` 132 → 1400, both chosen so `Visual.MinFlyDuration` always
binds. The flight is now a flat 0.08s at every distance, rather than 0.08s point-blank stretching to
0.45s (basic) and 0.76s (Ability1) across the canyon.

This matters because damage now resolves instantly (see Fixed), so the card visibly lagged its own hit
by an amount that grew with distance — and a gap that *varies* is what reads as desync, not the gap
itself. **`Speed` is no longer a live knob** for either throw; `Visual.MinFlyDuration` is. That 0.08s
floor is left alone deliberately: at 30fps, which is most of the player base, it is already only ~2.4
frames, and below that a card stops reading as a thrown object and just appears already stuck.

**Not changed: the ultimate's ring** (`OutSpeed` 105 over a 35-stud `OutRange` = 0.33s), now the only
damage-dealing throw whose cards still lag their damage. Owner's call — the expanding ring is the ult's
visual payoff, and the 440 needed to match would snap all 20 cards out in a single frame.

Touched: `CardsConfig`.

### Redwater Canyon now loops matches instead of closing after one

A Redwater server used to play exactly one round and then kill itself — `closeServer()` teleported
everyone back to Bounty and drained. It now plays rounds continuously until it retires. Nobody is
auto-teleported to the lobby any more; the only ways out are the RETURN TO BOUNTY button on the match
summary and the settings panel's leave button, both of which already fired `LeaveToLobbyRequest` and
neither of which changed.

- **`RoundController.Start`'s round body is wrapped in `while true do`.** Every per-round reset
  already lived inside it, so an iteration is self-contained. Two things had to move: **`unfreezeAllPlayers()`**
  is now called in the loop tail — it only ever ran inside `closeServer`, and without it every player
  would carry the `VictoryFreeze` movement override, an anchored root, `ACImmune` and their
  `VictoryImmunityField` straight into the next round, unable to move or take damage. And the
  "Not enough players!" branch `continue`s back to waiting instead of closing the server.
- **New round phase `"Summary"`.** The buzzer sets it instead of `"Ended"`, which is now written only
  by `closeServer`. This is the whole reason **Bounty needed no change**: its `FriendJoinService`
  refuses on `phase == "Ended"` with "your friend's match has already ended" — right for a retiring
  server, wrong for a podium 17 seconds from the next round — while `MatchmakingService` governs
  `"Summary"` purely by the directory entry's `open` flag. `MatchDirectoryService` lists `"Summary"`
  alongside `Waiting`/`Countdown`/`Starting` as open, and reports a full `ROUND_TIME` remaining for it.
- **Server retirement (`Config.SERVER_RETIRE_AFTER_ROUNDS` = 5, `SERVER_MAX_LIFETIME` = 3600).**
  Evaluated after the buzzer, so a round in progress always plays out. After five rounds (~32 min) the
  server calls the new `MatchDirectoryService.setMatchmakingClosed()`, which forces the entry's `open`
  flag false **without** setting `closing` — the entry stays published, so friend joins keep working
  (they gate on `hardCap`, not `open`) and the lobby can still show the roster. Once retired *and*
  empty of humans it closes; the wall-clock cap is the second exit, worst case ~66 min. Both exits are
  re-checked inside the `MIN_PLAYERS` wait, which is the one place a looping server could otherwise
  sit forever.
- **Players who join during a summary are held for the next round** rather than bounced to the lobby.
  `forwardLateJoiner` is now reachable only on the `serverClosing` path. Such an arrival is parked at
  the jail spawn unfrozen and picked up by the next countdown; `lateArrivals` is cleared at the start
  of every round so the flag describes one summary rather than a whole session.
- **Analytics.** `MatchStart`/`MatchEnd` now fire once per round per player, which is correct — five
  rounds is five matches. The `MatchLoop` funnel needed explicit work: `renewMatchFunnel` mints a
  fresh session id for anyone who already reached step 5 on this server, replaying steps 1 and 3 onto
  it, because the funnel counts completions per session id and reusing one would both double-count
  step 5 and inflate lobby-to-round conversion. `DailyRewardService.registerPlay` is idempotent per
  UTC day and needed nothing; `RankedService.awardRound` is per-round and correctly fires each time.

Touched: `RoundController`, `Config`, `MatchDirectoryService`.

### Match summary collapsed from three steps to two

The end-of-round sequence was cinematic → podium + CONTINUE → stats + RETURN TO BOUNTY. It is now
cinematic → one combined screen: podium on the right, match stats on the left, intermission countdown
bottom-centre, RETURN TO BOUNTY bottom-right.

- **Step 1 halved, ~8.15s → ~4.08s.** A single `CINEMATIC_PLAYBACK_SPEED` constant (0.35 → 0.70) is
  now shared by the `AdjustSpeed` call and `getCinematicCutTime`, which divides by it — they are the
  same number and silently desync into a visible animation-reset frame if written twice. Letterbox
  bars, fades and the stadium hold were halved with it. **The muzzle flash could not simply be
  halved**: its old `min(3, cutTime * 0.62)` only landed correctly because the 3s cap happened to bind
  at 0.35x speed, so it is now expressed in clip time (`MUZZLE_CLIP_TIME / CINEMATIC_PLAYBACK_SPEED`)
  and lands on the same animation frame at any speed.
- **The CONTINUE button is gone**, along with the `CanvasContinue` subtree in
  `StarterGui.VictoryShowcase` and its wiring. The stats panel, RETURN TO BOUNTY and the countdown now
  fade in together once the #1 podium reveal lands (2.35s).
- **The local player's own podium dummy is gone.** The camera holds the stadium shot for the whole of
  step 2; the player's result is fully covered by the stats card. `tweenCameraToLocalPlayerPart`,
  `getLocalCameraPart` and their constants were removed, and `getFinishSpawns` no longer *requires*
  `PlayerLocal` / `CameraPartLocal`. It still resolves `SpotlightPartVictoryLocal` best-effort, because
  that part's SpotLight is authored on and an empty podium slot would otherwise sit lit.
- **The authored `TimerCanvas` → `TimerLabel` is finally driven.** It existed in the data model,
  referenced by no script. It now counts "NEXT MATCH IN: n" from the server-supplied `countdownSeconds`
  on the `VictoryShowcase` payload — never a client-side literal, or the panel drifts against the
  server's intermission. A `ResponsiveTextSize` constraint (`MaxTextSize` 48) stops the shorter copy
  ballooning under `TextScaled` (43px → 75px at 1080p) and removes the size jump at 10 → 9.
- **Three loop-safety fixes that did not matter when the server died after one match:** `Canvas`'s
  authored Position is cached once so the summary slide-in cannot compound its offset round over
  round; `hideVictoryShowcaseGui` parks the canvas back on it and hides `TimerCanvas`; and the RETURN
  TO BOUNTY handler restores its own authored transparencies after fading out, so a player whose leave
  teleport failed still sees the button at the next summary.

Touched: `VictoryShowcaseClient`, `StarterGui.VictoryShowcase` (`CanvasContinue` deleted, `TimerLabel`
copy + `ResponsiveTextSize` added), `Config`, `RoundController`.

### Podium framing on the summary screen is now solved, not tuned

Pushing the podium right to make room for the stats panel turned out to have no fixed answer.
`computePodiumFraming` solves the camera CFrame **and** the podium plate scale together, once per
showcase, against three constraints: nothing crosses into the stats panel, rank 3's plate stays on
screen, and adjacent plates do not overlap.

A flat "shift 15% of screen width, dolly back 9 studs" was measured failing two of the three: on 4:3
and near-square windowed viewports rank 3 landed at 1.02–1.03 of screen width and rank 2 fell back
inside the stats panel at 0.25, and at *every* aspect the 9-stud dolly collapsed the rank 1 → rank 3
plate gap to 234px against a 340px plate. `FieldOfView` is vertical, so visible **width** scales with
aspect ratio: on 4:3 the podium's own span already fills ~0.61 of the screen against a band of ~0.64
and the camera must dolly back before any shift fits, while on 16:9 and wider a dolly is actively
harmful — it costs plate size for room that is already there.

`getPodiumPlateScale`'s viewport-height rule (renamed `getPodiumPlateHeightScale`) is therefore now a
**ceiling** rather than the answer; the achieved on-screen gap can lower it. The solve takes the
smallest workable dolly, shifts the minimum needed to clear the panel, then spends leftover slack on
centring — but never at the cost of plate scale. Verified against all three constraints at 14
viewports from 667×375 to 3440×1440; 1920×1080 still resolves to plate scale exactly 1.000, so the PC
look is a pure rightward shift. The camera stays inside `VictoryScreenBoxModel` throughout (max dolly
used 11.5 studs of the ~63 available).

One thing the arithmetic could not catch on its own: because the plate scale is *derived* from the
gap, it lands exactly **on** the collision boundary whenever the gap binds — the numbers read a
satisfied `121 / 121` while the screenshot showed the rank 1 and rank 3 content bands abutting to the
pixel. `PODIUM_PLATE_GAP_MARGIN` (1.1) buys that slack back so neighbours read as separated. Inert
wherever viewport height binds instead, i.e. every ordinary desktop viewport.

Screenshot-verified at 667×375, 1024×768 (4:3), 1280×1024 (5:4) and 1920×1080. **Note for anyone
repeating this:** the device simulator does *not* change the edit camera's `ViewportSize`, so it
cannot check 3D framing across aspects at all — the previews were done by pillarboxing the Studio
viewport to a centred region of width `viewportHeight × targetAspect`, which reproduces that aspect's
projection exactly.

Touched: `VictoryShowcaseClient`.

### Match summary stats card is 29% larger on phones

`MOBILE_VICTORY_UI_SCALE` 0.7 → 0.9. At 0.7 the card drew **128×248** on an iPhone 14 — a narrow
strip in a lot of empty left-hand screen. A phone is height-constrained and the card is a
fixed-aspect object, so height sets the ceiling: 0.9 puts its top edge at 0.095 of the viewport,
clear of Roblox's ~36px mobile topbar, and 1.0 would put it underneath. Widening the card rather
than scaling it would mean re-laying-out its internals, which is a different job.

Nothing else needed changing, because `computePodiumFraming` reads the same
`getVictoryEndScreenScale`: the podium band walked right on its own (`bandLeft` 0.275 → 0.305) and
the solve absorbed it. Re-checked across nine viewports from 667×375 to 3440×1440 — all three
constraints still hold, and 1920×1080 still resolves to plate scale exactly 1.000, so PC is
untouched. Phone plate scale moves 0.398 → 0.374, tablet 4:3 0.686 → 0.647, both well clear of the
0.25 floor.

Touched: `VictoryShowcaseClient`.

## Fixed

### Bounty cards swapped places in the row (launch issue #29)

Two bounties placed within about a second of each other traded card positions roughly once per
second for as long as both were up. The trigger the report suspected — near-simultaneous
registration — was exactly right, and it is load-bearing.

The row was ranked on the scaled cash value, and that value is **quantised**. `getBountyReward` is
`BOUNTY_BASE_CASH + floor(duration * BOUNTY_TIME_SCALE_CASH)` with both constants, so cash steps
once every 2 seconds. Two bounties placed Δ seconds apart with Δ < 2 are therefore exactly **tied**
for 2 − Δ of every cycle and untied for the rest — and the two states were ranked by *different*
comparators: untied by cash, which favours the older bounty; tied by name, which favours the
alphabetically first. Whenever those two answers disagreed the pair flipped every publish. Δ ≥ 2
never ties, which is why bounties placed well apart were always stable.

**The client made it worse by re-sorting the server's array on `cash` alone, with no tiebreak at
all** — discarding the server's resolution, and `table.sort` is not stable, so a tied pair landed
wherever the sort happened to leave it. Slot assignment is positional (`entries[index]` into
`slots[index]`), so rank *is* screen position with nothing sticky in between.

- **Ranked on `startedAt`, oldest first.** Cash is a pure function of age, so this is the identical
  order with the resolution kept instead of thrown away — verified over 240,000 samples across
  4,000 random configurations: not once did it rank a cheaper bounty above a richer one.
- **The client's re-sort is gone**, with a comment saying why it must not come back. The general
  point is that a second comparator over the same data can disagree with the first; the defect was
  having two rankings, not the particular one that was there.
- **The tiebreak is `userId`, not `name`.** Roblox display names are not unique, so two players
  sharing one would land right back on an unresolvable comparison. It now fires only on an exact
  float tie between two `tick()` reads.

**The open design question — whether a card should hold its slot once placed — answered itself and
needs no mechanism.** A live bounty's start time never changes (`setBountyStart`'s only caller is
`placeBounty`, guarded by `not getBountyStart(target)`; every other write is a clear), so the
relative order of surviving entries is fixed for the life of the list. Cards move only when one
*leaves* it, which is a real rank change and still works. The ordering is sticky by construction.

⚠️ **This holds only while `BOUNTY_BASE_CASH` is a constant.** If base cash ever becomes per-target
— streak-scaled, say — age and value stop agreeing and the intended order has to be decided
outright rather than derived from either one. Both the code and the vault carry that warning.

Verified: both scripts compile, and the symptom was reproduced against the shipped constants before
being fixed — the old comparator swaps on 119 of 120 publishes for a pair placed 0.6s apart, the new
one on 0 of 120. **Signed off by the owner 2026-08-06**, on sign-off rather than a reported playtest
run.

Also corrected in the vault: `BountyListClient.md` claimed `applySnapshot` "assigns `LayoutOrder` by
rank". It does not — `LayoutOrder` is stamped once at registration to fix the three authored slots
in the list layout, and rank reaches the screen purely through positional slot assignment.

Touched: `BountyService`, `BountyListClient` (Redwater only — neither is a mirrored module).

### Fortuna lost her damage whenever she died in the same exchange

A confirmed hit paid nothing if she was killed while her card was still in the air. `raycastCard`
applied damage inside `task.delay(travelDuration, ...)`, behind a guard that aborts when the
*attacker's* humanoid is dead — so any trade inside the flight window silently voided a hit the server
had already resolved. At the old basic speed that was up to 0.45s of exposure per shot at max range.

`damageHumanoid` is called synchronously now. The hit was already decided by the raycast at throw time,
so there was never anything left to arbitrate by waiting; only the card *landing* stays delayed, so the
visual and the resting card still agree. `damageHumanoid` does not yield, so the inline call is safe in
the remote handler. This covers the basic throw, Ability1 **and** the ultimate's outward ring — all
three route through `raycastCard`. Recall is a separate path and is unchanged.

Side effect worth knowing: at long range an enemy can now die a fraction of a second before the card
visually reaches them. The constant-speed change above shrinks that window to 0.08s.

Touched: `CardsServer`.

### Bots tracked Fortuna perfectly through her invisibility ultimate

Reported as "it feels like they don't take her stealth into account at all", which was exactly right —
there was no code path that did. Her ultimate publishes three attributes, and `UltimateUntargetable`
had precisely **two** readers, both of them Dust's High Noon lock-on (`HighNoonTargeting` for a player,
`_ultimateCandidates` for a bot). Every other layer of bot perception reads positions, and positions
do not care about transparency: `_targetCandidates` collected her like anyone else, `_fight` tracked
her live root with `FacePosition` every tick, `CanSee` returned true because her parts stay
`CanQuery`, and `FireAt` raycast at her torso with 2.4° of scatter on Hard.

Her own ultimate made it worse. It **opens with a ring of cards**, and every bot that ring damaged ran
`OnDamaged` → `TargetLockedUntil = now + DamageTargetLockDuration` — **7 seconds of hard lock against
a 2.1-second invisibility window**. The escape tool was what pointed them at her.

- **One shared predicate.** New `HunterCharacterUtil.IsStealthed` reads `UltimateInvisible` (player
  Fortuna) **and** `BotFortunaInvisible` (bot Fortuna) — the pair `HealthBarController` already had to
  check for the same reason. Deliberately not `UltimateUntargetable`, which answers "may High Noon
  lock this" and must stay separate from Bram's Last Call; they coincide for Fortuna today but are not
  the same rule. This was the third subsystem to independently re-learn the stealth window, which is
  why it is a module function and not a fourth open-coded check.
- **She can no longer be acquired while hidden.** `_targetCandidates` excludes stealthed characters,
  so she is never newly targeted and never trips Vesper's closest-threat scan or her "Crowded" perch
  eviction. It deliberately does *not* un-target someone already being fought — the commit window
  holds `self.Target` regardless, which is what lets the next point work.
- **A bot now shoots at where it last saw her.** `_fight` keeps a sight memory and, while she is
  stealthed, faces, walks toward and fires at that remembered point. Standing still is therefore still
  punished and *moving* is what breaks the lock, which is the counterplay the ability is meant to
  sell. Reappearing re-arms `ReactionTime`, so a bot cannot snap back onto her the frame the
  transparency lifts.
- **The card ring no longer buys a target lock.** `OnDamaged` still records the damage and the threat
  position, so retreating and repositioning react normally to being shot by someone unseen — it just
  does not hand out the 7-second lock.
- **No ability aims at a hunter the bot cannot see.** One early return in `ThinkAbility` covers Dust's
  dash and Fan, Fortuna's burst and recall, Vesper's perch choice and pinning resolver, and Bram's
  charge and shell. It sits below `TryUltimate` on purpose: ultimates run their own candidate scans
  and never consult the brain's target, so gating them there would let her stealth suppress an
  ultimate aimed at somebody else entirely.

`_ultimateCandidates` is deliberately unchanged — Fortuna's own outgoing ring and Bram's proximity
gate still see her, because their player-side equivalents are plain raycasts and *untargetable never
meant unhittable*. Supporting changes: point-taking twins of the perception helpers
(`CanSeePosition` / `IsPositionInFireCone` / `GetFireConeDotToPosition`), a `FireAt` `AimPoint` option
that moves only the aim and leaves the shot's own raycast to resolve whoever is really on that line,
and `_combatMovement` now taking a position instead of a root part.

Verified without a playtest: the real `_fight` was driven through a simulated stealth window with
mocked controller/target and produces the intended sequence (fires live → fires at the remembered
point while she flees → holds fire on reappear for `ReactionTime` → resumes). `CanSeePosition` was
checked against real map geometry for all five cases, including the load-bearing one — a body standing
*at* the aim point still returns true, so a Fortuna who vanishes without moving is still hit.

**Known gap, left deliberately:** `AimAssistController` has the same blind spot on the player side —
mobile aim assist will still snap onto an invisible Fortuna. Out of scope for this pass by decision;
see `OPEN-ITEMS.md`.

Touched: `HunterCharacterUtil`, `BotBrain`, `BotCombat` (Redwater only — `HunterCharacterUtil` is not
a mirrored module and Bounty has no copy of it).

### Ammo, health and ability cooldowns did not reset between looped rounds

Only visible once Redwater started playing rounds back to back. Health, ammo and the client-side
ability state are all rebuilt on `CharacterAdded` — and a player who **survived to the buzzer never
fires one**, so they walked into the next round on whatever health they finished the last one with,
holding a half-empty cylinder. Ultimate charge was already fine (`UltimateChargeService` resets on
`RoundActive`), and so were bots: `BotService.PrepareRound` destroys every controller and clears the
records, so a bot's entire state is fresh by construction.

- **A fresh character is the single chokepoint for the first three.** New
  `SpawnService.respawnAllPlayers()` replaces `ensureAllPlayersAlive()` at round start and rebuilds
  *every* character, not just the dead ones. `hookCharacter` restamps `MaxHealth`/`Health`, each
  hunter client's respawn path refills its magazine and clears its cooldown sweeps, and
  `ServerWeaponGuard` rebuilds its per-character magazine and burst window (its state carries the
  character it was built for, so a new one invalidates it). Skipped on the server's first round,
  where there is nothing to reset and rebuilding characters seconds old would make a party of eight
  pay for two `LoadCharacter`s each at the busiest moment in the server's life.
- **That forced a phase fix.** The loop top now writes `State.roundPhase = "Waiting"`. `"Summary"`
  has to outlive the showcase, but leaving it set into the next iteration meant the respawn fired
  `hookCharacter` while the phase still read `"Summary"` — whose branch freezes everyone it cannot
  identify as a late arrival, after the loop's single unfreeze has already run. Every survivor would
  have been anchored in the cage for the whole next round.
- **Server-side ability cooldowns are cleared explicitly**, on the same `RoundActive` signal
  `UltimateChargeService` already used. They are absolute `os.clock()` deadlines, so they do not
  care that a round ended. New `ResetAll()` on `RevolverDashService`, `GrappleHookService`,
  `PinningRoundService`, `ShrapnelShellService` and `BreachChargeService`, wired up in all four
  hunter server modules. **Fortuna is why this is a fix and not a tidy-up:** her ultimate's 22s
  cooldown is the longest in the game against a buzzer-to-next-shot gap of only ~24s, so it was
  clearing on about two seconds of margin set by constants that have no reason to stay put.

Touched: `SpawnService`, `RoundController`, `RevolverDashService`, `GrappleHookService`,
`PinningRoundService`, `ShrapnelShellService`, `BreachChargeService`, `RevolverModule`,
`CardsServer`, `SunspikeModule`, `GrudgekeeperModule`.

### Players could jump all the way through the victory freeze

`ManuelJumpClient.canJump()` honours `Humanoid:GetStateEnabled(Jumping)` — but **`SetStateEnabled`
does not replicate**, so a lock the *server* applied was invisible to it. `HunterMovement` publishes
the `MovementJumpLocked` character attribute for exactly this reason, and nothing had ever read it.

`RoundController.freezePlayerForVictory` applies a `LockJump` override to every player for the whole
showcase, and a player could jump through it for the entire podium. The jump goes nowhere — the
freeze also anchors the root — but it still drives the state machine, which is enough to fire the
landing/impact effects over the shot. That is the "camera moves a little" half of the same bug.
`canJump()` now refuses while the attribute is set, which closes the hole for every other
server-applied movement lock at the same time (stuns, Bram's breach charge on a pinned victim).

Touched: `ManuelJumpClient`.

### The podium camera could be taken over mid-summary

The showcase assigned `camera.CFrame` once per shot and trusted it to stay put. `Scriptable` stops
Roblox's own camera scripts writing it; it does not stop another of ours, and one does —
`RoundClientController.restoreCamera` fires on the same `GameplayVisibility(false)` that immediately
precedes the showcase, and spawns a task that writes `CameraType = Custom` again *after* a
`WaitForChild("Humanoid", 5)`. Landing up to five seconds later, that is in the middle of the podium,
and it hands the player mouse look over the shot.

`holdVictoryCamera` now pins the frame every render step at `RenderPriority.Camera + 1` from the
cinematic's cut until `"Clear"`, re-claiming `Scriptable` as well as re-writing the CFrame, so the
framing is unmovable whatever the source. `restoreCamera` releases it **first** — otherwise the next
frame re-claims the camera and strands the player on the podium shot permanently.

Touched: `VictoryShowcaseClient`.

### The jail flashed on screen between the podium and the intermission

Clearing the summary hands the camera straight back to the player's own character, which is standing
in the holding cage, and the intermission camera does not take over until a second later. The loop
tail now fades to black *before* `VictoryShowcase "Clear"` and holds it through the respawn, the jail
teleport and the camera hand-off; `fadeOutForAll()` lifts it only once `startIdleCameraForAll` owns
the view — the same fade the round start already used in the other direction. One guard: if the loop
is about to *wait* for players, the curtain lifts first, so nobody sits on a black screen for an
open-ended wait.

Touched: `RoundController`.

### Step 1's cinematic showed about a second of a 2.7-second animation

`getCinematicCutTime` spun for up to two seconds waiting for `AnimationTrack.Length` to become
non-zero — with the clip **already playing** behind a shut letterbox, and with the cut timed from
*before* that wait. Everything the asset cost to resolve came straight off the only part the player
was meant to see, and it is worst on the first match end of a session, which is the only one most
players ever get.

The wait moved into `spawnCinematicAvatar`, in front of `track:Play`, so the full ~2.44s is visible;
`getCinematicCutTime` no longer waits at all. Two things stop that costing step 1 any wall time: the
clip is now `PreloadAsync`ed at script init (it is only ever used at a match end, so every client hit
it cold), and whatever the resolve does cost is measured and paid back out of the full-blackout hold
between the cut and the podium, which has slack and shows nothing. `CINEMATIC_LENGTH_WAIT` (0.55) is
bounded at what that hold can absorb above its floor — which is why `SUMMARY_CINEMATIC_TIME` did not
have to move at all: step 1 measures 4.08s warm **and** cold, and the client's margin under
`POST_GAME_VICTORY_TIME` is still +0.57s. `CINEMATIC_MIN_VISIBLE_TIME` (1.5) is a floor on screen
time measured from the letterbox opening rather than the clip starting; it does not fire today and
exists so a future retune cannot quietly take the animation back.

Touched: `VictoryShowcaseClient`, `Config` (comment only).

### A phantom cooldown counted down on an ability that was already ready

Die while an ability is cooling down, respawn, and the tile correctly snapped back to ready — then a
countdown reappeared over it and ran to zero on an ability that had been usable the whole time.

`setSkillReady` cancelled the cooldown bar's **tween** but never retired the countdown **thread**.
That thread guards itself on a per-skill token which only `startSkillCooldown` ever incremented, so
the respawn path left it running: it kept overwriting the restored blank label with a remaining time,
and on expiry called back into `setSkillReady` as though the cooldown had just finished.

Latent for as long as the code has existed, because on its own it was a label flicker. The readiness
pop above is what promoted it to a visible phantom bump on a tile that never went on cooldown, which
is how it was caught. `setSkillReady` now increments the token itself, so any in-flight countdown
exits on its next check.

Reproduced against the shipped structure before fixing — a respawn 0.3s into a 1.0s cooldown put a
stale `0.6` back on a ready ability and fired a pop at expiry. After the fix, three cases pass:
respawn mid-cooldown leaves the label blank with **zero** pops, a normal cooldown still completes
with **exactly one**, and retriggering mid-cooldown still yields **exactly one** (the newest only).

**Fixed in four places, one identical edit each** — the clearest argument for the shared cooldown
controller specced in `HUNTER_HUD_COOLDOWN_REFACTOR.md`, where token retirement becomes structural
rather than something four scripts have to remember.

Touched: `HunterGuiClient`, `CardsHunterGuiClient`, `VesperHunterGuiClient`, `BramHunterGuiClient`
(Redwater only — none is a mirrored module).

### Fortuna's ultimate read 0.0% for a moment after every respawn

Her `CharacterAdded` handler zeroed the ultimate meter. Nothing on the server agrees with that:
`UltimateChargeService` resets only on `RoundActive`, never on death, so a Fortuna who died holding a
charged ultimate still had it — the HUD simply misreported it until the next `UltimateChargeChanged`
push arrived and put the real figure back.

Harmless-looking on its own, and wrong in a way that mattered more once the tile could pop: the false
`0% → READY` jump on the correcting push also fired a phantom bump.

She now re-renders the **cached** charge on respawn instead of zeroing, which is what Vesper and Bram
already did — Vesper's code carries a comment explaining exactly this. Dust never had the defect;
`HighNoonChargeUI` feeds its cached charge back through `Update`. Fortuna was the only one left, and
her initial-wiring call still zeroes, which is correct there.

Confirmed while fixing it that **no path resets ultimate charge on death**: every
`chargeService:Set(player, 0)` in the place is round start, hunter swap, or spending the ultimate,
plus `CleanupPlayer` when a player leaves the server.

Touched: `CardsHunterGuiClient` (Redwater only — not a mirrored module).
