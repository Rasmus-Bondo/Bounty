**Summary:** Bram's Breach Charge geometry collapsed into one shared module, closing five charge
defects that previously had to be fixed per-implementation — plus the bounty list rebuilt as portrait
cards under the match timer, kill-cam swap cards showing equipped skins, and Alt no longer stranding
the camera after a respawn.

## Changed

- **Bram's Breach Charge geometry is now one shared module instead of four hand-copies** (Redwater,
  new `ReplicatedStorage.Hunters.Bram.BreachCarryGeometry`; `BramServer.BreachChargeService`,
  `Bots.BotCombat`, `StarterPlayerScripts.Bram.GrudgekeeperClient`,
  `StarterPlayerScripts.BramWorldFXClient`, `Hunters.Bram.Config.GrudgekeeperConfig`). Implements the
  root-`BREACH_CARRY_SHARED_REFACTOR.md` spec. **No gameplay change is intended by the refactor
  itself**; the three deliberate behaviour changes are listed under Fixed below.

  The charge exists in four implementations. The *decision* layer legitimately differs between them —
  a player charge is keypress-driven, camera-steered and multi-victim; a bot charge is server-driven,
  distance-scaled and carries one enemy — but the **geometry** does not, and had been hand-copied
  into all four, each with its own copy of `cos(55)`, `1.5` and `0.1`, and each carrying a comment
  saying it must match the others. That split is what made launch issue **#5** a three-implementation
  fix, left **#3** working on the bot path and missing on the player path, and produced a slam settle
  written twice that disagreed with itself. Wall test, clear-space clamp, lane offsets, carry point,
  slam settle and the past-the-plane test now come from one place, and every constant resolves from
  `GrudgekeeperConfig` at call time — the module deliberately holds no tuning literals of its own.

  **What was deliberately NOT unified**, because doing so would turn a refactor into a balance
  change: input and target selection; wall-detection *authority* (the client still reports at
  `ChargeWallCheckDistance` 5 and the server still re-verifies at `WallVerifyDistance` 18 — the
  module supplies the test and takes the distance as a parameter); single-victim bot carry against
  multi-victim player lanes; and each caller's own exclude list, which genuinely differs — a bot also
  excludes the `VesperHookSpotsBot` and `WallMap` folders, so a bot Bram cannot slam a victim into
  its own navigation scaffolding where a player Bram can.

  Placed in `ReplicatedStorage.Hunters.Bram` rather than `Hunters.Shared`, which is scoped to
  hero-agnostic code; in `ReplicatedStorage` because two of its four callers are LocalScripts.
  **Not mirrored to Bounty and does not need to be** — Bounty has no `ReplicatedStorage.Hunters` at
  all, and `GrudgekeeperConfig` remains Redwater-only and outside the 16 mirrored modules.

  **Verified by probe rather than playtest** (MCP playtesting is unavailable). All six scripts
  compile, and an A/B harness ran the old and new wall test over **10,944 comparisons** — 76 origins
  across real map geometry x 72 headings x both cast distances — with **zero** mismatches. The clamp
  matched over 4,800 cases, and `settlePoint` / `carryPoint` / `isPastPlane` match their old inline
  forms to within float noise (worst 1.4e-4 studs). The two historical baseline sites could not both
  be reused: `workspace.CHARGEPOINT` was deleted since Hotfix 3, which is why the sweep was broadened
  across the map instead of anchored on two points.

- **The bounty list is now a row of portrait cards under the match timer instead of a text list in
  the corner** (Redwater, `PlayerServices.BountyService` + `StarterGui.BountyListGui.BountyListClient`,
  with `RoundStatusGui.PhaseAnnounce` repositioned to make room). Each of the top three holders gets a
  live 3D portrait of the hunter **and skin they are actually wearing**, their name above it and the
  duration-scaled cash below. Players and bots still share one list and sort against each other.

  **The portrait spec is resolved server-side and sent as `{category, subfolder, modelName}`, never
  as a skin name** — the same discipline the kill-cam swap cards follow, so the name→model rule stays
  solely in `HunterSelectionService` and a card cannot drift from the body the shooter is looking
  for. For a player it is `GetSelection`, the live selection SkinService spawned them from. For a bot
  it is read off `BotConfig.HunterBehavior[<hunter>].DefaultCharacter` rather than rebuilt from the
  hunter name, so a BotConfig change moves the portrait with the rig; bots have no skins, since
  `BotService` clones that one path and nothing else.

  **It is resolved per publish, not captured when the bounty is placed, and the publish signature
  now includes it.** A player keeps their bounty across lives and can swap hunter from the kill cam,
  so a captured spec — or a snapshot suppressed as "cash unchanged" — would strand the card on the
  hunter they stopped being for the rest of the spree. Either field may be nil and the client falls
  back a step at a time (resolved model → the hunter's authored `ViewportFrame` → no portrait): a
  card losing its picture must never cost the row its name and cash.

  Portraits rebuild **only when the rendered spec key changes**, because the snapshot republishes
  about once a second as the reward ticks up and re-cloning a ~270-part rig at that rate is not
  affordable on a phone; hidden rows destroy theirs, since three hidden `ViewportFrame`s still render
  every frame. The dressing call is `pcall`ed inside the shared slot loop — a throw on one card would
  otherwise skip every later card and leave those rows stale, a cosmetic failure taking the readable
  part of the panel with it.

  **Two layout defects were found and fixed during the build, both worth recording because the
  symptom looks nothing like the cause.** A `UIGridLayout` wraps on *any* overflow, and three 0.3
  cells plus two 0.05 gaps computed to 278.84px inside a 279px frame — the cards stacked vertically
  on a sub-pixel margin. It is a horizontal `UIListLayout` now, which cannot wrap and centres a
  partial row for free. Separately, a `UISizeConstraint` floor of 92px — added as a legibility net —
  was itself breaking the scaling it was meant to protect, rendering the row at **26% of a 360px
  phone screen against 14% at 1080p**. The clamp was deleted outright rather than widened: any floor
  reintroduces the same class of problem, and at 320px tall a 64px floor put the row's bottom edge
  exactly on the phase banner. The row is now a flat **17.5% of viewport height on every device**.

  **`PhaseAnnounce` yielded the slot under the timer and moved below the row**, at
  `{0.5, 0}, {0.175, 68}` — the row's own height scale plus its y offset plus an 8px gap, so it
  tracks at a constant 8px everywhere instead of the 8px-at-1080p / 66px-on-a-phone a fixed offset
  gave. **That duplicated `0.175` is a hand-maintained coupling**: nothing reads the bounty panel's
  geometry at runtime and nothing should — KillFeedClient tracked it once and was carried across the
  screen when it moved — so changing the row's height scale or its y means moving the banner in the
  same pass. Noted in both vault entries.

  **Three signals make it read as a bounty rather than a leaderboard**, all authored and none costing
  vertical space. The portrait ring is now `(255,170,0)`, the exact `Highlight.FillColor` the server
  and BotVisualClient put on a bounty target, so the card and the figure glowing through walls are
  visibly the same thing. Gold cannot carry contrast on an orange map — measured at **2.1:1 on a
  sunlit canyon wall and 1.5:1 on bright sand, 48% of what the old white ring had** — so a black
  `UIShadow` sits behind it and the boundary that matters becomes gold-against-black at 7.4:1; colour
  for meaning, shadow for legibility. A `BOUNTY` stamp in Oswald Bold sits across the bottom of each
  portrait, resting at `TextTransparency 0.2` so the hunter still reads through it. And the cash
  moved from green to the house amber, matching every bounty string in the combat feed.

  **The word is BOUNTY because that is the game's own term** — the combat feed already says BOUNTY
  POSTED / BOUNTY CLAIMED / BOUNTY ON YOU. "WANTED" appears nowhere in either place and would have
  been a third label for one concept.

  `getTextVisibleTransparency` was added alongside the existing stroke helper, because the fade path
  assigns transparency absolutely and three elements here are deliberately non-opaque. Both seed from
  the **authored** value at registration, before any fade runs — an unseeded stroke is floored at
  0.82, which is right for the text hairlines and would have pinned the fully-opaque gold ring at 82%
  transparent for the rest of the session on its first fade.

  `Hunters.Shared.HunterMobileSprintButton` was touched **comment-only**: its 0.655 was tuned against
  the old left-edge bounty panel and that ceiling no longer exists, so there is now free band above
  the mobile SPRINT button. The value is deliberately unchanged — raising it is a tuning call, not a
  side effect of this. It is Redwater-only and **not** one of the 16 mirrored modules despite the
  folder name.

  **Verified in edit mode by measurement and screenshot** (MCP playtesting is unavailable): all four
  scripts compile, every instance path the client resolves still exists, no stray `ViewportFrame`s
  left authored under the GUI, and geometry asserted numerically across **ten viewport heights from
  320 to 1440** — 17.5% at every one, an 8.0px banner gap at every one, portraits scaling 31px to
  141px, row centred to within a pixel at 1, 2 and 3 holders, and every resting transparency proven
  to survive a show/hide cycle. Screenshot-checked on a Galaxy A06, an iPad, a 640×480 window and
  1080p through a CoreGui clone. **VERIFIED by the owner by playtest, 2026-08-05.** Unpublished.

  **Worth a look on real hardware:** at 800×360 the stamp renders around 6px tall. It reads as a word
  in the device emulator, but text metrics in this project have lied on real phones before, so that
  is the one thing the emulator cannot settle.

## Fixed

- **Vesper's Pinning Round no longer reveals enemies through walls when the enemy is a bot**
  (Redwater, `StarterPlayerScripts.BotVisualClient`). The blue aura she gets on everyone caught in
  her slow zone had not rendered against a bot since 2026-08-04, while working normally against real
  players — so it presented as "the aura is gone" in a bot playtest and nowhere else.

  **Nothing was wrong with Vesper.** `SunspikeClient` built the Highlight correctly throughout:
  right colour, right adornee, fired by the right server events. `hideServerInstance` was disabling
  every `Highlight` parented into a bot's hidden server rig **by class**. That branch was added
  alongside the bot bounty glow, to stop the server's gold glow rendering on the hidden rig as a
  jittering duplicate beside the smoothed clone — and it swept up the pinning mark, which parents
  onto the server rig but *adorns* the `ClientBotVisuals` clone. The instance was created and then
  switched off a frame later, which is why it left no error and no missing instance to find.

  It now disables only highlights that actually **draw that hidden rig**: a nil `Adornee`, which
  falls back to the Parent and is exactly what the server bounty glow does, or an `Adornee` that is
  the rig or a descendant of it. The three other writers that land a Highlight on a bot rig were
  each checked against the new rule and are unchanged — `BountyService.applyHighlight` (nil
  adornee), `BotCombat:_flash` and `HunterHitPipeline:_flashHit` (both adorn the rig itself) all
  still get switched off.

  Diagnosed against a live playtest rather than by reading: firing `PinningZoneUpdate` by hand
  produced a correctly configured highlight, and calling `PinningRoundService:_land` on a bot found
  the victim and set `StatusMarked` cleanly, which left rendering as the only remaining link.

  **Needs live verification** (MCP playtesting is unavailable): that E marks bots visibly for the
  zone's life and clears them on exit or at 6s, and that a bounty bot still shows a single gold glow
  with no jittering copy at the raw server position. A marked bot that is then shot will flicker —
  the mark and the hit flash share Roblox's one-Highlight-per-model channel, which is open item
  **#27** and deliberately not addressed here.

- **Two thirds of every `MatchStart` analytics row was `Unknown|Unknown`, and the third that
  survived was a biased sample** (Redwater, `RoundSystem.RoundController`). Found by reading the
  live Creator Hub custom-event chart on 2026-08-05, not from an error — nothing warned, because
  both fields have legitimate `"Unknown"` fallbacks.

  Measured over the last 7 days: **2,604 `Unknown|Unknown` against 1,373 resolved rows (65%)**.
  Both halves of the `Platform|Hunter` composite are join-timing dependent, and on the late-join
  path `logMatchStart` runs **inside `setupPlayer`** — before the `task.defer`'d
  `SkinService.loadCharacter` has set `SelectedHunter`, and before TelemetryClient's platform
  report (~2s after the client boots, with the profile's `LastPlatform` fallback not yet loaded
  either). Both resolve to `"Unknown"`, which is why the two always failed *together*: the small
  `Unknown|Dust` tail is the in-between case, a player who arrived during the countdown whose
  character loaded but whose platform report had not landed.

  **`Config.MIN_PLAYERS = 1` is what turned an edge case into the majority.** A reserved server
  starts its round on the first arrival after a 5s countdown, and the 90s match lock then feeds
  the remaining 6–9 players into an already-active round — so the late-join path is the *normal*
  one and only the player who seeded each server goes through the round-start loop. That made the
  hunter pick-rate chart **biased rather than merely lossy**: the surviving third is specifically
  party leaders and solo seeders, a different population from the players who fill the server.

  The counters (`MatchesPlayed`, `LastMatchAt`, `SittingMatchIndex`, the `matchStartedByUserId`
  entry) still advance **inline** — they are the completion-rate denominator and must not depend
  on whether a player stayed long enough to spawn. Only the `Telemetry.log` call moved, into a
  spawned `emitMatchStart` that polls every 0.5s for up to 10s until `SelectedHunter` and a
  non-`Unknown` platform both resolve. It is keyed on the actual signal rather than a guessed
  duration, and it **still emits on timeout and for a player already on their way out** — dropping
  one would understate completion rate for exactly the players who quit fastest, which is a worse
  distortion than an honest `Unknown` row. Both call sites benefit, so the small countdown-window
  tail closes too.

  This is the trap `startMatchFunnel` already dodges fifteen lines above, with a comment spelling
  out that a step fired before the platform is known produces a permanently `Unknown` row. The
  reasoning was applied to the funnel and missed for `MatchStart`.

  **Chart values change from the day this ships** — before and after are not comparable, and
  historical `MatchStart` breakdowns stay unusable for hunter pick rate since custom events do not
  backfill. `HunterSwitch` is unaffected and always was: it fires on apply during a mid-round
  respawn, when both fields are long since resolved. Until this ships, `MatchDeaths` broken down
  by CustomField03 is the correct pick-rate proxy — its hunter comes from `stats.hunterLabel` at
  the buzzer.

  **Verified by compile only** (MCP playtesting is unavailable): `RoundController` recompiles clean
  at 1,636 lines. **Needs live verification:** play a match and confirm new `MatchStart` rows carry
  a real `Platform|Hunter` pair, and that the `Unknown|Unknown` count stops growing.

- **The kill-cam hunter swap cards now show each hunter in the skin you have equipped for them**
  (Redwater, `PlayerServices.HunterSwitchService` + `StarterPlayerScripts.HunterSwitchClient`).
  Closes open item **#22**. Every card rendered its hunter in the default skin, so the portrait you
  picked from was visibly a different character to the one that spawned.

  **The standing note on this item was wrong about where the fix had to go, and that is the part
  worth keeping.** It assumed the equipped skin was already known client-side. It is not: the only
  skin attributes a client has are `SelectedHunterCategory` / `Subfolder` / `ModelName`, and those
  describe **the hunter you are currently playing** — one hunter, not four. (That is why the HUD
  portrait, which reads exactly those three, was always right while these four were not.) Per-hunter
  equipped skins live in the profile, which is server-only, so this could never have been a
  client-only change — the card row had no way to know what Vesper was wearing while you played Dust.

  The state push now carries a per-hunter `models` map, built from **exactly** the
  `getEquippedSkin` + `resolveSelection` pair that `applyPending` spawns from. Reusing both rather
  than re-deriving is what makes the picture and the spawn agree by construction, including the
  "equipped a skin, then stopped owning it" case, which both paths degrade to the default
  identically. It is sent as the **resolved** `{category, subfolder, modelName}` and never as a skin
  name, so the name→model rule stays solely in `HunterSelectionService` — the drift that module's
  own note exists to prevent — and the client just walks `ReplicatedStorage.Characters`.

  Client-side the rig inside each portrait's `WorldModel` is swapped and the camera re-derived from
  it, since skins differ in height and silhouette. Four properties, each load-bearing: it is **not**
  done at build time (portraits are built at card registration, before the state remote is even
  connected — so the card shows its authored default immediately and is re-dressed on the first
  push); it is **idempotent**, because `render` runs on every push and rebuilding each time would
  flicker all four portraits mid-kill-cam; it is **generation-guarded** so a later spec beats one
  still resolving; and it is **off-thread**, because resolving a skin model yields. A `Default`
  spec maps to the authored template clone rather than re-resolving through
  `Characters/<Hunter>/Default` — the template *is* the default look, and those two are not
  interchangeable in the way you would assume: Bram's authored viewport rig is named `BramDefault`
  where every other hunter's is named `Rig`. A model that fails to resolve leaves the default
  portrait standing and warns; a wrong picture beats an empty box, which is exactly the symptom of
  **#20**.

  **`Anchored` is deliberately left alone**, against the instinct from the portrait-anchoring rule.
  These cards are static — no `Animate`, no animation loaded — the authored templates are unanchored
  `AnimationConstraint` rigs that already render correctly, no physics runs inside a viewport, and
  root-only anchoring is what makes welded accessories snap to their joint-defined position instead
  of the authored one.

  **The dressing is isolated from the visibility path, and that isolation is the important part of
  this change rather than a footnote.** `render` runs from the state handler **above**
  `refreshVisibility`, and its card loop is a shared `pairs` pass — so a throw while dressing a
  portrait skips every later card *and* the visibility call, leaving the SWAP HUNTER button hidden
  for the rest of that life with nothing scheduled to look again. That is **#20's exact failure mode
  arriving by a new route**, introduced by this feature and caught by fuzzing the payload rather than
  by reading the diff: `state.models and state.models[hunter]` passes a truthy check and then
  **throws** on a non-indexable value such as a number, and the measured result was the button never
  appearing. Our own server can only ever send a table, so it was not reachable in production — but
  it is a remote payload, the file already type-checks `state` and `state.message`, and a cosmetic
  portrait must never be able to cost a player their swap window. `models` is now type-checked, and
  the `applyPortraitModel` call is `pcall`ed so no future change inside it can reach the button.

  **Verified by simulation and screenshot** (MCP playtesting is unavailable). The real shipped client
  source was recompiled with `loadstring` into a `setfenv` sandbox against the real `Characters`
  folder: 6 of 6 passed — skin applied per hunter, a push carrying **no** `models` field leaves the
  default standing without throwing, Skin→Default restores the authored rig, an unresolvable spec
  warns exactly once and keeps the default, rapid re-spec lands on the last one, and the camera
  re-frames to `PORTRAIT_DISTANCE` every swap. Re-pushing identical state rebuilt **zero** rigs.
  `resolveSelection` was sandboxed separately with `require` stubbed — so DataService/ProfileService
  are never touched, since Studio writes the live store — and all 7 authored skins across the four
  playable hunters resolve to real Models. A second suite fuzzed the `models` payload across 12
  malformed shapes (wrong type at both the map and entry level, missing fields, unknown category,
  a hunter outside the roster) and asserts on the **button** rather than the portrait: the handler
  survives, `swapFrame.Visible` still goes true, and all 4 cards still render — 12 of 12, against
  1 failure before the guards. Retiring the button still works both ways (respawn push, and the
  round ending while down). Screenshot-verified through a CoreGui clone: Vesper
  renders unmasked and Fortuna in Ivory Gambler while Dust and Bram keep their authored look, all
  four framed identically, and an all-`Default` push is pixel-identical to the previous behaviour —
  so a player with nothing equipped sees no change at all.

  **Needs live verification:** die with a non-default skin equipped on a hunter you are *not*
  currently playing and confirm that card shows it; confirm no flicker or pop while the kill cam is
  up, which is the one thing simulation can only approximate.

- **Bram's charge no longer ends in a stun against the invisible map boundary** (Redwater,
  `BramServer.BreachChargeService` + `StarterPlayerScripts.Bram.GrudgekeeperClient`). **Reported from
  playtest** as the charge stopping and stunning too easily near sloped ground.

  `workspace.WallMap` is the map boundary: **64 fully invisible slabs** (`Transparency = 1`) up to
  **241 studs tall**, collidable, with vertical faces. The wall test correctly identified them as
  walls, so a charge anywhere near the edge of the map paid a full slam — 50 damage and a 2.5s stun —
  against something the player cannot see. Measured over 3,000 charge starts on walkable ground:
  **55 of 298 charge stops (16%)** were this, now **0**.

  **Bots have always excluded `WallMap`** (`BotCombat._rayParams`), which is why one boundary produced
  two different symptoms and looked like two bugs: a bot ground against the slab until its charge
  timed out, while a player was stunned by thin air. The player-side wall test now excludes it too,
  in both the server tick/re-verify (`_wallExcludeList`) and the client's report.

  **The boundary is still respected by the clear-space clamp**, which deliberately keeps the old
  list. The slabs are solid, and writing a carried victim inside one is exactly the void-death
  failure the clamp exists to prevent. Ignore the boundary when asking "is this a wall worth slamming
  into"; respect it when asking "is this space clear".

  **The terrain slope was exonerated**, and it is worth recording so it is not re-investigated: zero
  of 16 simulated approaches and zero of 3,000 map-wide samples were stopped by terrain. Terrain
  normals on those approaches measured 0.52–0.94, comfortably on the ramp side of the `cos 55`
  threshold. The area simply slopes, and the invisible slab sits where the hillside appears to be.

- **A stalled charge now releases instead of grinding to the timeout** (Redwater,
  `StarterPlayerScripts.Bram.GrudgekeeperClient` + `Bots.BotCombat`). The other half of the same
  report. A charge blocked by something the wall test deliberately does *not* accept — the boundary
  above, a shallow ledge, a prop wedged against a slope — used to leave Bram pressed motionless
  against it for the remainder of the 3s with no way out.

  It ends as a **plain release, never a slam**: nothing was rammed, so carried victims drop for
  `ReleaseDamage` with no stun, exactly as a timeout does. **That is what makes it safe to reinstate**
  — stall detection was removed once before for causing false slams, and `StallSpeed` (10) /
  `StallGrace` (0.15) have sat unread in `GrudgekeeperConfig` ever since. Ordered *after* the wall
  check, so a genuine wall still slams.

  Measured on **displacement, not `AssemblyLinearVelocity`** (and, as of the climb-assist entry
  below, on full 3D displacement rather than the flat XZ it first used): the LinearVelocity mover (and, on the
  bot path, `DashToward`'s WalkSpeed override) keeps asserting the full charge speed while the
  character is pressed against a surface, so velocity reads ~40 studs/s with Bram not moving at all.
  How far he actually got is the only honest signal.

  Simulated against the authored speed ramp: the slowest legitimate charge speed is **24 studs/s**
  against a `StallSpeed` of 10, so it cannot fire on a healthy charge; a charge blocked at t=1.00s
  now releases at **t=1.17s** instead of grinding to 3.0s. The bot path uses a **0.3s** startup floor
  against the player's 0.15 on purpose — a player charge is at full speed on frame one, while
  `DashToward` has to resolve a path and spin the humanoid up, and without it a bot would abort its
  own charge during startup every time.

- **Bram's charge now climbs sloped ground instead of dying on it** (Redwater,
  `StarterPlayerScripts.Bram.GrudgekeeperClient` + `Hunters.Bram.Config.GrudgekeeperConfig`).
  **Reported from playtest**: charging a victim up a slope cancelled short of the wall and let them go
  for minor damage. The two entries above were the *wrong half* of that report — excluding the
  boundary stopped the false stuns, and the stall release only converted a silent 3-second grind into
  a fast release. Neither made the charge get up the hill.

  **Nothing in the charge could lift Bram at all.** It moves on a Plane-mode `LinearVelocity` driving
  X/Z with **Y left free**, so pressed into rising ground he fed the whole horizontal push into the
  contact normal and crawled. The wall test only accepts a face at `|normal.Y| <= cos 55`, so a
  **40–55° lip is in a dead band**: too steep for him to ride up, too shallow to slam. The charge
  stopped there and the carried victim dropped for `ReleaseDamage` (15) instead of `SlamDamage` (50).
  This was long-standing and is described in `BreachCarryGeometry`'s own header — it was documented as
  a *carry* hazard without anyone noticing it also ended the charge.

  `breachClimbRate` probes the **ground** `ChargeClimbProbeDistance` (4) studs ahead — not the surface
  normal underfoot, which reads "level" right up until he is already against a riser — and holds the
  upward speed that clears the measured rise in the time it takes to reach it, capped by
  `ChargeMaxUpwardSpeed`. That key was **0**, read only by a `getBreachVelocity` that nothing called;
  it is now 52 and the dead function is deleted. It only ever climbs: a nil rate, or one slower than
  the fall already in progress, leaves velocity alone, so it is not a hover and does not cancel a drop
  off a ledge.

  **The stall check was also measuring the wrong thing** and would have fought this: it took **flat
  XZ** displacement, but climbing *is* progress, and on a 55° face over half the motion is vertical —
  so it read a charge riding cleanly up a slope as more stalled the better it was climbing. It now
  measures full 3D displacement. The stall release stays as the backstop for a genuine dead end.

  **Bots were never affected, and that asymmetry is the tell.** Both `BotController:DashToward` paths
  move the **Humanoid** (`Pathing:RequestMove` / `MoveTo` plus a `HunterMovement` base) and the
  humanoid controller climbs slopes by itself — which is why the same incline stopped a player Bram
  and not a bot one. No bot code changed.

  **Verified by probe against ramps at known angles** (MCP playtesting is unavailable), running the
  shipped function body extracted from the LocalScript rather than a retyped copy: 15–50° climb at
  exactly the needed rate, peaking at **50.1 studs/s** against `MovementChecker`'s

- **A player slammed into a wall now ends flush against it, exactly where a bot does** (Redwater,
  `BramServer.BreachChargeService` + `StarterPlayerScripts.BramWorldFXClient`). **Reported from
  playtest**, and distinct from the bot-Bram item below — this is a *player* Bram slamming a *player*
  victim, and it was long-standing rather than introduced by the refactor.

  Both victim types have always computed the same settle point from the same function; only the
  application differed. A bot was written server-side and unconditionally. A player had the point
  shipped to their own client through `"Unpin"` and applied there behind a push-back-only guard —
  and when that guard said no, **the entire reseat was skipped** and the victim simply stayed wherever
  the carry loop had left them, short of the wall.

  The guard was measured against the *client's* root position, on the belief — stated in its own
  comment — that "only the victim's client knows their true position, the server's copy lags". **That
  premise inverted on 2026-08-01**, when the server took network ownership of carried players and
  began driving them; from then on the client's copy was the lagging one and the comment was never
  revisited. `_unpin` compounded it by handing ownership back *before* firing the remote, so the
  guard ran in the middle of the ownership flip. The settle never depended on Bram's lagging root
  in the first place — it comes off the raycast hit on the wall, which is absolute.

  `_resolveSlam` now writes the settle server-side for player victims too, by the same call the bot
  branch uses and positioned **above** the `_unpin` that releases ownership. `"Unpin"` is bare again
  and the client only stops its follow loop. The bot branch is byte-unchanged, since that behaviour
  was the one reported as correct. Position is identical to a bot's; **orientation deliberately is
  not** — a bot is turned to face back out of the wall, a player keeps their own yaw, because they
  are about to spend `SlamStunDuration` unable to move and an imposed facing would just stick.

  This is the refactor paying for itself: one settle, one applier, one place to fix it.

  **Needs live verification:** slam a player into a wall and confirm they end flush rather than a
  couple of studs short, and that a slammed bot is unchanged.

- **Alt no longer strands the camera after a respawn, and no longer needs pressing twice**
  (Redwater, `StarterGui.ReworkedSettingsGui.SettingsController`). Closes open items **#28** and
  **#26**, which were one defect seen from two sides: **the Alt hold's saved camera state outlived
  the character it was taken against.**

  `unlockMouse` snapshots `CameraType` / `Player.CameraMode` / `MouseBehavior` / `MouseIconEnabled`
  on the press and `lockMouse` writes all four back on the release, and nothing invalidated that
  snapshot in between — its only two call sites are the `InputBegan` / `InputEnded` pair, and the
  script's existing `CharacterAdded` connection only closes the panel. While the player is **down**
  those four values belong to `KillCamClient`, which holds the camera `Scriptable` with a free
  cursor. So a hold that started (or was still running) during a kill cam and was released after the
  respawn stamped `Scriptable` onto the live gameplay camera and unbound the follow that was driving
  it: **a Scriptable camera with nobody steering it.** The view froze in place while the character
  walked out from under it. Nothing in the place takes a Scriptable camera back mid-life — only the
  next death (`KillCamClient.restoreCamera`) or the next round start (`RoundClientController` on
  `RoundActive`) — which is exactly the reported "the only way out is to die again". The same stale
  snapshot restored into a locked life is what made Alt need pressing twice, and why it came back
  after every death.

  A hold now ends at the character boundary rather than crossing it. On `Humanoid.Died` it stands
  down and deliberately touches nothing — the kill cam claimed the camera on the death frame and
  re-asserts every frame, so the hold no longer fights it either. On `CharacterAdded` it hands the
  camera back through `applyGameplayLock`, which writes the same four values every hunter client's
  own `setGameplayMouseLocked(true)` writes. A character check in `lockMouse` is the backstop for
  any path that reaches the release first. Pressing **and** releasing Alt entirely inside the kill
  cam is unchanged: the character is the same, so the snapshot is restored exactly and the kill cam
  is undisturbed — that case is legitimate and `Scriptable` is correct there.

  **Why it never reproduced in Studio.** The bad snapshot can only be taken inside the death →
  respawn window, and live that window is seconds wide — `KILL_CAM_REMOTE_GRACE` 0.35 s, the ~1.4 s
  death fade, `RoundActive` replicating, the hunter client's own `WaitForChild("Humanoid", 8)` —
  where in Studio the whole hand-off collapses into a frame or two. The fix is therefore keyed on
  character identity, never on a delay, and the hand-back is spawned and gated on the Humanoid
  rather than run inline: on a live server `CharacterAdded` regularly beats the Humanoid's
  replication, and an inline liveness check would read false for a character about to be perfectly
  alive — skipping the hand-back is precisely what strands the camera.

  Three further hardening changes in the same block, each a way the hold could fail on its own:

  - **The cursor is freed before the camera goes `Scriptable`, not after.** PlayerModule only
    releases its `LockCenter` mouse override while its camera controller is still awake, so going
    `Scriptable` first parked the module with the override installed — after which nothing restores
    it and the per-frame `MouseBehavior = Default` write is snapped back every frame, for the whole
    hold. This is the same order `KillCamClient.claimCameraOnDeath` uses, and that script's own
    comment records it as what turns PlayerModule's centre-pin off; the Alt path had it reversed.
  - **The follow bind re-reads `workspace.CurrentCamera` each frame** instead of steering the
    instance captured at press time, which a teleport or rejoin replaces out from under it.
  - **Losing window focus ends the hold** (`WindowFocusReleased`). Alt-tabbing is a press whose
    release Roblox never delivers, which latched the hold open: the next press then early-returned
    and did nothing, and only its release re-locked — a second, independent way for Alt to look like
    it needs pressing twice.

  `applyGameplayLock` only converts `CameraType` when it is currently `Scriptable`, so it un-strands
  its own camera without taking one off a script that owns it — inside "alive and in a live round"
  every other Scriptable owner (kill cam, intermission idle camera, victory showcase) has already
  stood down. The whole change is inert unless a hold is actually in progress, and the entire Alt
  path is skipped on touch, so nothing here reaches mobile.

  **Needs live verification** (MCP playtesting is unavailable, and this one demonstrably does not
  reproduce in Studio): hold Alt through a kill-cam respawn and confirm the camera is normal on
  release; press and release Alt inside the kill cam and confirm the kill cam is undisturbed; press
  Alt once after a death and confirm the cursor frees on the first hold; Alt-tab mid-hold and
  confirm the next press works.
