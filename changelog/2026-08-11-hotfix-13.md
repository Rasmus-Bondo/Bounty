# Hotfix 13 — 2026-08-11

**Summary:** The onboarding training run ships behind a test-account gate — built end to end across
both places but reaching no players yet — alongside a hold-TAB stats panel, new first-person
animation sets for Dust and Vesper, and fixes for Fortuna's mobile double jump and the training HUD
leaking into real matches.

## Added

- **New players get a guided training run before their first real match.** A new account arriving
  in the lobby is offered TRAINING on a card over the menu; accepting reserves a private Redwater
  server and teleports them into a solo lesson with a fixed Dust loadout, and finishing it returns
  them to the lobby with a "TRAINING COMPLETE" toast and **250 Cash, once per account**. The lesson
  is ten steps on a banner — move and sprint, fire and reload, the dash, the fan, first blood
  against two bots that walk in, the ultimate against a firing line, a bounty target to hunt down,
  freeing the cursor, the stats panel, and a live-fire finish against five bots that close from
  across the canyon. Every step advances on the real signal (the server watches the actual combat
  remotes, never the input that asked for them) and every step also has a timeout, so a confused
  player is carried forward rather than stuck. The invite is offered at most twice and never to a
  player arriving from anywhere but a cold join — no party, no friend follow, no return from a
  match — and "skip for now" is permanent. **Alt-freeing the cursor is taught on every platform now**
  rather than being skipped on touch: the step's real goal is opening settings, and a tap gets there
  without a cursor to free — so the lesson is the same ten steps everywhere. The zone still closes during the lesson, compressed and
  centred on the training ground, so the wall is something they have seen before it matters. It is
  worth nothing toward quests, the battle pass, bounties or the match directory: a training server
  never publishes itself, and none of the kills in it touch progression. Small things that only
  apply inside a lesson: dying respawns you instantly back at the training ground with no kill cam
  (there is one hunter and no one worth studying), the on-screen ability labels are forced on even
  for a player who has them switched off — the steps point at those icons — and the ultimate's
  bounty stinger is muted, since the lesson claims a bounty seconds later and the two cues stacked
  into noise over the kill it most wants watched. *Built end to end in both places, then **played
  through on PC (including the path where the player ignores the tutorial), on touch and on a
  tablet** — the tuning that came out of those runs is the entry below. **Live transport is still
  untested and cannot be tested in Studio**: dispatch, the reserved server and the return teleport.
  See the standing concern in OPEN-ITEMS.*

## Changed

- **Redwater's stats panel is one screen now, and you hold TAB to see it.** It stands in for the
  default Roblox player list — which the game already hides — so it answers "who is winning" the way
  a shooter's scoreboard does: hold TAB and it is up, let go and it is gone. Your own stats sit in
  the left column and the round leaderboard in the right, both on screen at once; the two tabs you
  used to click between are gone entirely. MY STATS was tightened to fit the narrower column and the
  leaderboard rows were shortened, so a full ten-player board still fits under its heading without
  the panel taking any more of the screen than it did before. **Phones are unchanged** — the STATS
  button in the top right still taps to open and taps to close, because holding a finger on a button
  to keep the board up parks that finger on top of the board. Two things that only surface at the
  edges: alt-tabbing away mid-hold closes the panel instead of leaving it draped over the game when
  you come back, and the panel's request to the server is rate-limited now that one keypress is an
  open *and* a close. The training lesson's stats step was reworded to match, and it no longer tells
  you to hold ALT to click around a panel you can no longer click inside. *Layout verified on a small
  phone, a 4:3 tablet and desktop; the hold behaviour itself has not been playtested.*

- **The bot scripting layer grew the pieces a scripted sequence actually needs.** A bot can now be
  told at spawn to walk to a specific player and stay with them, rather than being spawned and
  handed that order a line later — which mattered because a bot runs one full tick of ordinary
  behaviour the moment it becomes active, and for a sniper that tick is a long sideways walk away
  from the person she was meant to approach. A scripted bot can also be pointed at players only, so
  two of them stop hunting each other instead of the human; peaceful bots now stay peaceful through
  Fortuna's mobility bursts, which had their own triggers outside the normal ability path and fired
  even while she was disarmed; and any bot on an authored walk holsters its jumps and drops its
  aiming pose for the duration, so a choreographed approach no longer ends on top of the scenery.
  **All of it keys off flags only a scripted sequence sets, so a normal match is unchanged.**

- **New artwork on the mobile JUMP button.** Icon only — the button itself, its size, its size
  clamps and its place in the touch cluster are all untouched. The new art sits smaller inside its
  own canvas than the old one, so the icon's inset was opened up from 0.58 to 0.84 to land the
  visible glyph at the same weight as FIRE, SPRINT, RELOAD and SCOPE beside it; at the shared 0.58
  it rendered about a quarter smaller than the smallest button in the cluster. Reviewed against a
  screenshot next to SPRINT before landing.

- **Dust has a new set of first-person animations** — shoot, Fan-The-Hammer and reload — on the
  default revolver and the HellFire skin alike. The new fan clip is longer than the one it replaced,
  and the fan burst takes its fire rate *from the animation's length* (one shot per cycle), so the
  six-shot burst would have quietly slowed from about half a second to seven tenths. Its animation
  speed was re-derived to hold the old rate exactly, and the reasoning now sits next to the number,
  because swapping that clip again moves the weapon's damage output with nothing to warn anyone. The
  reload window is unchanged at 1.5s — that animation is time-scaled to fill it, so a longer clip
  costs no time. Worth watching in future play: the reload's left-arm and finish-sound cues are
  pinned to fixed moments in that 1.5s window, so they assume the new clip keeps roughly the old
  beat structure. *Verified in play by the user.*

- **Dust's ultimate has its own animation.** It plays a draw once as the ability starts, then holds
  on its final frame for the whole lock-on phase rather than dropping back to the gun's ordinary
  idle. Each shot fired during the ultimate now replays Fan-The-Hammer — one complete fan per locked
  target, compressed to fit the gap between shots — where before it reused the plain single-shot
  animation. The left arm stays on screen for the duration, since the draw and the fan both pose two
  hands; it used to be hidden for the entire ultimate. That last part is held by a latch rather than
  a one-off show, because the ability cancels the fan and reload as it starts and *those* teardowns
  finish a few frames later — their trailing hide would otherwise blank the arm for the rest of the
  ultimate. *Verified in play by the user.*

- **The training lesson was tuned on a real PC, a real phone and a real tablet, and most of what
  changed is the banner.** The step counter ("6 / 10") is **gone on both platforms** — on a phone it
  sat directly on the crosshair, and once the Alt step ran everywhere there was nothing left for the
  count to explain. The instruction itself is now sized per form factor: on a phone the banner is
  nearly full width with a short text box, which is what keeps a long line on **one** line rather
  than letting it break in two and shrink both halves; on a tablet it takes the desktop text size
  back, because screen units are physical units on these panels and the phone size rendered the same
  small stamp of text on a screen three times the size. On touch the whole block moved up under EXIT
  TRAINING and out of the sightline, and follows that button rather than being pinned near it. EXIT
  TRAINING itself is smaller on a phone and full size on a tablet.
  - **Every step now shows a picture of the key instead of the word.** New WASD and ALT art
    completed the set, so the last typed keycap is gone. The WASD cluster is drawn so each of its
    keys matches the SHIFT key beside it — sizing the cluster as a single glyph, which is how every
    other key is sized, would have rendered its letters at half the height of everything around
    them. The row grows to fit it and shrinks again on a short windowed client so it can never sit
    on top of EXIT TRAINING.
  - **On touch the button a step is asking for expands**, rather than only lighting up — SPRINT,
    FIRE, RELOAD and STATS, and FIRE a second time during the ultimate, once the ultimate has
    actually started, so the player learns to press F *then* fire rather than both at once. The
    buttons grow outward in every direction like the ability icons do, and the HUD's ability row and
    the touch cluster no longer get shoved around by the pulse — the layout now measures those
    buttons at rest instead of mid-animation. **That last part touches shared combat layout code and
    is inert outside the lesson**, since nothing else animates those buttons.
  - **Completing a step flashes the same green edge-pulse as picking up a health pack.** The effect
    became a shared module rather than a copy so the two can overlap cleanly — a health pack picked
    up mid-step is a real collision. Health pack feedback is unchanged for players.
  - **The two bots on the first-blood step walk to the player** instead of to a fixed mark, the way
    the bounty target already did. The mark was chosen before anyone knew where the player would be
    standing, so the pair read as marching to a spot rather than coming for the person shooting at
    them. They still stop and wait to be shot once they arrive.
  - **The kill feed and the combat reward feed are silent for the whole lesson.** It kills eight or
    more bots and every one of them was stacking rows into the corner the banner and EXIT TRAINING
    already share, while the player is being taught one thing at a time. Both resume on any normal
    server with nothing to tell them the lesson ended.
  - Smaller: the bounty step's second line now clears itself after four seconds so it stops covering
    the marker floating over the target's head, which is the thing that line is pointing at.

- **Vesper has a new set of first-person animations, plus two poses she never had at all.** Shoot
  and reload were replaced; **sprint and ultimate are new** — sprinting used to change only her speed
  and field of view, and Starfall Sight had no stance of its own. The new shoot clip **merges the
  shot and the bolt-cycle**, which were two chained animations before, so the rifle cycles in one
  motion instead of two stitched together; the old cycle clip is no longer played by anything. Its
  sound moved with it — the bolt-cycle layer used to start exactly when the second animation did, and
  now fires at a fixed point in the shot window instead, which is the one number here worth an ear
  check if it ever drifts off the visible cycle. The reload clip is a near-exact fit for the existing
  3s reload so it runs at its authored speed, while the shot clip is stretched to fill the 1.8s
  between shots and therefore plays about a quarter slower than authored — if that ever reads as
  sluggish, the fix is the fire rate, not the art. The ultimate pose is held for the whole Starfall
  window but **deliberately yields to the scope**: it reads while she is between empowered shots and
  gives way the instant she aims down sights, because keeping ADS correct was judged worth more than
  holding the stance. The practical effect is that the ult pose is a narrow slice of a window she
  spends mostly scoped — expected, not a bug. *Verified in play by the user.*

- **The training run is gated to a single test account this release — no player will be offered it,
  including genuinely new ones.** Onboarding is built but has never been through its sign-off
  checklist, and the one part that cannot be tested in Studio at all is the live transport: the
  dispatch, the reserved server and the return teleport. Shipping it to first-time players in order
  to find out whether that works is the wrong order, so the invite is restricted to an allowlist by
  account id while the whole path is driven end to end on live. Two deliberate asymmetries while
  the gate is on: a listed account is offered the lesson on **every** cold join rather than once, so
  a run can be repeated without wiping a profile — but it is still not offered on an arrival
  carrying teleport data, because the return leg out of the lesson carries its own, and re-offering
  there would stack the invite over the completion toast and loop straight back into another
  lesson. Opening onboarding to everyone is one switch, and flipping it restores the designed
  once-only rules for the listed accounts too. Redwater's Studio-only "force this server to be a
  training server" switch was turned **off** in the same pass: it is ignored on a live server, but
  it warned on every boot and would have converted every server in any environment that did not
  report itself as live.

## Fixed

- **The training banner and its EXIT TRAINING button could appear during a real match.** Dying in
  an ordinary round left a dark bar across the screen with a live EXIT TRAINING button above it.
  The training overlay ships in every Redwater server — the lesson and a real match are the same
  place — and is authored switched off. The round's HUD sweep restores every interface element when
  play becomes visible again, and its rule for an element it holds no record of hiding is to
  **show** it; nothing ever hides the training overlay, so it had no record, and the server re-runs
  that restore on every respawn. An overlay authored as hidden was therefore only hidden until the
  player first died. The sweep now leaves it alone entirely, in both directions — which also
  protects the lesson itself, where the banner has to survive the instant respawns training uses.
- **A training session could hand the player the wrong hunter, making the lesson impossible to
  finish.** The lesson is built entirely around Dust and every step watches for Dust's own actions,
  so a player who arrives as anyone else can press the right key all day and never advance, with
  nothing on screen or in the log to explain why. The only thing making the session Dust was the
  loadout carried in the teleport out of the lobby, so anyone reaching that server without it — by
  rejoining it directly, for instance — spawned as whatever they had equipped instead. A training
  server now pins Dust server-side regardless of how the player got there. This also stops the
  Studio-only "spawn as this hunter" testing switch from overriding the lesson, which is how it
  surfaced; that switch still governs every ordinary Studio round.
- **Dust's new smooth arm meshes left his off-hand on screen for the whole match.** The reworked arm
  art adds a second, smoother mesh **alongside** each original blocky arm rather than replacing it,
  and the code that hides his left hand only ever knew about the old one — so the left arm was
  visible permanently instead of only during a reload, during Fan-The-Hammer and for the ultimate.
  Nothing was misbehaving: it hid exactly the part it was told to, and that part was sitting invisibly
  inside the new mesh. Both now fade together on every path that shows or hides the arm, so the smooth
  arm follows precisely the rule the old one always did. *Verified in play by the user.* Two bits of
  harmless leftover came out of this and are worth a tidy-up someday: the old blocky **right** arm is
  still drawn inside the new right mesh, and Vesper's superseded bolt-cycle animation is still
  parented under her viewmodel with nothing loading it.
- **Fortuna can double jump on mobile again.** Her second jump had been unreachable by touch since
  the custom on-screen JUMP button shipped: that button is a plain GUI button, and it hides Roblox's
  built-in touch jump button, which was the only thing on a phone raising the input her air jump
  listened for. A press therefore reached only the root jump handler, whose gate requires the player
  to be standing on the ground — right for the other three hunters, wrong by definition for a second
  jump. Her ground jump kept working the whole time, which is why this read as "double jump broken"
  rather than "no jump input". The button now fans presses out to subscribers alongside its own
  handler and Fortuna listens on that; jump input ownership did not move, so every ability movement
  lock is still honoured through the same single gate. *Verified on device by the user.*
- **Mobile auto-fire no longer shoots a Fortuna who is invisible during her ultimate** — players and
  bots both. Aim assist had no notion of stealth at all, so an invisible rig stayed an ordinary
  acquire candidate and the lock tracked her for the whole window, which is the one thing that
  ultimate exists to prevent. The check is the same one the bot AI has always obeyed, and it is
  applied where targets are picked rather than where shots are taken, so it also stops the camera
  magnetism drifting toward someone nobody can see. Covers all four hunters through the one shared
  module. An invisible Fortuna still blocks line of sight to whoever is behind her, because she
  still absorbs a bullet aimed through her. *Not yet verified in play — needs a mobile playtest
  against a Fortuna ultimate.*
- **The HellFire revolver skin was animating with an older set of clips than the default gun.** Its
  viewmodel had been forked at some point and never caught up with the last animation update, so a
  player using the skin saw different shoot, fan and reload motions from everyone else — no error,
  no warning, just quietly the wrong art for anyone who had bought it. Both viewmodels are on the
  same set now, and the new animations went onto both in the same pass. *Its reload clip had drifted
  the same way and is also corrected.*
