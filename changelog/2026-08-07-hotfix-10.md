# Hotfix 10 — 2026-08-07

**Summary:** Mobile players now fire by holding the crosshair on an enemy, behind a new Auto Fire
settings toggle — plus a mirrored animation loader that turns silent clip-download failures into
real, retried, reported ones, and a combat reward feed moved off the crosshair.

## Added

- **Mobile auto-fire** (Redwater, all four hunters): touch players now fire by putting the
  crosshair on an enemy and keeping it there — the weapon fires on its own cadence while the lock
  holds, with a 0.08s acquire dwell so sweeping the camera past enemies doesn't waste shots and a
  0.12s loss grace so a strafe behind a thin post doesn't drop the burst. One new shared module,
  `ReplicatedStorage.Hunters.Shared.MobileAutoFireController`, is consumed by all four hunter
  clients (`FPSRevolverClient`, `SunspikeClient`, `GrudgekeeperClient`, `CardsClient`); the lock
  itself is computed inside `AimAssistController` (new `GetLock()` report — apparent-size
  tolerance `atan(1.6 studs / distance)`, 20° point-blank ceiling, no minimum angle) against the
  same rendered geometry the camera magnetism uses, so arena bots lock at the position they are
  rendered. Per-hunter gates: Dust holds during High Noon; Vesper's scoped shots wait for the
  full Deadeye Focus ramp (hipfire unaffected); Bram holds during Breach Charge; Fortuna holds
  during ultimate/Ability1 casts and auto-fire calls `useBasic()` directly, leaving her FIRE
  button's hold state machine untouched. Two hunters carry a range cap (a new optional
  `MaxLockDistance` on the controller — a lock farther away reads as no lock): Bram never
  auto-fires past **60 studs** (`GrudgekeeperConfig.Basic.AutoFireMaxDistance`, set equal to his
  blast's max range so only literally-zero-damage shots are blocked — it started at 50 and was
  raised to 60 the same day at the user's call)
  and Fortuna never past her card's max range (`CardsConfig.Basic.Range`, which `CardsClient` now
  requires — her card raycast is hard-capped there, so a farther lock would burn the 3-card hand
  into a reload for zero hits; **150 studs** since the range buff below, and the cap tracks the
  config by construction). The FIRE button stays unrestricted for deliberate long pokes on
  both. Dust and Vesper are uncapped: within the lock's 200-stud acquisition ceiling Dust always
  deals at least his 20/40 falloff floor and Vesper's falloff hasn't even started (240+), so
  neither can waste a shot. Controllers are destroyed on mid-match hunter switch.
  The FIRE button is unchanged and remains the only way to shoot with auto-fire off. The server
  is untouched and stays authoritative on every cooldown.
- **Auto Fire settings toggle** (both places): new `INPUT` section in the settings panel, between
  CROSSHAIR and HUD, holding an `Auto Fire` toggle — default ON, persisted on the shared profile,
  row and header hidden on non-touch devices. The value reaches the hunter clients as an
  `AutoFireEnabled` player attribute (absent = ON, so the first seconds of a match behave before
  the settings payload lands). The rows are authored instances cloned from the existing
  `ShowPingFPSRow` / `HUDHeader`, so styling cannot drift.
- ~~Crosshair lock ring~~ — built and then removed the same day at the user's request; auto-fire
  ships with no lock indicator. The hunter clients still publish the `AutoFireLocked` player
  attribute (currently unconsumed) as the hook if lock feedback returns; `HitMarkScript` and
  `GunGui` are back to their pre-ring state.

## Changed

  100 → 150 — flat 24/42 damage across the whole reach, since the card has no falloff. Ability1's

- **The intermission countdown now pops on each tick** (Redwater): "NEXT MATCH IN: n" expands ~10%
  and settles back with a slight overshoot as it counts down, so the number reads as counting
  rather than sitting still. Deliberately subtle — it is ambient motion under a stats screen.
  Two properties are driven together (the label's `UIScale` and its `MaxTextSize` cap) because

- **The lobby no longer floods the Creator Hub with "Infinite yield possible" on every slow join**
  (Bounty). A Studio playtest logged eight of them at once — `CameraFolder`, `SkinMenu`, `PlayModel`
  — from `LoadingScreen`, `CollectionClient`, `ReworkedUIPlay`, `MenuHandlerScript`,
  `FeaturedSequence`, `HuntersBuyController` and `Refs`, plus one from Roblox's own stock `Animate`
  script. Nothing was broken: all four Workspace targets exist and are `Persistent`, and every
  warning fired within 1ms of the others, which is a boot-time replication burst crossing the 5s
  threshold rather than a bad reference. **Studio replicates a local place far faster than a real
  client pulls one over the network, so a wait that clears in a playtest crosses that threshold
  routinely in production** — and warnings are listed on the Creator Hub, where volume buries real
  errors.

  New Bounty-only `ReplicatedStorage.Modules.WaitForRequired` — a single function that loops
  `WaitForChild(name, 30)` instead of calling it untimed. Passing any timeout suppresses the engine
  warning (verified: an untimed wait on a missing name warns at 5s, a 30s-timed wait on the same
  name stays silent while outstanding), so the semantics every call site already relies on are
  preserved exactly — **block until it arrives, never return nil** — while the report moves to 30s,
  where it means something, and names the full path instead of leaving you a stack trace.

  Fifteen client call sites across nine scripts converted — the seven above plus
  `TradingPostController` and `ReworkedPartyClient`, which had not yet been slow enough to warn —
  along with `LoadingScreen`'s `CameraPart` child wait, which goes through it defensively because

  alone: they arrive with their parent and never warn. Two are
  **deliberately left untimed**: `LobbyService`'s `JailSpawn` because on the server there is no

  rather than at the top, since `ReplicatedFirst` runs before ReplicatedStorage is guaranteed to
  have replicated — by then the veil is up and the camera seized, so the wait sits behind the
  loading screen. Adding a *bounded* timeout was rejected: with no fallback available for any of
  these, it would convert a slow load into a nil-index crash, which is what `ReworkedUIPlay`'s

  Diagnostic value is preserved rather than muted — `TradingPostController`'s `PersistentMarkers`

  just at 30s. Also fixed in passing: `FeaturedSequence.md` listed its caller as a `Required by:`
  **wikilink**, the backwards-edge trap `CLAUDE.md` documents and names that file as still carrying.
  The graph had `featuredsequence → huntersbuycontroller`; it now reads the correct direction only.

## Fixed

- **The combat reward text no longer sits on the crosshair, and its dark background now fits the
  words** (Redwater, mobile and PC). Reported from a Galaxy S25 Ultra: the "ELIMINATION +250 XP"
  text landed squarely over the aim point, and the black banner behind it ran roughly twice the
  width of the text on both platforms. Measured on the reporter's device (685x338 safe area) the
  newest row spanned y 108–136 against a crosshair centre of y 121.5.

  The rows now run in a lane to the **right** of the crosshair, left-aligned and offset by 0.045 of
  screen width — a fraction of width rather than a constant, because the crosshair scales with width
  too (`GunGui`'s Canvas is 0.065 of width on `RelativeXX`). **Sideways rather than upward was the
  load-bearing choice:** the band above the crosshair is only ~110px on a phone once the 58px round
  timer is paid for, and `KillCamClient` already claims it for the "KILLED BY" card via the
  `FeedBaseYScale` attribute. Raising the feed would have traded one bug for a new collision.

  The banner was `UIShadow` with `BlurRadius = UDim.new(0.5, 0)` — a **scale** radius, taken against
  the element's own size — on a label whose box was a flat 0.78 of the 680px row, so it blurred 530px
  of box holding 258px of text. It is now a fixed 14px radius on a box measured per row with
  `GetTextBoundsAsync`. **`KillFeedClient` carries the identical `UIShadow` bug** and works around it
  by narrowing its rows; that is the precedent, and it is still there.

  Three further defects surfaced while fixing it and are fixed too. The shadow was **clipped on
  three sides**, not just the reported left one — a `CanvasGroup` renders children into a texture of
  its own `AbsoluteSize`, and the label sat flush at x=0 in a canvas exactly `ROW_HEIGHT` tall, so
  only the right edge had canvas to fade into; the canvas now carries an 18px margin that is
  subtracted back out **scaled**, or rows stagger as they shrink. On PC the stack grew *down* from a
  fixed 0.565 base to ~0.79 and **covered the equipped weapon**, the viewmodel being on the same side
  as the lane; the base is now solved backwards from the stack's bottom edge (0.58) so a taller stack
  starts higher. And the five hardcoded per-device profiles are replaced by a curve —
  `(height/1080)^0.7`, clamped 0.40–1.15 — with row step, row count and base-Y all derived from it.
  They jumped 0.64→1.12 across a one-pixel viewport change and left phones at 20.5px of text, 6.1% of
  a 338px screen against desktop's 3.0%; mobile is now 14.2px (4.2%) and 1080p is unchanged at 32px.

  **Two truncation traps, both invisible at desktop scale.** A fit allowance has to be
  *proportional*: bounds are measured at `TEXT_SIZE` but the row renders through a `UIScale`, and
  scaled glyph layout rounds up per glyph (258px renders 133 wide at 0.5x, not 129), so a flat 3px
  slack that was ample at scale 1.0 still ellipsised `+$120` at 0.44. And a box sized to fit its own
  text must never be *allowed* to truncate — `TextTruncate` is now enabled only for lines that
  genuinely overran the lane, so an underestimate costs the last glyph's final column rather than the
  whole reward. Also: **`TextBounds` is reported in rendered pixels while `Size` is unscaled**, and
  mixing them collapsed a clamped bounty row to `BOUNTY…`.

  The lane's right bound reads the live `MobileFireButton` position rather than a per-device table,
  because `HunterMobileFireButton` resolves its own edge inset at runtime against the ability HUD. A
  hard floor keeps the ordinary reward row from ever truncating whatever the lane says; on a
  1.78-aspect phone that puts it nearer the FIRE button, which was chosen deliberately over shipping
  the commonest line in the game with an ellipsis. **Still truncated on a phone: the second name in a
  bounty announcement** — the lane is 188px and two names need ~624px, so nothing beside the
  crosshair can show it whole (improved from `BOUNTY CLAIMED …` to
  `BOUNTY CLAIMED RUSTYJAKE TOOK DOWN…`). Owner-tested on mobile and PC. Tracked as launch issue #31.

- **A failed animation download now reports as a failure instead of a default pose** (both places).
  A 2026-08-07 two-client Studio playtest logged `Failed to load animation with sanitized ID
  rbxassetid://123958453269998 (x2)` and Dust's world revolver hold pose was missing. The asset is
  fine — `GetProductInfo` resolves it, it belongs to the group, and both it and the hold clip fetch
  successfully; the URL carried `serverplaceid=0` and the `(x2)` matched the two clients, so it was
  a transient CDN fetch failure.

  **The real bug was that nothing could detect it.** `Animator:LoadAnimation` returns a track
  *immediately, without downloading the clip* — the fetch resolves asynchronously afterwards. So
  `pcall(function() return animator:LoadAnimation(anim) end)` returns success and a valid track for
  a clip that never arrives. Two consequences: the failure was completely silent, and every retry
  loop that tested a `playXxx()` helper's return value **exited on pass 1 on a false success** and
  never retried. That is what left Dust standing in his default pose for the rest of the life.
  `ContentProvider:PreloadAsync(instances, callback)` blocks until the fetch resolves and reports a
  real `Enum.AssetFetchStatus` — the only mechanism that separates the two cases, and it was checked
  nowhere in either place before this.

  New mirrored `ReplicatedStorage.Modules.AnimationLoader` wraps it: `load()` blocks and is
  authoritative (nil track unless `Success`), `loadCached()` never yields, `prewarm()` warms a set
  off-thread, and statuses cache per id so the fetch is paid once per clip per session. Thirteen
  call sites were converted — Redwater: `HunterWorldModel` (covers the Fortuna, Vesper and Bram
  world guns), `WorldRevolverAimIdleClient`, `FortunaCardsWorldIdleClient`, `BramWorldIdleClient`,
  `HunterPortraitClient`, `BotController`; Bounty: `LobbySpawn`, `CollectionClient.PreviewModel`,
  `CollectionClient.ViewportIcon`, `BattlePassClient`, `CollectionGuiController.Viewport`,
  `EmoteBuyController`, `HuntersBuyController`. Bram's watchdog was already shaped like a retry loop
  and starts working as one now that the false success is gone.

  Two rules govern which entry point a call site gets, and both are about **not trading a silent
  bug for a stall**. `load()` yields, so anything that can be superseded while parked re-checks its
  own token or `model.Parent` afterwards and discards the track if it lost the race — every
  converted site does. And a path that runs per shot or per tile never blocks: `PlayOneShot` and
  `Viewport.playEmoteAnim` use `loadCached` (a different clip per shot / per tile, so there is no
  single id to warm), `BotController` uses `loadCached` behind a server-start prewarm of every bot
  clip because it runs inside the constructor *after* `BotService` has parented the rig into
  Workspace, and the grid builders (`LobbySpawn`, `BattlePassClient`, `EmoteBuyController`) prewarm
  their whole set in one call so N tiles cost one fetch round-trip rather than N.

  **The client half — which is where the original failure actually was.** `HunterWorldModel` and
  `BotController` run on the server, so their guard covers the server's own fetch and nothing else:
  a server-played track replicates to each client as a bare asset id, and every client then
  downloads the clip itself, which the server never learns about. The same holds for a clip played
  by another *player's* client, since a character's network owner plays the track and it reaches
  everyone else as an id. That is exactly what the log showed — the error appeared `(x2)`, once per
  connected client, never on the server.

  New ungated `StarterPlayerScripts.WorldAnimationPrewarmClient` (Redwater) closes it. It warms the
  **12 hunter world-gun clips** at script start — Dust's hold and shoot, plus every authored
  `Animation` in Fortuna's, Vesper's and Bram's world folders, all of them custom group uploads and
  so the ones a CDN blip actually costs — and the **48 bot clips** on a second tier deferred until
  the local character exists plus a 3s cushion, so ~50 fetches do not contend with map streaming and
  the viewmodel during the join burst. Anything short of `Success` is retried at 5s and 15s, then
  **warned once at 45s naming the ids**, which is the whole point of this change carried onto the
  client: a clip that never arrives stops being silent there too. Nothing downstream depends on the
  timing — a replicated track fetches on its own the moment it arrives, and prewarming only pays
  that cost early, which is what makes the deferred tier free. Bot clips belong in it for the same
  reason players' do: `BotVisualClient` renders a bot from a clone whose Motor6Ds are copied off the
  server rig each frame, and that server rig is animated on this client by this client's own
  Animator.

  It sits at the **root** of `StarterPlayerScripts`, ungated, because what it warms is what you see
  on *other* people — every viewer needs it whatever their own hunter is, and `HunterClientLoader`
  would disable it inside a hunter folder. Dust's hold clip was already prewarmed in
  `WorldRevolverAimIdleClient`, but that script returns early unless the local player *is* Dust, so
  it warmed the clip on the one client that never needed to fetch it and on none of the clients that
  did. His two ids are string literals in the new script because his `WorldAnimations` folder is
  authored empty — a fourth copy of ids that already live in `RevolverWorldModelServer`,
  `WorldRevolverAimIdleClient` and `BotController.getWorldAnimationId`. Change one, change all four.

  **Extended the same day to the first-person viewmodel and the showcase emotes**, which were the
  two client-fetched sets the tiers above still missed. Every viewmodel track is built by a raw
  `animator:LoadAnimation` — in `RevolverViewModel.LoadTrack`, `CardsClient`, `GrudgekeeperClient`
  and `VesperViewModelAnimationClient` — so none of them can tell a clip that arrived from one that
  never will, and what the player gets is **their own gun with no recoil, no reload and no idle
  sway, and a clean log**. `VesperViewModelAnimationClient` was the only one preloading its set at
  all, and only at bind time, which pays the cost at the exact moment of need. The showcase emote at
  `VictoryShowcaseClient.playVictoryAnimation` is the same raw pattern inside a `pcall` that by
  construction cannot see a fetch failure.

  Two new tiers, ordered by deadline. The local player's own viewmodel warms as soon as
  `SelectedHunter` resolves (via `HunterGate`), because those clips are wanted the instant the
  character spawns; every hunter's set plus the whole 18-emote catalog then warms behind the bot
  tier, since a hunter switch is offered from the kill cam and the showcase not until the buzzer. A
  mid-match switch re-warms the new hunter's set, which is near-free because `prewarm` skips any id
  already fetched. **20 distinct viewmodel clips and 18 emote ids**, verified by resolving them
  live.

  Nothing here is a hardcoded path: hunters are discovered by scanning `ReplicatedStorage.Hunters`
  for a `Config.Manifest` (which drops `Shared` on its own and picks up a fifth hunter for free),
  each viewmodel resolves through that manifest's `Client.ViewmodelTemplate`, and the emote ids come
  from `EmoteConfig`, whose catalog is the only place they exist before the match-end payload
  arrives. Skins are taken from a **sibling** folder named `Skins`, copying what
  `RevolverViewModel.resolveViewModelTemplate` and `CardsClient` already do — and that rule is
  load-bearing, because the data model holds five dead viewmodel sets that a broader walk would have
  swept up: `RevolverDefaultViewmodel.Backup`, `SniperDefaultViewmodel.Backup`,
  `Grudgekeeper.ArmsViewModelMCP`, `BackupViewmodel`, and `Hunters.Dust.Viewmodel.Skin.HellFire` —
  the last dead twice over, since Dust's manifest points at top-level `RevolverDefaultViewmodel` and
  the resolver looks for `Skins` where that folder is named `Skin`. It carries **different**
  Reload/Shoot ids from the live `RevolverDefaultViewmodel.Skins.HellFire`, so warming the wrong one
  would have looked like it worked. Exclusion of all five was asserted in the verification pass.

  **Owner-verified 2026-08-07 on mobile and PC, all four hunters.** What that confirms is the
  absence of harm — every hunter's own gun animating, no new warns, no join-time hitch from the
  deferred sweeps. It cannot confirm the retry-and-warn path, because this code only does anything
  when a fetch fails and that is a transient CDN condition nobody can induce on demand; see
  `OPEN-ITEMS.md`.

- **Fortuna's Prestige Card was invisible to everyone else** (Redwater). A player who equipped it
  saw it in first person, threw it, and stuck it in walls — but every other player in the match saw
  her holding the default cards, because `CardsWorldVisualServer` handed
  `HunterWorldModel` a hardcoded `WorldCards` template and never read
  `EquippedWeaponSkin_Fortuna`. The skin was wired everywhere else (viewmodel, thrown card, landed
  glow tint, lobby display); the third-person prop was the one path missed when the art landed. It
  now resolves per-skin the way Dust's revolver skins do, falling back to the default cards for an
  unlisted skin or missing art. Verified in a two-client server test.

  A card skin also needs its own **hand offset**, which is not just an axis correction and is the
  part worth remembering: the prop is placed with `Model:PivotTo`, and a model's pivot is
  `PrimaryPart.CFrame * PrimaryPart.PivotOffset` — *not* the part's CFrame. The two card models
  carry different baked `PivotOffset`s on their root meshes on top of being authored on different
  axes, so the offset tuned for the default fan drops a second model somewhere unrelated, and a
  correction derived by comparing the models' root parts is computed in the wrong frame (that was
  the first attempt, and it put the prestige fan in sideways). Offsets are now solved for per skin
  rather than eyeballed, and the shipped value lands the three prestige cards within 0.06 studs of
  the default fan. Re-derive if either model is re-exported — a re-export can change `PivotOffset`
  without changing anything visible.

- **The match summary's RETURN TO BOUNTY button was below the minimum touch size on phones.** At
  its old size it drew 36.5px tall on a 360px-tall viewport, against the 44px minimum — it is 0.52
  of a canvas that is only 0.2 of the viewport, and the mobile UI scale then takes another 10% off.
  Making the button smaller would have made that worse, so it now carries a `TouchFloor`
  `UISizeConstraint` and measures 82x44 there. The floor sits on the **canvas, not the button**
  (per `UI_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §4.5b — flooring the button grows it inside a container that has not
  grown with it), and its `MinSize` is pre-divided by the responsive UI scale, because a
  `UISizeConstraint` is evaluated *before* an ancestor `UIScale`, not after: a flat 44 lands at
  39.6 on exactly the phones the floor exists for. Inert on every desktop and tablet viewport.
  The ordering rule is now recorded in `UI_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §4.5b.
- **Bot muzzle flashes, tracers and rig movement no longer break up for the first seconds after a
  player joins.** The dropped-client-event warnings that Hotfix (2026-08-01) took on were still
  live on the error dashboard at Redwater r2244, on `Hunters.Shared.Remotes.BotShotFX` and
  `BotTransformUpdate`. Both are `UnreliableRemoteEvent`s, which have no invocation queue and
  discard on arrival when the receiving client has no handler attached yet.

  Hotfix drained both remotes from `ReplicatedFirst` as early as a client possibly can. That
  narrowed the window but could never close it, because **a client cannot attach a handler to an
  instance it has not received yet** — the drain has to `WaitForChild` its way down a
  7,000-descendant `ReplicatedStorage` to reach them, which on a slow connection is seconds.
  `BotService` was meanwhile broadcasting transforms via `FireAllClients` at 10-20Hz from the
  instant a player entered the server's player list, which happens far earlier. A client-side
  drain was structurally the wrong half of the problem; the gate had to move to the send side.

  New `ServerScriptService.Bots.BotFXAudience` owns a `BotFXReady` handshake:
  `ReplicatedFirst.EarlyShotRemoteDrain` fires it once both bot handlers are attached, and until it
  arrives the server sends neither remote to that player. `BotService`'s broadcast is now a per-player loop rather than
  `FireAllClients`, and `BotCombat.fireBotShotFX` skips unready players — its pre-existing
  `player.Character` test was never a load gate, since the server sets `Character` when it spawns
  the rig regardless of what that client has replicated.

  A gated player loses nothing visible: `BotVisualClient` builds its visuals from the `ArenaBot`
  tag rather than from these events, and the next transform batch lands within 100ms of the client
  going ready. The gate fails **open** in both directions — a 10s per-player timeout, and a
  module-wide flag that stays off if the listener could not install at all — so every failure path
  degrades to the old behaviour (a few discarded events at join) rather than a player who never
  sees a bot move for a whole round. The self-start is `pcall`ed for the same reason: a throw would
  propagate out of `require()` and take `BotCombat` and `BotService` with it, breaking bots
  entirely to fix a log warning. `BotUltimateFX` and `BotFortunaCardFX` stay reliable and ungated,
  for the paired-state reason recorded in the Hotfix entry. Confirmed fixed in a live playtest.

- **Two "Infinite yield possible" warnings at join are gone** (Redwater). The quest-complete toast
  and the mid-match hunter-swap panel each warned on their `PlayerGui` lookup on any slow join —
  routinely in Studio, where the profile read hits the live DataStore.

  Both were false positives on a real timing gap, not hangs. Redwater runs
  `Players.CharacterAutoLoads = false`, and StarterGui is copied into `PlayerGui` only when the
  character **first** spawns — a spawn that is itself deferred through `SkinService.loadCharacter`,
  which blocks on the profile-backed hunter selection before it calls `LoadCharacter`. Both scripts
  boot from `StarterPlayerScripts` the instant the client starts, so they sat on an empty
  `PlayerGui` for that entire window and crossed the 5s threshold. `QuestToastClient` and
  `HunterSwitchClient` now gate on `player.CharacterAdded:Wait()` before the lookup, which removes
  the false positive while leaving the `WaitForChild` after it as a real diagnostic — a timeout
  argument would have silenced it by returning `nil` into code that then indexes it.

  Deferring each script's whole boot with the gate is safe, and for different reasons worth
  keeping straight. The toast's two senders (`QuestService`, `BattlePassService`) fire off quest
  *progress* — kills, damage, wins — none of which can happen before the player has a character.
  The swap panel is death-gated, so it cannot be wanted before the first spawn either; its
  `CharacterAdded` handler would have missed that first character regardless, which is exactly
  what the `if player.Character then watchCharacter(...)` fallback at the bottom of the script
  already covers, and its priming startup query re-pulls roster/ownership so a state push landing
  during the wait is re-asked for rather than lost.

  Two related things checked and deliberately **not** changed. `VictoryShowcaseClient` and
  `VesperScopeController` pass timeouts to the same call, which suppresses the warning by handing
  back `nil` — left alone here, but they are the same latent shape. And the
  `CoreGui.RobloxGui:WaitForChild("Modules")` warning seen alongside these is **not ours**: zero
  references to `RobloxGui` across all 292 scripts in the place, no vendored `PlayerModule`, and
  in edit mode `CoreGui.RobloxGui` has one child (`ControlFrame`) and no `Modules` at all. It comes
  from Roblox's own CoreScripts or a Studio plugin. Verified by compile only — no playtest.

- **The ultimate-ready glow washed over the ability icon on phones** (Redwater, all four hunters).
  At full charge the gold light filled the ult tile's face and pooled inside the icon's negative
  space, so it read as sitting *in front of* the glyph instead of behind the tile.

  **The layering was never wrong.** The desktop and mobile HUD trees are identical on ZIndex —
  `ReadyGlow` 0, its 24 `UIShadow` layers at −1, then `CooldownBar` 1, `Background` 2, `Ability` 3,
  `ZIndexBehavior = Sibling` on both — and the glow was behind everything the whole time. What
  differs is the surface in front of it: `HunterGuiMobile` authors **every** tile fill at
  `BackgroundTransparency = 0.45` so the map reads through the HUD, where the desktop tree authors
  the same fills at `0`. A `UIShadow` is a *filled* dilation, so the stack covers the tile's own
  footprint as well as the halo around it; desktop's opaque fill swallowed that interior and
  mobile's let 55% of it straight through. Worth stating because the symptom points squarely at a
  render-order bug and the cause is a translucency one.

  `UltimateReadyFX` now masks the interior: the layers render behind their own parent, so it gives
  `ReadyGlow` an opaque body while lit — tinted from `CooldownBar` so the masked interior is the
  tile's own dark rather than a second grey — and restores the authored transparency on extinguish
  and on `Destroy`. The mask sits on `ReadyGlow` rather than on the charge bar for two reasons:
  `HunterHudCooldown` owns the bar's properties, and the bar is only full-height at full charge, so
  masking there would be correct in exactly one state. It applies on every device and is a provable
  no-op on desktop, where the opaque fill already hides it. One shared module and one HUD shape, so
  all four hunters pick it up.

  Consequence accepted rather than worked around: the mobile ready tile now goes solid dark while
  its translucent ability neighbours do not. That is exactly the desktop look, and it is the
  *charged* tile. Reproduced and verified by rendering the mobile tree at
  samsung_galaxy_s25_ultra/landscape before and after, plus a desktop render to confirm no change
  there; owner-verified in a playtest. `SetReady(false)` and `Destroy()` were checked to restore
  every captured value exactly, mask included.
