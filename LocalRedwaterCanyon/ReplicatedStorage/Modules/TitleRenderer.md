# TitleRenderer

Applies a cosmetic title's text **and** its visual style to a TextLabel. Every surface that shows a title calls this instead of assigning `.Text` directly.

## What it does
1. `apply(label, text, titleId)` sets the label's text and builds its style: accent colour, a UIGradient, a tight glyph-edge UIStroke, a halo, and — depending on the preset — a haze, particles, a specular rake or a storm flash. `titleId` is optional; when nil the style is resolved by reverse lookup from the display text, so styling still works before the id attribute replicates
2. Style selection: [[TitlesConfig]] entry → `style` name → [[TitleStyles]] preset, with the entry's `color` as the accent. Both the id and reverse-label maps are built off `TitlesConfig.EntryById`, which flattens every hunter's list, so a battle-pass title styles exactly like a bought one
3. `clear(label)` destroys the effect children and restores the label's authored colour. `apply` calls it first, and re-applying the same text+id is a no-op, so attribute-change handlers can call it freely without rebuilding the effect
4. Effect instances are always **children** of the label, never siblings, so the renderer can't disturb a caller's UIListLayout (the lobby nametag and TITLES preview both use one). Everything except the gradient and the tight stroke lives under a single `TitleFX` container frame, so one Destroy tears it all down
5. **Gradients are fitted to the GLYPHS, not the label.** A UIGradient maps across the whole GuiObject, and a title label is routinely 4-5x wider than the word inside it, so an unfitted sequence showed the word only ~20% of its own palette — `holo` rendered as flat pink instead of a rainbow. `fitSequence` squeezes the ramp into the slice the word actually covers, resolved from `TextBounds` together with `TextXAlignment`/`TextYAlignment`, and holds the end colours flat outside it
5b. **The fit lands on the glyph BOX, which is taller than the letters, so a VERTICAL ramp still wastes its ends.** `TextBounds.Y` is the whole line box and the title font spends roughly its top fifth and bottom fifth on ascender/descender room no capital reaches (caps ≈ 57% of `TextBounds.Y`), so a `rotation = 90` ramp authored across a full `0..1` shows the word only the middle of its palette — `fire` rendered as flat mid-orange with its white-hot crown and ember base both hidden in the padding. Fixed 2026-07-30 on the authoring side: `baseStops` now emits its `lightScale`/`darkScale` two-tone bunched into `0.20..0.80` with the ends repeated (**gated on `gradientAxis == "y"`** — a `rotation = 0` ramp is already fitted to the word's width and must not be bunched), and every fixed-palette preset in [[TitleStyles]] authors its stops pre-bunched the same way
6. The glow is built from **ghost TextLabels**: copies of the host whose fill sits at `TextTransparency = 1` carrying a UIStroke, since under the default Sibling ZIndexBehavior a child can never draw behind its parent and there is no way to put a glow layer underneath the glyphs. Several stacked at rising thickness and transparency give a word-shaped falloff — see [[TitleStyles]] for why that replaced the rectangular haze
7. **Ghost sizing is split by host type, and the split is the whole point.** A **TextScaled host outside a BillboardGui** (the Collection TITLES rows) gets a **TextScaled ghost plus a mirrored copy of the host's `UITextSizeConstraint`** — `mirrorSizeConstraint`, re-run on the geometry throttle so a cap parented *after* `apply()` is still picked up, which is exactly what made a build-time clone useless before. The ghost box is already the host box, so the engine derives both sizes from the same inputs in the same layout pass: they agree by construction, on every device, with no number crossing through Lua. Every **other** host keeps an explicit `TextSize` from `renderedTextSize` — for a non-TextScaled host the rendered size simply *is* `TextSize`, so nothing needs measuring there either, and BillboardGui hosts are excluded deliberately because a TextScaled ghost resamples into faint hairlines above a nametag. **Redwater's overhead plate is TextScaled inside a billboard, so it takes the explicit-size path**, unchanged by the phone fix
7b. **A host that cannot be measured yields nil, never a guess.** `renderedTextSize` used to fall back to the `UITextSizeConstraint`'s `MaxTextSize` when `TextBounds` read zero or NaN — but that is a **ceiling**, so the fallback could only ever be too big. `TextBounds` can stay unresolved indefinitely (see 15), and Roblox goes on drawing the host perfectly at whatever its box allows, so nothing corrected it: photographed on a phone, a Collection TITLES row ran 24px ghosts over 15px text, and every lightning strike flashed a copy of STORMCALLER half again the size of the title sitting behind it. Now `syncGhost` keeps a ghost's last known size when the host is unmeasurable, and `animate` refuses to draw the **specular rake and the flash strobe** — the two effects that copy the whole word — against anything but a *verified* size (see 7d). Losing a strike is cheap; a wrong-sized copy of the word is the artefact the ghosts exist to prevent
7c. **`geometryChanged` compares the ghosts' size to the host's, not just the glyph box** (`ghostsOutOfSync`). The four fractions cannot stand in for it: `wFrac`/`hFrac` are clamped to ≤ 1, so a label whose text overflows its box saturates both and a later change is invisible, and neither moves at all under a proportional resize — either way `textPxY`, the number every ghost's `TextSize` comes from, goes stale with nothing to notice. One property read per throttle tick makes any desync self-heal within `GEOM_INTERVAL` (measured: 15 frames), whatever caused it
7d. **`TextBounds.Y` DOES NOT EQUAL THE DRAWN SIZE ON A REAL PHONE. That was the bug, and it is why nothing reproduced in Studio.** The module's original note claimed the equality was "verified to equal the drawn size exactly" — verified *in Studio*, where it holds to the pixel at every size, aspect and device-simulator preset that was tried. On device it over-reports, and because every consumer reads the same source they all agree with each other while the screen shows something else: the ghost is built too big, `renderedTextSize` confirms the too-big number, and any check comparing the two finds them in perfect agreement. That self-consistency is what let the artefact survive two rounds of fixes aimed at the measuring code — a full-size copy of the word behind every `chrome` and `lightning` title, on every row, the instant the TITLES tab opened, phone only. **The way out is not a better measurement, it is not measuring** — see 7. Corollary worth remembering: a check is only as trustworthy as its most independent input, so `ghostInStep` compares what the ghost and the host each actually *laid out* (`TextBounds` against `TextBounds`, from the same layout pass) rather than anything this module derived. Bookkeeping was tried first and let 20 wrong-sized draws through where reading the instances let none. **Everything sized from `textPxY` is downstream of the same unreliable reading** — stroke thickness, particle size, the aura LOD thresholds and the gradient fit — so those are proportionally off on device too; they degrade gracefully rather than drawing a second word, which is why only the ghosts were worth restructuring
7e. **`laidOut` is a weaker claim than "the ghosts are the right size" — a real hole, though not the one above.** `glyphBox` rejects only non-finite values and a zero *box*; it accepts a **zero `TextBounds`**, while `renderedTextSize` refuses to guess from that same reading and returns nil. A label in that state reported itself laid out, kept its ghosts parked at whatever they last held, and was skipped by `ghostsOutOfSync` too, so nothing could correct it. `measured` now gates on `ghostsInStep` and re-syncs on the spot rather than waiting out the throttle. During an *active* resize a one-frame lag is irreducible — `TextBounds` only updates at end of frame — but it settles to exact agreement the moment resizing stops (verified: 0 wrong-sized draws over 180 settled frames while the effects still drew 159 times)
8. **Every effect dimension is proportional to the rendered text**, resolved live against `TextBounds.Y` — stroke thickness, particle size, haze size. See [[TitleStyles]] for why a fixed pixel thickness is wrong at both large and small text sizes
9. `shine` and `specular` sweep their band across the **word** by offsetting the gradient over a reach derived from the glyph span, rather than across the whole label — the old sweep ran -1..1 in label space and spent about three quarters of every cycle parked off the end of the text. `holo` scrolls by rebuilding its fitted rainbow on a quantised phase (roughly 7 rebuilds a second) instead of rotating the gradient, because rotation spins the axis around the label
10. All animation runs off **one shared Heartbeat connection** over a weak-keyed registry — never one connection per label. Static presets register too, but only to re-fit their geometry on a 0.25s throttle when a TextScaled label resizes under them; they do no per-frame work. The connection disconnects itself when the registry empties, and entries self-evict once their label is destroyed. **Each label's step is wrapped in its own `pcall`, and that is load-bearing:** a raw error in a shared `pairs` loop does not cost one label one frame, it abandons the rest of the iteration — and `pairs` order is stable for a given table, so every label sorting after the failing one stops being stepped for good. Measured with a deliberate throw: of five registered labels one ran 31 times over half a second and the other four ran zero; with the `pcall` all four survivors run every frame. The visible result of the old behaviour was a *frozen* effect rather than a missing one, which is the same wrong-sized-copy artefact as 7d reached from the other side. A label that fails `MAX_STEP_FAILURES` (10) consecutive times has its effects parked and is dropped from the registry, so it falls back to plain styled text instead of wearing a half-drawn effect. This matters most here: a FFA server puts one of these plates over every player, and a nameplate on a streamed-in adornee is exactly the label whose geometry can go non-finite
9b. **A surface can switch the aura off outright: `TitleAura = false` on the host label.** The bloom stack and the haze are the two things that draw *behind* the word rather than on it, and on a dense list they read as a second, blurred copy of the title sitting behind the real one. The size LOD thins them but never removes them, and the pixel caps cannot express "none" either — `strokePx` clamps **up** to `MinStrokePx`, so a tiny `TitleBloomMaxPx` just pins every layer to the floor. Everything that draws *on* the glyphs is untouched (gradient, tight stroke, particles, specular, flash), so an opted-out title still renders in its real style. No Redwater surface opts out -- the caller is Bounty's Collection TITLES rows; the kill cam, match summary and nameplate here all keep the full aura
10a. **The aura stack is resolved TOGETHER, not layer by layer** (`resolveBloom`). The pixel ceiling (`TitleBloomMaxPx`, which dense menu surfaces set as low as 2.25) is applied per layer, so past a certain text size *every* layer clamps to the same number — measured on a Bounty Pass card at 44px text, all three came out at exactly 2.25px. Three dilations at one thickness are three copies of ONE silhouette, and stacking them multiplies their alphas into a hard, near-opaque outline: the "ghost slab behind the word" this module exists to avoid. The layer-count LOD below cannot see it, because that reads only the TEXT size and never what the cap did to the stack. So a layer that is not at least `BLOOM_MIN_STEP` (1.35×) thicker than the one inside it is dropped rather than drawn twice. Below `BLOOM_OFF_PX` the aura goes entirely: a GothamBold stem is barely over a pixel at phone menu sizes, so even the capped tight rim doubles every glyph's weight and floods its counters — measured at 15px, rims of 1.35 / 0.90 / 0.65px ALL read as a grey slab, so this is a switch rather than a taper
10c. **`BLOOM_OFF_PX` is 16, raised from 13.** 13 was picked against an *estimate* of 11px for a pass card on a phone; measured, that card renders its title at 12px on a Galaxy A06, 13px on an iPhone 13 and 15px on an iPhone 16 Pro Max — the old floor sat INSIDE the phone range, so the slab appeared or not depending on the handset. 16 clears every phone menu card and still keeps the rim on nameplate text: LobbySpawn authors its title line at 16px and every catalog label fits `TAG_WIDTH` at that size, so no lobby nameplate loses its aura. Redwater's plate is TextScaled inside a billboard, so there the rim now drops out slightly closer in — the same slab, seen further away
10b. **The aura carries an LOD, and it is load-bearing.** The `bloom.layers` stack is authored for showcase text; scaling is proportional, so at a 22px shop card three dilations land at roughly 2 / 5 / 10px — close enough in *pixels* that they merge into one solid mass hugging the word and read as an ugly ghost slab behind it rather than as light coming off it. `bloomLayerCount` keeps all layers at ≥34px, two at ≥26px and only the tight rim below that, and `bloomThickness` caps that lone rim at 0.10 of text height so even it cannot fill the gaps between glyphs. The fix is **fewer layers**, not smaller fractions — shrinking the fractions just flattens the showcase sizes, which is where the effect earns its price. Dropped layers are parked at `Transparency = 1` in all three sizing paths (the stepper, `refreshStatic`, and the initial `apply`)
11. **A particle's sideways wander is a fraction of the TEXT HEIGHT, converted into the label's X scale** (`driftX = cfg.drift * textPx / state.absX`). An X scale unit and a Y scale unit are not the same length: one Y unit is the label's height, which a title label hugs, but one X unit is its full WIDTH, and a title label is routinely three times wider than the word inside it. Spending `hFrac` (a Y fraction) on the X axis made the wander a fraction of the LABEL — on a Collection title row, a 161px box around a 56px word, lightning's `drift = 0.42` threw sparks 60px clear of the word and off the end of the row, and on a pass card they left the card entirely. That is why `refreshGeometry` keeps `state.absX`
11b. A preset may run **several particle systems at once** (`preset.particles` is one system or a list); `animateSystem` steps one and `animateParticles` walks them. `state.blobs` stays a flat list of every blob so teardown and the fade path never have to know about systems. `fire` is why: tongues and embers differ in birth point, speed and fade curve, so one emitter cannot do both. Each system carries its own **LOD**: when the rendered text drops under 20px — nameplate territory, where a dozen grit motes are sub-pixel noise — the count is halved and the dropped particles are parked once rather than every frame. A FFA server puts one of these plates over every player and this is a mobile-first game
12. Every effect follows the label's own `TextTransparency`, which is the fade signal each surface already drives. That keeps effects in lockstep with the text without any caller knowing this module exists, and means a fade system that only knows how to hide the host label still hides the whole effect correctly. Fully faded labels short-circuit to a single property read per frame
12a. **The apply-time and static paths honour that signal too, and neither used to.** Effects are built at their authored alpha, so `apply()` on a label that is *already* faded out painted a fully-drawn title over an invisible plate. Only the animated path recovered, because `animate` re-derives `hostAlpha` every frame and parks the effects on the next Heartbeat; a **static** preset has no per-frame pass at all. Worse, `refreshStatic` rewrote every bloom layer's `Transparency` from the *authored* value, so it could re-light a hidden title whenever the glyph box moved under it — which a stud-sized `TextScaled` nameplate does continuously as the camera moves. `apply()` now parks the effects when the host is fully transparent, and `refreshStatic` gates on `hostAlpha` exactly as `animate` does, rewriting on the hidden→visible transition so the restore does not depend on the surface. **Only `tint` and `gradient` were affected** — they are the only static presets that build effects (`plain` builds none), which is 7 of the 28 catalog titles: DEADEYE, DRIFTER, EAGLE EYE, IRON BUCK, THE DEALER, BREACHER and GUNHAND. Parking at `Transparency = 1` is safe because every surface restores an effect from its `TitleFXBase` attribute rather than from the live property
13. Effect instances — including the ghosts — publish their authored alpha as a `TitleFXBase` attribute (exposed as `BaseTransparencyAttribute`) so fade systems restore them to their intended transparency. This matters most for the ghosts: a generic fade that drives every TextLabel to 0 would paint a solid duplicate of the title over the real one
14. Particles are rounded Frames (flames get a vertical alpha UIGradient tapering to the tip; round puffs skip it, since a vertical wipe across a circle leaves a flat bar), so there is **no image-asset dependency**. Flame tongues are born *inside* the glyph box at `cfg.birth` (a fraction of glyph height, ~0.6) and rise off the top edge. Two earlier placements were wrong: born at the baseline they rose straight **through** the letters, walking a column of orange blobs across the word every cycle — particles draw above the host label and a child can never go behind its parent — and born exactly at the top edge they read as detached bars hovering over it
15. **Geometry is only trusted once the label has laid out.** `AbsoluteSize` and `TextBounds` can read as NaN on a label that has not resolved yet (a BillboardGui whose adornee is still streaming in), and NaN survives both `math.clamp` and `math.max` untouched because each only compares — so `math.max(abs.X, 1)` does **not** guarantee a divisor of 1. `glyphBox` returns nil for non-finite or zero geometry, `refreshGeometry` keeps the last known-good values and reports failure, and a fresh state seeds `left/top/wFrac/hFrac/textPx` so the per-frame particle and haze maths can never read nil. A `laidOut` flag makes the stepper retry every frame until real numbers arrive, then fall back to the throttle
16. `accentOf(titleId)` / `styleOf(titleId)` let callers match surrounding chrome to a title

Mirrored verbatim from Bounty (the lobby owns and equips titles; Redwater only displays them).
