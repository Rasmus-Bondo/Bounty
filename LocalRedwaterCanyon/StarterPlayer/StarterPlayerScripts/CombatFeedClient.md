# CombatFeedClient

Unified rolling feed for the local player's combat rewards and for server-wide bounty announcements.

## What it does
1. Listens to three remotes and pushes each into one shared rolling feed: `CombatReward` (kind, xp, cash) from [[ProgressionService]], and `BountyPlaced` / `BountyClaimed` from [[BountyService]]. Bounty announcements about the local player are filtered out — they already got the personal `bounty_claim` / `bounty_placed` reward row
2. Eight styles (`kill` → "ELIMINATION", `assist` → "ASSIST", `bounty_claim`, `bounty_survived`, `bounty_placed`, `bounty_news`, `bounty_taken`, `tutorial_step` → "STEP COMPLETE", plus a "REWARD" default), each a colored title followed by a warm-white detail. `tutorial_step` (2026-08-17) is green rather than the feed's usual warm gold, because the player has already been taught that colour by the step-complete cue — TutorialClient flashes the healing vignette on the same instant the row arrives
2a. **A row is a SEQUENCE OF SEGMENTS, not one string** (2026-08-17), because the currency amounts now carry the game's real coin art and **RichText silently strips `<img/>`**. Verified by rendering all three forms side by side: the tag vanishes and `+25 <img .../>` comes out as `+25`, measuring 226px against the 290px the same line measures with the words spelled out. There is no markup route to an inline image, so `rewardSegments` returns a list — a text run holding each number, followed by an icon segment — and the coins are real `ImageLabel`s. `+$120` became `+120 [coin]` and `+5 Tokens` became `+5 [token]`. **XP keeps its word**: there is no XP icon, and inventing one would put a symbol on screen that appears nowhere else in the game. `push`'s `detail` argument accepts **either** a plain string (the two bounty announcements, one text segment, behaving exactly as they always did) or a segment list
2b. **The icons are the lobby's own `CurrencyHudGui` chip assets**, so a coin means the same thing on the HUD as it does in the shop. Measured with `EditableImage` rather than eyeballed: both are 1024x1024 canvases with glyphs of 790x886 and 775x868 — aspects 0.892 / 0.893, height-fills 0.865 / 0.848. They agree closely enough that **one box renders both at matching visual weight**, unlike the tutorial's keycaps, which needed a per-asset fill because Q filled 63% of its canvas and TAB 49%. `ICON_PX` (30) is the box HEIGHT and the glyph lands at ~0.86 of it, so the coin draws ~26px against Teko's ~23px cap height at `TEXT_SIZE` 32 — a shade taller than the digits, which is what makes it read as a coin rather than as punctuation. Measure a third currency before assuming it fits this box
2c. **The measured box, the padding and the shadow moved up one level** to a `Content` frame holding a horizontal `UIListLayout`; the text runs became its children. **One `UIShadow` on `Content`, never one per segment** — per-segment shadows would double up at every seam and darken the joins. The icon's gap lives inside its own box as a `UIPadding` rather than in the layout's `Padding`, so it applies before the coin and never after it; a trailing gap would put dead space between the last coin and the box edge the shadow is hugging. Truncation is applied to the **last** text run only, which is the one that reaches the edge, and the deferred refit pass is restricted to single-run rows — which is every row that can actually overrun, since the long ones are the bounty announcements and they carry no icons
3. Rows run in a **lane to the RIGHT of the crosshair**, starting `LANE_OFFSET_SCALE` (0.045 of width) past screen centre. The feed used to be centred, which put the newest row squarely over the aim point — on a Galaxy S25 Ultra (685x338 safe area) the row spanned y 108–136 against a crosshair at y 121.5. The offset is a fraction of **width** because the crosshair scales with width too (GunGui's Canvas is 0.065 of width, `RelativeXX`)
3a. **On touch, rows hang off the lane's RIGHT edge rather than starting at its left** (2026-08-24), so the reward figures line up in a column at the screen edge and short rows sit clear of the crosshair instead of beside it. `laneBounds` returns both edges plus a flag; `RIGHT_ALIGN` is `UserInputService.TouchEnabled`, resolved in `applyLayoutProfile` beside `LANE_MAX_SCALE`. **Desktop keeps the left-anchored lane it always had** — the feed is right-aligned to solve the crosshair and the thumb's half of a phone screen, and a desktop player has neither problem. A row too wide to fit still starts at the lane and grows right; it never crosses back over the aim point. `laneXOffset` is re-resolved on every layout pass, since a right-aligned row that kept its first X would creep left as it shrank down the stack
3b. **The right edge is the screen's, not a button's** (2026-08-24). It was the measured left edge of `MobileFireButton` held 12px clear, and rows shrank and then ellipsised to respect it. That trade was backwards: FIRE sits mostly *below* the feed's band, the touch buttons are translucent circles, and the clearance was being paid for in every row's size — on a tablet, where FIRE clamps to its 135px ceiling, it cost the longest Domination row its coin. **Rows may overlap the touch cluster**, so the bound is now `LANE_EDGE_SCALE` (0.02 of width) in from the GUI's own right edge — pure geometry, nothing measured, and `HunterFireGui` is no longer read at all. The GUI carries the default `CoreUISafeInsets`, so that edge is already the safe area. `LANE_MAX_SCALE` survives only as the pre-layout fallback for the frames before `AbsoluteSize` resolves
4. Newest row is inserted at the top and the stack grows **downward** from `BASE_Y_SCALE`, `ROW_STEP` px apart. Older rows shrink as they descend (`rowScale`: 11% per step, floor 0.58), so the feed reads as a receding stack rather than a wall of equal text
4a. **The pop-in tween became a loop over `FadeParts`** (2026-08-17). A row is several instances now and they do not share a transparency property — text fades on `TextTransparency`, coins on `ImageTransparency` — so the old single-label tween would have lifted the words to 0 and left the coins sitting at their starting 0.12. `entry.Label` still points at the title run so anything reading it finds a TextLabel
5. Each row is a `CanvasGroup`, so the fade-out tweens `GroupTransparency` on one channel — text, `UIStroke` and `UIShadow` stay at the exact same alpha instead of shimmering apart. Rows hold for `VISIBLE_TIME` 3.4s then fade over 1.25s; overflow past `MAX_ROWS` is evicted with a fast 0.2s fade. The canvas is `SHADOW_MARGIN` (18px) larger than the label on every side so the shadow has somewhere to fade out — a CanvasGroup clips to its own `AbsoluteSize`, so a label flush at x=0 had its blur cut dead on three sides. That margin is subtracted back out of the row's position, **scaled**, so rows don't stagger as they shrink
6. **Layout is derived from the viewport, not picked from a bucket table** (`applyLayoutProfile`): `FEED_SCALE` is `(height/1080)^0.85` clamped to 0.34–1.15, and row step, row count and base-Y all follow from it. It is a curve rather than a ratio because small screens need proportionally *larger* UI — a straight ratio puts a phone at 0.31 (10px text). Lands 338px at 0.37 (12px, 3.5% of height) and 1080px at 1.0 (32px, 3.0%). 1080 is the curve's fixed point, so **the exponent only bends the small end and desktop is untouched by it**; it was 0.7 until 2026-08-24, which put a phone at 14px — three of those rows plus their shadows covered a fifth of a 338px screen. The five old discrete profiles jumped 0.64→1.12 across a one-pixel change and left phones at 6.1% of height. Re-runs on `ViewportSize` and `CurrentCamera` changes and re-lays out the live rows
6a. `ROW_STEP` is `ROW_HEIGHT * FEED_SCALE * 1.05`, which leaves about 0.42 of the text height clear between one row's glyphs and the next at every viewport. It was 0.95, leaving 0.26 — and since `UIShadow` blurs 14px *inside* the same `UIScale`, the halos of adjacent rows met in that gap and the stack read as one dark block
7. **`BASE_Y_SCALE` is solved backwards from the stack's BOTTOM edge** (`STACK_BOTTOM_Y` 0.58), so a taller stack starts higher instead of reaching further down. At the old fixed 0.565 base the stack grew down to ~0.79 on PC and sat on top of the first-person viewmodel, which renders into the lower right — the same side as the lane
8. **Row width is measured, not guessed.** `GetTextBoundsAsync` (with `RichText` set, or the `<font>` tags measure as literal characters — 258px parsed vs 785px unparsed) sizes each box to its own text plus padding, so the `UIShadow` hugs the words. It previously blurred a flat 530px box holding 258px of text, producing a dark slab twice the width of the line. The lane's right bound is geometric — see 3b
9. Publishes the resolved base-Y as a **`FeedBaseYScale` attribute on its own `CombatFeedGui`**. KillCamClient bottom-anchors the "KILLED BY" card a clearance gap above the feed's first row and reads this attribute rather than duplicating the layout maths. The value now moves with the viewport rather than coming from a fixed table, so nothing on the kill cam side may assume the old 0.565 desktop constant
8a. **Segment boxes are `AutomaticSize.X`, not measured** (2026-08-24), and this is what stopped a row reading as one run-on line on a phone. The measurement only ever *estimates* what the renderer lays out: hinting at small sizes moves the real width by anywhere from −5% to +14% of the prediction, in both directions and not as a function of length, so when the estimate came up short the `UIListLayout` started the next segment inside the tail of the previous one. It never showed on desktop, where the scale is 1.0 and the estimate is exact. `AutomaticSize` asks the renderer instead of predicting, so each box *is* its text; `SEGMENT_GAP` and `TITLE_GAP` then supply real separation, the title's as a `UIPadding` because `AutomaticSize` discards a width. The measured widths survive only to size the outer box, where a few pixels either way cost nothing but shadow, and to decide the clamp. `Content` auto-sizes too on an unclamped row, so `layoutRows` reads its real width back for the right-alignment and `push` runs a second layout pass once that has resolved
8b. **A row wider than its lane shrinks before anything is cut** (2026-08-24): `fitScale` rides on the row's existing `UIScale` as a second factor down to `MIN_FIT_SCALE` (0.8), and only past that does it truncate. A long row a fifth smaller reads as part of the feed's existing size ladder, where an ellipsis reads as a fault. Since the lane was opened to the screen edge (3b) it is rarely reached at all — it is the backstop for a long player name in a bounty row on a narrow screen, not something the reward rows should meet
10. `TextTruncate` is enabled **only** for a line that genuinely overran the lane, never for one whose box was sized to fit it. A clamped row walks a width budget across its segments so the ellipsis lands on the **box** edge; sized to their own full text instead, the labels truncated against their own width, so the tail simply ran on past the shadow and off the lane. The fit allowance estimates the renderer's scaled-glyph rounding (bounds are measured at `TEXT_SIZE` but the row renders through a `UIScale`, and that error grows as a share of the text the smaller the row gets), so with `AtEnd` always on, a few pixels of underestimate cost the whole `+$120`; with `None` they cost the last glyph's final column, clipped invisibly inside a 680px canvas. A clamped line refits its box to the truncated text, converting `TextBounds` out of rendered pixels first — it is reported **scaled** while `Size` is not, and using it raw collapsed a bounty row to `BOUNTY…`
11. Destroys the legacy `CombatTextGui` and `BountyGui` ScreenGuis on startup, so an older build's popups can't render alongside the feed. `ResetOnSpawn = false`, DisplayOrder 8500
12. **Silent for the whole onboarding lesson** (2026-08-10) **except for one row** (2026-08-17). `push` returns immediately when `workspace.TutorialMode` is `true`, so the reward rows a tutorial generates — bot kills, bounty announcements — never reach the screen. The lesson teaches one thing at a time and the banner is what the player is meant to be reading; a feed lane firing beside it is the same class of noise the kill feed was suppressed for. The gate is on `push`, not on the remote listeners, so the remotes stay connected and the feed resumes the instant a normal round starts on a normal server. Reading the attribute rather than a flag of its own means nothing has to be told when the lesson ends
12a. **The exception is `TUTORIAL_ROWS`, and it is an exception rather than a loosening of the rule.** Since 2026-08-17 the lesson pays its completion award a step at a time, so a `tutorial_step` row **is** the feedback for the thing the banner just asked the player to do, arriving on the step-complete cue rather than during it. It is a named set tested alongside `tutorialSuppressed()`, so a future row kind cannot slip through by accident — everything else the feed carries stays suppressed for the reasons above. Verified by running the file with `TutorialMode` forced true: an `ELIMINATION` and a `SOMEBOT IS ON A SPREE` were both dropped and only the instalment rendered

## Domination rows (2026-08-22)

Three `STYLES` entries: `domination_capture` (OBJECTIVE CAPTURED), `domination_neutralise` (OBJECTIVE
NEUTRALISED), `domination_defend` (OBJECTIVE DEFENDED) — warm golds like the kill row; the kind
strings are the ones ProgressionService.awardObjective sends.
