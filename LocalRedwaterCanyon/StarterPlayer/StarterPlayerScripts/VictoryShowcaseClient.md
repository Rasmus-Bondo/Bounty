# VictoryShowcaseClient

End-of-round cinematic: a podium reveal for the top 3 players, then a single combined summary screen — podium on the right, match stats on the left, intermission countdown bottom-centre, RETURN TO BOUNTY bottom-right. Replaces the old PostGameScript leaderboard popup.

## What it does
0. **The match summary is TWO steps, not three** (2026-08-06, the infinite match loop). Step 1 is the Dust cinematic + "MATCH COMPLETED", now **halved to ~4.08s**; step 2 is one screen carrying everything the old steps 2 and 3 carried separately. There is no CONTINUE button — `CanvasContinue` and its whole subtree were deleted from `StarterGui.VictoryShowcase` along with `continueButtonConnection` — and **no local-player podium dummy**: the camera holds the stadium shot for the whole of step 2, and the player's own result is fully covered by the stats card. `tweenCameraToLocalPlayerPart`, `getLocalCameraPart`, `LOCAL_PLAYER_CAMERA_FOV` and `LOCAL_CAMERA_TWEEN_TIME` are gone with it, and `getFinishSpawns` no longer *requires* `PlayerLocal` / `CameraPartLocal`. `SpotlightPartVictoryLocal` was resolved best-effort for a while after that, to switch off a light authored `Enabled` over an empty slot; it went with the sky room in entry 0g and is no longer looked up at all
0a. **Step 1's halving is one constant, not six.** `CINEMATIC_PLAYBACK_SPEED` (0.35 → **0.70**) is shared by the `AdjustSpeed` call and `getCinematicCutTime`, which divides by it — they are the same number and silently desync into a visible animation-reset frame if written twice. The letterbox bars, the fade in/out and the stadium hold were each halved alongside it (0.28→0.14, 0.5→0.25, 0.35→0.18, 1.65→0.82, 0.5→0.25). **The muzzle flash could not simply be halved.** It was `math.max(0, math.min(3, cutTime * 0.62) - elapsed)`, and it only ever landed where it did because the `min(3, …)` cap *bound* at 0.35x (0.62 × 5.371 = 3.33, clamped to 3.0 — i.e. clip-time 1.05s of the 2.000s clip). At 0.70x the cap stops binding and the same fraction drifts the flash to clip-time 1.166s, off the frame the shot fires on. It is now `MUZZLE_CLIP_TIME / CINEMATIC_PLAYBACK_SPEED`, expressed in **clip time**, so it lands on the same animation frame at any playback speed. `getCinematicCutTime`'s no-length fallback is likewise derived from `CINEMATIC_CLIP_LENGTH` rather than restating a number
0a-i. **The clip's `Length` is resolved BEFORE it starts playing, and that is what made step 1 read as ~1s of animation** (2026-08-06). `getCinematicCutTime` used to spin for up to 2 seconds waiting for `AnimationTrack.Length` to become non-zero — with the clip **already running** behind a shut letterbox, and with the cut timed from *before* that wait. Whatever the asset cost to resolve therefore came straight off the only part the player was meant to see, and it is worst on the first match end of a session, which is the only one most players get. The wait moved into `spawnCinematicAvatar`, in front of `track:Play`, so the full ~2.44s of animation is now visible; `getCinematicCutTime` no longer waits at all. Two things keep this from costing step 1 any wall time: the clip is `ContentProvider:PreloadAsync`ed at script init (it is only ever used at a match end, so every client hit it cold), and whatever the resolve *does* cost is measured into `cinematicLoadCost` and paid back out of `CINEMATIC_STADIUM_HOLD` — the full-blackout hold between the cut and the podium, which has slack and shows nothing. `CINEMATIC_LENGTH_WAIT` (0.55) is bounded at what that hold can absorb above its 0.25 floor, which is why `RoundSystem.Config`'s `SUMMARY_CINEMATIC_TIME` did not have to move: step 1 measures 4.08s warm **and** cold. `CINEMATIC_MIN_VISIBLE_TIME` (1.5) is a floor on screen time measured from the letterbox opening rather than from the clip starting, capped at the clip's real end — it does not fire today, and exists so a future retune cannot quietly take the animation back
0b. **Step 2 fades in as one beat at `REVEAL_SEQUENCE_TIME` (2.35s).** The three staggered `revealRank` calls are unchanged (3rd at t=0, 2nd at 0.95, 1st at 1.90; each costs `REVEAL_FADE_DELAY` 0.05 + the 0.4s `REVEAL_FADE_INFO`, so #1 is fully in at 2.35). At that moment `populateVictoryCanvas` runs and `Canvas`, `CanvasReturnToBounty` and `TimerCanvas` all fade up together on the same curve. Client total is 4.08 + 2.35 + 6 = **12.43s** since 2026-08-16, held under [[RoundController]]'s `POST_GAME_VICTORY_TIME` of 13.0 — the server must outlast the client's sequence, never the reverse; the margin is unchanged at +0.57s. **This is also the moment the map-vote tiles arrive**: the server opens the ballot at exactly this offset so the stats card, podium and ballot land as one screen (see MapVoteClient, which owns its own ScreenGui and is not wired to this script)
0c. **The countdown drives the authored `TimerCanvas` → `TimerLabel`**, which existed unwired. Its seconds come from the payload's `countdownSeconds`, **never a client-side literal** — the intermission runs on the server's clock, and a local number would drift into "still counting while the next round starts". Copy is `NEXT MATCH IN: n`, single digits, no `mm:ss`. Ticking stops at 1: reaching 0 is not the client's business, the server's `"Clear"` is what ends the screen. The countdown window is also the map-vote ballot window — the same six seconds, which is why the vote panel renders no timer of its own
0c-i. **`TimerLabel` is authored `TextScaled = false`, `TextWrapped = false`, `TextSize = 100` (a sentinel), and its drawn size comes ENTIRELY from the `ResponsiveTextSize` `UITextSizeConstraint`** (2026-08-16). This is not a style choice. `TextScaled` and `TextWrapped` are **coupled in the engine**: setting `TextScaled = true` forces `TextWrapped = true`, and setting `TextWrapped = false` forces `TextScaled = false` — there is no scaling without wrapping. That matters because `TextScaled` + `TextWrapped` does not pick a size and then wrap; it picks whichever of the two the box affords the LARGER text on, so a label near the threshold flips between one line and two on a change of a single glyph. Measured on a 1080x810 iPad: `NEXT MATCH IN: 6` and `: 4` wrapped to two lines while `: 5`, `: 3`, `: 2` and `: 1` did not, and the pop below crossed that threshold and back every tick — the reported "bugged and flickery" countdown. Turning wrapping off turns scaling off with it, which is the trap: the label then silently renders at its authored `TextSize` on every device forever. The sentinel plus the constraint is what buys size control back — a `UITextSizeConstraint` governs the drawn size whether or not `TextScaled` is on (verified: `TextSize` 200 under caps of 48/32/16 renders exactly 48/32/16, one line, `TextFits`). Swept all six countdown values against both pop states at 1080p: zero multi-line or overflow cases
0c-ii. **The cap is DERIVED per showcase, not authored.** `computeCountdownBaseMax` sizes it from the label's own width (viewport x the authored `TimerCanvas`/`TimerLabel` X fractions x `endScreenScale`) over `COUNTDOWN_TEXT_WIDTH_RATIO` (5.6 — the copy measures 4.83px of width per px of text size, plus padding), clamped to `COUNTDOWN_MAX_TEXT_PX` (48). A flat authored 48 was only ever right at 1080p: 48px of this copy is 232px wide and an iPad's label is 194px. Resolved sizes are 21px on an iPhone 7, 27px on an iPhone 14, 34px on an iPad, 35px windowed, and 48px at 1080p — desktop unchanged. Deriving it also removes the old capture-once hazard outright: `countdownBasePopMax` no longer reads the property the pop tweens, so a teardown that `Cancel()`s a tween mid-flight cannot leave it inheriting a half-finished 51 and ratcheting upward each round
0c'. **Each tick pops the countdown ~10% and settles back with a little overshoot** (2026-08-07) — 0.12s Quad out, 0.22s `Back` back, inside the house 0.12–0.28s band. It drives **two** properties — the `CountdownPopScale` `UIScale` on the box and the `ResponsiveTextSize` cap — and since 0c-i the cap is the one that decides the drawn size on every device, the box scale riding along so the text keeps its proportion inside a box growing by the same 10%. That equality is what keeps the pop safe: the derived cap sits at ~85% of the label width, and popping cap and box together preserves the fit rather than blowing it. `countdownBasePopMax` is now assigned from `computeCountdownBaseMax` per showcase (see 0c-ii) instead of captured once off the instance. `resetCountdownPop` puts both back at the top of each summary, before the fade state is collected
0d. **Three loop-safety fixes, none of which mattered when the server died after one match.** `victoryCanvasOriginalPosition` caches `Canvas`'s authored Position once — the slide-in offsets from it and tweens back, so re-reading it each round is how the offset would compound. `hideVictoryShowcaseGui` parks the canvas back on it and also hides `TimerCanvas`. And the RETURN TO BOUNTY handler restores its canvas's authored transparencies after `tweenGuiOut` finishes: `getGuiFadeState` reads **live** values, so a player whose leave teleport failed would find the button faded to 1 and never see it again
0e. **The camera is PINNED every render step, not assigned once** (2026-08-06). `holdVictoryCamera` binds at `RenderPriority.Camera + 1` from the moment step 1's cut lands and holds until `restoreCamera` releases it on `"Clear"`. Scriptable stops Roblox's own camera scripts writing `CFrame`; it does not stop another of ours, and one does — RoundClientController's `restoreCamera` fires on the same `GameplayVisibility(false)` that immediately precedes the showcase, and spawns a task that writes `CameraType = Custom` again after a `WaitForChild("Humanoid", 5)`. Landing up to five seconds later, that is in the middle of the podium, and it hands the player mouse look over the shot. The pin re-claims Scriptable as well as re-writing the CFrame, so the framing is unmovable whatever the source. Releasing it **first** inside `restoreCamera` is not optional: otherwise the next frame re-claims the camera and strands the player on the podium shot for good
0e-i. **The podium band's left edge is READ off the authored `Canvas`, not restated as literals** (2026-08-16). `getStatsPanelBandLeft()` returns `centre + halfWidth * getVictoryEndScreenScale() + PODIUM_BAND_PAD`, taking centre and half-width from the instance. It used to be the literal `0.15 + 0.15 * scale`, which was correct only while the panel was 0.30 wide at 0.15 — widening it to 0.34 at 0.17 would have left the band where it was and walked the podium back over the scoreboard, with nothing in the solver complaining because it would simply have solved the wrong problem. Only the half-width takes the scale: a `UIScale` shrinks the box about its own centre, so scaling the centre too would drift the panel sideways. The **box** is used rather than the drawn card inside it — the more conservative of the two, and what the solver was tuned against
0f. **The end-screen scale is 0.9 on touch, up from 0.7.** At 0.7 the stats card drew 164→**128x248** on an iPhone 14, a narrow strip in a lot of empty left-hand screen. A phone is height-constrained and the card is a fixed-aspect object, so height sets the ceiling: 0.9 puts its top edge at 0.095 of the viewport, clear of Roblox's ~36px mobile topbar, and 1.0 would put it underneath. Widening the card instead of scaling it would mean re-laying-out its internals. Nothing else needed changing because `computePodiumFraming` reads the same `getVictoryEndScreenScale`, so the podium band walked right on its own (`bandLeft` 0.275 → 0.305); re-solved across the device matrix, every viewport still passes all three constraints and 1920x1080 still resolves to plate scale exactly 1.000, so PC is untouched
0g. **The podium moved out of the sky room and down onto the map** (2026-08-23). `getFinishSpawns` now reads the GROUND set - `workspace.FinishSpawns.Player1/2/3`, three pads standing on the `Pillars` plates out in the canyon - and the summary shot is `CameraFolder.CameraPartVictory`. The old set was `FinishSpawns.BoxPlayerSpawns`: a sealed 150x58x150 room at y~205 whose walls WERE the backdrop, which is why the showcase had to hide the room, cut to it behind a blackout and then reveal it. On the ground the map is the backdrop - already there, already lit, never toggled - so `BoxModel` is gone from the scene table entirely, along with its `PreloadAsync`, both `setModelLocalVisible` calls and the `not scene.BoxModel` clause in the scene-complete check. **The spotlights did not come down with it.** `Spotlights` is still resolved but is now OPTIONAL and normally absent, via `FindFirstChild` and **not** `WaitForChild` - three 10s timeouts on parts that are meant to be missing would stall the reveal well past RoundController's `POST_GAME_VICTORY_TIME`. Drop parts named `SpotlightPartVictory1..3` into `FinishSpawns` and the reveal lights them again with no code change. `CameraPartAnimation` was parented inside `BoxPlayerSpawns` but frames `VictoryScreenRigSpawn` out in the map, so it was lifted up to `FinishSpawns` first - it is the cinematic intro rather than part of the old set. **`BoxPlayerSpawns` was then deleted outright from both match places** (21 descendants each), together with RoundController's `setVictoryBoxVisible` and its saved-state table, which were its last references anywhere. `PODIUM_MAX_DOLLY` (40) keeps its value but not its old justification - it was bounded by the room's back wall and is now bounded by map geometry getting between camera and podium. Mirrored verbatim into Frostbite Claim, which has the same ground pads and its own `CameraPartVictory`; that camera already sits at podium distance and barely moves, while Redwater's starts tight and `computePodiumFraming` dollies it back 6-14 studs
0h. **Tablets get their own podium camera base** (2026-08-26). `getCameraPart()` returns `CameraFolder.CameraPartVictoryTablet` when `isTablet()` - `UserInputService.TouchEnabled` **and** `math.min(viewport.X, viewport.Y) >= TABLET_MIN_SHORT_EDGE` (600), the same short-edge test KillFeedClient and TutorialClient use, so there is one definition of "tablet" in the game rather than four. PC and phone resolve `CameraPartVictory` exactly as before and nothing on those paths changed. A separate base is needed at all because `computePodiumFraming` solves the dolly against viewport **aspect**, so a 4:3 tablet asks for far more of it than a 16:9 desktop - 9.5 studs against Redwater's 1.5 - and reversing 9.5 studs out of the authored shot parks the camera 2.1 studs inside the lumber pile behind it, drawing a crate across the bottom of the frame with the podium behind it; Frostbite did the same into a terrain lip at 7.5. Capping the dolly is the wrong fix, because the dolly is what holds the podium clear of the stats panel - a cap trades one collision for another. **Both bases sit at 18 deg elevation, and that number is a floor rather than a preference.** A first pass at 24 deg (Redwater) and 30 deg (Frostbite) cleared the geometry and was rejected on sight for looking down on the podium; 18 is the lowest either map allows. Redwater's is 26 studs out, swung 5 deg LEFT of the authored azimuth (solved dolly 2.5, plate 0.74 against the authored shot's 0.73, nearest geometry in frame 9.0 studs against 2.1) - below 18 the crate re-enters the bottom-right corner. Frostbite's is 27 studs out, swung 15 deg left (solved dolly 0.0, plate 0.56, nearest 7.2). **Frostbite's floor is a cliff, not clutter:** its podium stands on a ledge above a frozen lake whose `IcePart` sits at y=2.1 against the podium plane's y=14.4, so a lower camera looks straight over the edge and fills the bottom 40% of the frame with the lake. The left swing matters for the same reason - ray a line straight down from the camera and at the authored azimuth the ground under it drops to the ice past r=27, while at 15-30 deg left it stays solid terrain at y=17-19 all the way out to r=33. The swing is bounded by the winners' facing: `placeDummyAtSlot` pivots each rig to its slot's own CFrame, the three average to azimuth 96.8 (the authored camera is 3.2 deg off their front), so anything past roughly 35 deg starts showing their backs - which is what rules out the otherwise-perfect 14 deg shots from the far side. The lookup is `FindFirstChild` rather than `WaitForChild` for the same reason `Spotlights` is: absent has to stay free so a map with no tablet shot pays no timeout while the summary is being built, and a miss degrades to the authored framing rather than to nothing
1. Listens to the `VictoryShowcase` RemoteEvent: `"Start"` (with `stats`, `topPlayers`, `duration`, `countdownSeconds`, `defaultHunter`, `victoryAnimationsByRank` payload from [[RoundController]]; each `stats`/`topPlayers` entry also carries an `emote = { name, animationId }`) and `"Clear"` — which now arrives at the end of **every** round, not only at server shutdown
2. Spawns scripted, physics-disabled clone "dummy" character models (using each entry's `character` selection, falling back to a default Dust skin). That selection is the hunter + skin the player **finished the match as**, resolved server-side by [[RoundController]] from the live `SkinService.getSelection` rather than their arrival teleport data, so a mid-match hunter swap (HunterSwitchService) is reflected here at dedicated `workspace.FinishSpawns` slots for ranks 1-3 (see entry 0g -- these were `FinishSpawns.BoxPlayerSpawns` until 2026-08-23). `placeDummyAtSlot` does **not** pivot the rig straight onto the slot part — it first measures the clone's real foot plane with `getFootLift` and raises the placement by however far the feet hang below the model's pivot. The slot parts were positioned assuming every rig's pivot sits at foot level, which was true until Bram's rig was scaled up 1.1x: that pushes his feet 0.3 studs below his pivot, so a Bram on the podium stood buried to the ankles in the floor while the other hunters looked fine. The lift is clamped to zero so it can only raise — Vesper's and Fortuna's feet sit 0.15 studs *above* their pivot, and pulling them down would change how they already stand. It is measured on the fresh clone, before `setupDummyPhysics` unanchors the rig and the emote starts moving joints, because these rigs are posed by AnimationConstraints relative to the anchored HumanoidRootPart and the stored pose is exactly the constraint rest pose. The cinematic `VictoryScreenAvatar` is placed through the same helper (a no-op for that template today, since its feet already sit on its pivot). Each dummy plays **that player's equipped emote** — `makeAnimationFromServerChoice(rank, emoteData)` prefers the entry's `emote` (the player's equipped emote resolved by [[RoundController]] from `EquippedEmote`) and falls back to the random per-rank `victoryAnimationsByRank` choice
3. Runs a cinematic intro: locks the camera to a dedicated `FinishSpawns.CameraPartAnimation` part and then to `CameraFolder.CameraPartVictory`, fades in letterbox bars, applies a desaturated color-correction + depth-of-field "focus" look, spawns a separate slow-motion `VictoryScreenAvatar` cinematic actor with a muzzle-flash/smoke/sound effect, then cuts to the podium
4. Reveals 3rd, then 2nd, then 1st place sequentially (staggered ~0.95s apart) — each reveal turns on that rank's spotlight *if one is authored* (see entry 0g -- none are, on the ground podium), plays a spotlight sound, and then fades the hunter dummy and its 3-row BillboardGui up **together**. The dummy and the plate share one delay and one curve (`REVEAL_FADE_DELAY` = 0.05s, `REVEAL_FADE_INFO` = 0.4s Quad/Out): `tweenModelIn` tweens every BasePart's `LocalTransparencyModifier` from 1 to 0 on exactly the tween `tweenBillboardIn` uses for the plate's labels and strokes. The dummy previously hard-popped to visible via `setModelLocalVisible(model, true)` while the plate faded up over the following 0.4s, so the name and title landed out of step with the hunter. The fade rides `LocalTransparencyModifier` rather than `Transparency` because it composes with each part's authored value (`effective = T + LTM * (1 - T)`) — parts meant to stay invisible (HumanoidRootPart) or semi-transparent keep their authored look instead of being forced opaque, which is also why the hide path uses it. The spotlight and its sound still fire first so the beat reads as light hitting the podium before the winner resolves out of it -- with no spotlight authored, the sound alone carries that beat. The plate rows are **row 1** = rank + kills (e.g. `#1   2 KILLS`, rank-colored — replaces the old `#1 CHAMPION`/`RUNNER-UP`/`TOP PLAYER` descriptor), **row 2** = player name, **row 3** = that winner's equipped cosmetic title (resolved from their live `EquippedTitle` / `EquippedTitleId` attributes published by the server's TitleDisplayService via `entry.userId`, and rendered through [[TitleRenderer]] in its cosmetic style — gradient, halo, flames; hidden if they left or have none). The plate uses `SizeOffset = (0, 0.5)` so it sits entirely ABOVE its anchor point — the text can never overlap the podium dummy's body on mobile or PC. **The rank and name rows use `TextScaled` with a `UITextSizeConstraint` `MaxTextSize` cap (18 / 28) instead of a fixed `TextSize`**, in non-overlapping top-anchored bands (`0.01–0.27` / `0.29–0.67` / `0.70–0.98`). That keeps each row inside its own band on any plate size. The caps are the PC sizes, which the bands are big enough to reach on desktop, so the PC look is unchanged and a smaller plate scales its text down
4a. **Plate size comes from `getPodiumPlateScale()`, which tracks viewport HEIGHT — not from `getVictoryEndScreenScale()`** (fixed 2026-07-30). A BillboardGui's offset `Size` is **absolute screen pixels**: it does not shrink with camera distance and it does not shrink with the viewport. Verified directly — two identical plates at 12 and 24 studs render the same pixel size. The on-screen gap between the three podium dummies is the exact opposite, being world geometry projected through the camera, so it is proportional to viewport height (Roblox's `FieldOfView` is vertical). A fixed-pixel plate therefore eats a larger share of that gap the shorter the viewport is, which is why the top-3 plates piled into each other on a phone while sitting clear on a monitor. **A fixed mobile scale cannot fix this** — the old flat `0.7` picked one number for every phone when the number needed is whatever keeps the plate proportional to a gap that keeps changing. `scale = clamp(viewportY / 800, 0.4, 1)`: the ~245px gap at a 614px-tall viewport scales as ~`0.4 * viewportY`, and the widest content a plate can draw is 282 of its 340px, so break-even is ~708px of viewport height; the 800 reference keeps `1.0` reserved for viewports with room to spare, and every ordinary desktop (768+) still clamps to essentially 1. Measured after the change: 1080p gives scale exactly 1.000 with a 430px gap against a 340px plate, 720p gives 0.900 with 39px of worst-case slack, a 360px-tall phone gives 0.45. `getVictoryEndScreenScale()` is deliberately left alone and still drives the stats-screen canvases: that is a ScreenGui whose room *is* the viewport, so its fixed mobile/small-screen ladder is right for it and wrong here
4c. **The level badge sits in a reserved gutter, and the gutter reserves its DIAGONAL.** The badge template carries `Rotation = 45` to make the diamond shape and sits at `0.9` of its slot, so it *draws* `53.5 * uiScale` across while being sized into a `42 * uiScale` slot — it overflows its own slot by a third. It used to be pinned at a flat `Position = fromScale(0.14, 0.5)` with nothing reserved, which put it far from its own name with empty plate in between; that empty space is exactly where the *next* dummy's plate lands, so on a phone the badge read as belonging to the player beside it and drew straight through their kill count. Now the name row is inset by that gutter on **both** sides (so it stays centred over its own dummy) and the badge is centred in the left gutter. Because the badge can only ever be in its gutter and the name can only ever be in the region between them, a plate's drawn content can never exceed its own width — which is what makes the scale rule above a sufficient guarantee rather than a tuning. `TextScaled` shrinks a long name into the narrower region, so nothing has to be measured here; that matters, because `TextBounds` on a `TextScaled` label over-reports on real phones (see 4b') and could not be trusted to place the badge against the name's edge anyway
4a'. **The height rule in 4a is now a CEILING, not the answer** (2026-08-06). It is `getPodiumPlateHeightScale()`; `getPodiumPlateScale()` returns whatever `computePodiumFraming` decided. The combined summary screen pushes the podium into the band of screen to the **right** of the stats panel, and 4a's whole derivation is "keep the plate proportional to the on-screen gap between two podium anchors" — a gap that is no longer a function of viewport height alone, because the camera's dolly and strafe both compress it, and the strafe compresses it **unevenly**: rank 1 stands ~7 studs nearer the camera than rank 3, so it swings right faster and the rank 1 → rank 3 gap closes first. `computePodiumFraming` therefore solves the camera CFrame and the plate scale **together, as one problem**, once per showcase, and parks the answer in `podiumFraming` so the plates and the shot can never be derived from different framings. The collision width is the plate's **drawn content** (282 of its 340px), exactly as 4a established
4a''. **Why the framing is solved rather than tuned.** Three constraints hold at once: nothing crosses into the stats panel (whose right edge is *not* a flat number — `Canvas` is Size X **0.34 anchored at 0.17** since 2026-08-16, was 0.30 at 0.15, and its `UIScale` shrinks it about that anchor, so the edge moves with `getVictoryEndScreenScale()`); rank 3's plate stays on screen; and adjacent plates do not overlap. No fixed "shift N studs, dolly back M" satisfies all three at every aspect, because `FieldOfView` is **vertical** so the visible *width* scales with aspect ratio. On a 4:3 window the podium's own anchor span already fills ~0.61 of the screen against a band of ~0.64 — no shift fits it and the camera must dolly back first — while on 16:9 and wider a dolly is not merely unnecessary but harmful, costing plate size for room that is already there. A flat 15%-of-width shift with a fixed 9-stud dolly was measured pushing rank 3 to 1.02–1.03 of screen width and rank 2 back to 0.25 (inside the panel) on 4:3 and near-square windowed viewports, *and* collapsing the rank 1 → rank 3 gap to 234px against a 340px plate at 1080p. The solve takes the **smallest dolly that admits a solution**, shifts the minimum needed to clear the panel, then spends any leftover slack on centring — but never at the cost of the plate scale the viewport height would otherwise allow, which is why an ultrawide keeps the podium beside the panel with full-size plates instead of centring it with small ones. Verified against all three constraints at 14 viewports from 667×375 to 3440×1440; 1920×1080 still resolves to plate scale exactly 1.000, so the PC look is a pure rightward shift
4a'''. **`PODIUM_PLATE_GAP_MARGIN` (1.1) exists because a derived scale lands ON the boundary, not near it.** Wherever the gap is the binding constraint, `plateScale = gap / contentWidth` is an *equality* — so adjacent plates come out exactly touching, with zero slack, every time. Caught on a screenshot rather than in the arithmetic, which read a satisfied `121 / 121`: the rank 1 and rank 3 content bands abutted to the pixel. The margin requires the gap to be 10% wider than the content, so neighbours read as *separated* rather than merely non-overlapping. It is inert wherever viewport height binds instead, which is every ordinary desktop viewport — 1920×1080 is still exactly 1.000. Screenshot-verified at 667×375, 1024×768 (4:3), 1280×1024 (5:4, the aspect the framing is most sensitive to) and 1920×1080, previewed by pillarboxing the Studio viewport to each target aspect: masking to a centred region of width `viewportHeight × targetAspect` reproduces that aspect's projection exactly, since vertical FOV already spans the full height and horizontal position scales linearly. **The device simulator cannot do this job** — it does not change the edit camera's `ViewportSize`, so the 3D framing renders at the Studio window's own aspect no matter which device is selected
4b. **The TITLE row is the exception: it is sized explicitly and must never be `TextScaled`** (fixed 2026-07-30). [[TitleRenderer]] builds a title's halo from ghost copies of the label, and for a host inside a BillboardGui it sizes those ghosts from an explicit `TextSize` — a `TextScaled` ghost resamples into hairlines over a nametag, so that path is deliberately closed to billboards. A `TextScaled` **host** therefore left the renderer deriving the size from `TextBounds.Y`, which over-reports on real phones while being exact in Studio, so the ghosts came out bigger than the word: on mobile, STORMCALLER and STAR SIGHT (the two styles that copy the whole word, for the specular rake and the storm flash) drew an oversized duplicate of the title behind the real one — the same artefact that hit the Collection TITLES rows, and the same conclusion, *stop measuring*. An explicit size is **exact** here rather than an approximation of what `TextScaled` would have picked, because this plate is sized in **pixels** (`340×82 * uiScale`) not studs, so its local layout is identical at every camera distance. `TextSize = clamp(floor(0.28 * plateHeight) - 1, 6, 16)` reproduces what was drawn before on desktop (16px, where the band binds) and tracks the plate down from there, so a phone now lands below the 15px it drew when the mobile scale was a flat 0.7; width is nowhere near binding (widest of all 33 catalog labels is 129px in a 333px band on desktop, and it shrinks with the band). Note the **stats card's** title label is `TextScaled` but *not* in a BillboardGui, so it takes the engine-sized-ghost path and needs no equivalent change; the overhead health-bar nameplate is stud-sized and **cannot** take this fix, since its text genuinely has to rescale with camera distance
5. `setupVictoryShowcaseGui(stats, revealDelay, countdownSeconds, token)` runs the whole of step 2: at `revealDelay` it populates and slides in the stats canvas with the full per-player kill/death/XP breakdown, and fades up "RETURN TO BOUNTY" and the countdown on the same beat. The local player's stat card is a name-sorted vertical `UIGridLayout` of stat rows (`1KillFrame`..`8AccuracyFrame`) populated via `setExactStatFrameNumber`; a `9RankPointsFrame` row (separated by an empty `9GapSpacer` row) shows that round's Rank Points gain from the payload's `rankPointsEarned` field (awarded by RankedService via [[RoundController]]). `populateVictoryCanvas` also calls `updateVictoryPortrait`, which replaces the statically-baked Dust rig inside `MatchResultFrame.InnerFrame.PlayerInfoFrame.PlayerHunterFrame.ViewportFrame.WorldModel` with a clone of the local player's actual equipped hunter + skin — resolved from the same `stats` entry's `character` selection (`ReplicatedStorage.Hunters.<category>.Character.<subfolder>.<modelName>`) that drives the podium dummies, so both summary scenes show the hunter the player finished as, preserving the baked rig's pivot for camera framing and playing a looped idle animation. The swapped-in clone is anchored through the same `setupDummyPhysics` helper the podium dummies use — root anchored, every other part free. Anchoring every part (what this did before) pins them in world space, and the joint transforms the idle writes can never move an anchored part, so the portrait stood frozen for any hunter/skin other than the baked Dust rig
5a. The local player's stat card also shows their equipped cosmetic title in a `LocalPlayerTitleLabel` TextLabel inside `PlayerInfoFrame` — read from the local player's replicated `EquippedTitle` / `EquippedTitleId` attributes (published by the server's TitleDisplayService for their match hunter) and rendered through [[TitleRenderer]] in its cosmetic style; shown when non-empty, cleared and hidden when the player has none equipped

5b. **Every fade path that forces a transparency routes through `titleFxAlpha` / `titleFxBase`**, which read the renderer's `TitleFXBase` attribute — `setBillboardTransparency`, `tweenBillboardIn` and the match-summary canvas reveal, for both `UIStroke` and `TextLabel`. A title's semi-transparent halo therefore fades to its authored alpha rather than being tweened to fully opaque along with the ordinary labels. The TextLabel half matters more than it looks: the halo is built from ghost labels sitting at `TextTransparency = 1` whose UIStroke is the only visible part, so a fade that drove every TextLabel to 0 would paint a solid duplicate of the title over the real one. A title effect's `TextStrokeTransparency` is also left alone, since a ghost wants no black outline of its own
6. The "RETURN TO BOUNTY" button fires `LeaveToLobbyRequest` (same remote the settings-panel leave button uses). **It and the settings panel's leave button are now the only ways out of Redwater** — nobody is auto-teleported to Bounty any more, because [[RoundController]] loops rounds instead of calling `closeServer` at the end of one. Both remotes are handled by [[LeaveToLobbyService]] and neither needed changing
6a. **The button is deliberately undersized — it is the opt-out, not the thing to press.** Rebuilt on 2026-08-16: `ReturnBackground` is `{0.44, 0.364}` (X and Y differ — the width is what "RETURN TO BOUNTY" on one line needs, the height is the button's own proportion), pinned to the **top middle** of the screen, and carrying **no touch-target floor at all**
6a-i. **The `TouchFloor` `UISizeConstraint` and `MIN_TOUCH_TARGET_PX = 44` were DELETED, instance and code.** `UI_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §4.6 is explicit that there is no blanket 44x44 floor in this game and that adding one is not a fix — measured across every `GuiButton` in Bounty at 800x360, 31 of 154 reach 44x44 and 123 do not, all shipped and played on phones without a mis-tap problem. The floor here was written 2026-08-07, a week before that sweep, and then outlived it while still citing §4.6 as its authority. All it did in the end was hold this button at 44px tall on exactly the devices it was being shrunk for, growing an invisible canvas around it to do so. What §4.6 does ask for on a **leave** control is room, since the cost of a mis-tap is asymmetric; that is bought here with placement and spacing, not a square minimum. If it ever needs a bigger tap area than its painted size, §4.6 names the idioms: an invisible hitbox sibling, or floor the container — never a `MinSize` on the button
6a-ii. **Top-edge pinning copies `ReworkedSettingsGui.SettingsButton` / `StatMenuGui.MobileStatsButton`** — `AnchorPoint = (·, 0)` with `Position.Y = {0, 10}`, a flat 10px offset from the physical top rather than a scale. `CanvasReturnToBounty` is anchored `(0.5, 0)` at `{0.5, 0}, {0, 10}` and `ReturnBackground` is top-anchored inside it at `{0.5, 0}, {0, 0}`, so the button's top edge **is** the canvas's top edge and the canvas cannot push it down. Verified at 10px under both the desktop and the 0.9 mobile scale: a `UIScale` inside the canvas scales its size but not its `Position` offset, which is what makes the 10 stable the way the gear's is. `VictoryShowcase` is `ScreenInsets = DeviceSafeInsets`, so y=0 is the physical top (minus a notch), not the bottom of the Roblox topbar — 10px puts the button clear of the topbar's controls, which sit top-LEFT
6a-iii. **The label is `TextScaled = false`, `TextWrapped = false`, `TextSize = 100`, size from its `ResponsiveTextSize` cap** — the same construction as the countdown and for the same coupling reason (see 0c-i). The cap is computed from the label's own width over `RETURN_TEXT_WIDTH_RATIO` (6.2 — the copy measures 5.14-5.23px per px of size, plus ~15% padding so the text lands near 80% of the label rather than against its border), clamped to `RETURN_TEXT_MAX_PX` (40). The **width is derived from the authored fractions, not read off `AbsoluteSize`**, which is a frame stale at that point because the `UIScale` was assigned on the line above — reading it live would size round 1's text against round 0's layout. NaN is rejected with `fitted == fitted`, since `math.clamp` passes NaN straight through to the property. The ceiling must not bind at ordinary desktop sizes or it becomes the thing making PC text small: at 1080p the derived value is 30 (81% fill) where a stale 26 gave 71%. Resolved sizes: 9px iPhone 7, 12px iPhone 14, 15px iPad, 16px windowed, 30px at 1080p
7. **(removed)** There is no longer a third beat spawning the local player's own dummy at `PlayerLocal` and tweening the camera to face it — see 0 and 0b. The camera stays on the podium for the whole of step 2
8. `"Clear"` tears down all dummies, GUIs, tweens, and connections, and restores the normal camera. Fired every round by [[RoundController]]'s loop tail, so it is now a between-rounds reset rather than a shutdown-only teardown
9. Unlocks the mouse for the entire sequence (`UserInputService.MouseBehavior = Default`)

## Team modes

A plate above the podium carries the TEAM result, riding the same `Start` payload as everything else
here. The podium below it is untouched and still ranks every fighter individually.

It reads **YOUR TEAM WINS** / **ENEMY TEAM WINS** rather than naming a colour: the plate is already
drawn in the viewer's own colours ([[TeamView]] -- your side blue, theirs red), so naming the side
spares the player decoding a colour in the second the banner is up. A draw is a first-class outcome
and prints `DRAW  41 - 41` on the neutral panel colour, never an empty-name win line.

A viewer with no stamp of their own -- someone who joined during the intermission -- has no "your"
to be told about and gets the absolute BLUE / RED names instead.
