# TutorialClient

Drives the authored `TutorialGui` during the onboarding lesson; inert on every normal server (gated on the `workspace.TutorialMode` attribute).

## What it does

1. Waits for `workspace.TutorialMode`: `true` enables `TutorialGui` and starts listening; an explicit `false` exits forever; unresolved waits on the attribute-changed signal. **`TutorialGui` is authored `Enabled = false` and this is the only script that may turn it on.** The GUI ships in every Redwater server because the lesson and a real match are the same place, so an `Enabled` left true in the Explorer puts EXIT TRAINING and the intro card over ordinary gameplay — which is exactly what happened between the card being authored and 2026-08-12. RoundClientController's sweep cannot catch it either: `TutorialGui` is in its `SWEEP_EXEMPT_GUI_NAMES`, and deliberately so (both halves of the sweep get it wrong; the note there explains why).
2. Renders each `TutorialStep` payload into the banner: per-platform copy (the server ships only ids and timing) and the bounty step's 3s lead-in text swap. **The `n / 10` step counter was removed outright on 2026-08-10**, on both platforms — `payload.index` and `payload.total` still arrive from the director and nothing reads them, The authored label survived hidden and blank and was **repurposed on 2026-09-09 as the Live Fire countdown, renamed `CountdownLabel`** — an already-positioned, already-styled label rather than a new one typed from scratch (its `TextTransparency` was authored at 1, which is exactly the invisible-but-correct-looking trap §1.1 of the style guide is about). It was **reparented out of the banner onto the gui root** the same day and now sits under EXIT TRAINING; `payload.index` and `payload.total` still arrive from the director and still nothing reads them.

   Its rect is derived, never authored: `layoutCountdown` places it from the button's LIVE height, because that button is 44 on PC and `TOUCH_`/`TABLET_EXIT_HEIGHT` on touch, so any fixed offset lands on top of it on one of the three. **The same function owns the banner's touch position**, and that is the point — the number goes between the button and the banner, so the two rects cannot drift apart. On touch the banner is otherwise pinned `TOUCH_BANNER_GAP` (16) under the button, which is not room for a number and is a drop tuned to put the line in the sightline around the crosshair; rather than retune it for all eight steps, `countdownActive` makes the banner take the reservation for the life of the countdown only, and `applyTouchLayout` reads the same flag so a viewport change mid-countdown re-applies the shift instead of quietly undoing it. PC needs none of this — its banner keeps the authored `{0.175, 68}`, 152px down even on a 640×480 window. Verified at 1080p, 640×480 windowed and a Galaxy A06 (16px clear of the banner on the phone, the tightest of the three).

   The line reads **`TIME LEFT: 14`**, not a bare number: under EXIT TRAINING a lone figure reads as a score, or as a count of something still to do, rather than as the fight running out. That makes the box **WIDTH-bound** at every size — 13 glyphs, so a taller box buys nothing (style guide §3.3) and the width is what sets the type. Three authored pairs rather than one, because the row sits under a button that is 44 tall on PC and `TOUCH_`/`TABLET_EXIT_HEIGHT` on touch: **300×44 PC, 280×38 tablet, 220×26 phone**. The phone pair matches `TOUCH_STEP_LABEL_HEIGHT` on purpose — a countdown taller than the instruction it sits above reads as the headline. Its removal also retired the touch renumbering, which had shifted every step past the Alt step down one and shown a total of 9; that had already stopped being correct when the Alt step started running on both platforms. **The swap is timed from the pop, not from the server's send, and that is what makes it land on the same instant as the bounty bot walking out of cover** — the pop is `STEP_BEAT` after the send, so the swap fires at `STEP_BEAT + leadIn`, which is exactly what the director's `afterBeat(leadIn)` waits before spawning. One event, two halves; changing the reading time moves both. Copy pattern, settled 2026-08-09: **only the key instruction is in caps — `PRESS X`, `HOLD Y`, `LEFT CLICK TO FIRE` — and everything else is sentence case**, the explanation after the dash included ("PRESS Q while moving — dash and reload in one move", "Take the bots down", "Fight! The bots shoot back now"). It went through a round of ACTION-IN-CAPS first, which put whole lines in capitals and left the caps emphasising nothing; the point of them is that a player scanning the banner mid-fight catches the key and reads the rest later. PC lines name the key as a verb ("PRESS F", "HOLD ALT") rather than as a bare letter, and the line drops any tail that teaches nothing — the Ult line lost the name "Dead Eye", the one word in it a new player cannot act on. The Dash line names the movement requirement on both platforms because `RevolverDash.TryDash` bails under `MoveDirection` 0.05 — a standing Q press never reaches the remote the step gates on, so the step used to time out on a player pressing the right key. The Ult line names the **skull**: the ultimate marks a target only after the lock-in, when HighNoonTargetUI swaps the ring for `DustUltimateTarget`'s SkullMark, and the trigger executes what is marked — players were firing on the ring.
2a. **A completed step is a three-beat sequence, not a swap** (playtest 2026-08-09, second pass). On an advance-by-action the cue fires over the line that is still on screen — the authored `StepCompleteSound` (`ObjectiveComplete2`, SFX SoundGroup set explicitly since the staged asset carried none), a Back-out pulse on the banner's authored `FlashScale`, the warm text flash — then the finished line **fades out** (0.35s, taking the key art with it) so the banner is empty for the rest of `STEP_BEAT` (2.0s), then the next instruction pops in on its authored `PopScale` with stroke and shadow fading in alongside the glyphs. Fading the old line out is what makes it read as one step ending and another starting rather than one instruction mutating into the next. **TutorialDirector carries the same constant and adds it to every step deadline**, because a player cannot act on a line that is not on screen yet; the two must move together. `presentStep` carries everything a step turns on, its client gate included, so what the banner asks and what the script watches start together; the server's own timeout is 15s or more, so the beat costs a step nothing. **`Complete` is handled on its own and gets neither the cue nor the beat** — it clears the *banner* at once and the player is teleported, and it deliberately **leaves the outro card standing**. `presentStep` handles it **before** its own `hideWelcome()` call rather than after, because that call hides both notice labels and would blank the thank-you at the exact moment the player is teleported, which is the one frame the card exists to fill. It used to take the cue and the beat, which was right while it arrived in the same breath as the last step passing; it no longer does — the outro card is already on screen and has already played the cue, so firing the chime and the green vignette again would announce a second achievement that never happened, and holding the banner a `STEP_BEAT` first would only delay the teleport. Step 1 and a `"__sync"` resend appear at once — nothing was completed, and a reconnecting client must not stare at an empty banner. Every cue instance is read with `FindFirstChild`: missing means a quieter advance, never an error.
2b. **The welcome card** (2026-08-09): the director sends one `Welcome` payload before step 1 — no index, no gate, no progress count — and the client pops it in over 0.45s, holds it, and fades it over 0.6s. The client owns the whole animation; the server just holds the session for `WELCOME_HOLD` before firing step 1, so the lesson does not open mid-instruction on a player still working out where they are. The card is `currentPayload` like any other, so a `"__sync"` pull during it re-shows the card rather than an empty screen — which is exactly why every fade step is gated on a **token** and `presentStep` calls `hideWelcome` outright: the card can be delivered twice (push + pull), and a client that booted two seconds in started its three-second animation two seconds late and walked the card straight over step 1.
2b-i. **What the card IS changed on 2026-08-12: the authored `CompletionGui` group, not the single `WelcomeLabel` line.** It is a logo (`BountyLogo`), a gold rule (`GoldBackgroundLine`) and one sentence of copy (`Info` — "The tutorial will show you the game mechanics"), and it is the first thing a player sees of the lesson. `showIntroCard` drives it; `WelcomeLabel` ("WELCOME TO BOUNTY") is demoted to the fallback the branch takes only when a place copy has no card, and its authored text is still captured at boot into `WELCOME_TEXT` for that path.
   - **The frame's name is a leftover and means nothing.** Nothing shows `CompletionGui` at the end of the lesson — `OutroLabel` still owns that. Its hidden `Header` child is a dead duplicate reading "WELCOME TO BOUNTY", left in place and rendering nothing.
   - **The three pieces fade on one tween each rather than through a parent transparency.** A Frame has no group transparency to animate, and giving the card a `CanvasGroup` for one fade would flatten the logo into its own compositing pass for the rest of the lesson. Each entry records the property it fades on (`ImageTransparency` / `BackgroundTransparency` / `TextTransparency`) and its authored rest value, read off the instance so a re-show restores exactly what the Explorer holds. An added `PopScale` gives it the same 0.86 → 1 Back-out pop the notice labels use.
   - **`Info`'s `UIShadow` is in that fade, and it has to be.** A `UIShadow` plates the whole box and ignores `TextTransparency` (the same trap item 2c documents for the banner), so left out of the tween it arrives a frame early as a bare dark bar and then never leaves.
   - **The hold is 3.4s** — 2.4 until 2026-08-12, then 4.4 until 2026-09-09. 2.4 was tuned for the three words the welcome used to be; 4.4 for the card that replaced it, a logo, a gold rule and a full sentence. **3.4 since 2026-09-09** (owner): that sentence is one line and does not need four and a half seconds, and every second on this card is a second before a new player touches anything. **`TutorialDirector`'s own `WELCOME_HOLD` moved with it every time — 6 → 5 in this pass — and it is the one that actually gates step 1**: the server firing while the card is up does not fade it, it *cuts* it, because `presentStep` calls `hideWelcome` first thing. Keep the server's above pop + hold + fade, now 4.45s against 5.
   - **The ScreenGui is authored `Enabled = false`** and boot also hides the card outright, for the reason the banner is blanked: what the Explorer holds is what somebody sees while authoring it. Leaving `Enabled` true is what put EXIT TRAINING and this card on screen through every normal Redwater match — see item 1.
2d. **The outro card** — one, since 2026-08-12. After the last step the director sends `{ id = "Outro", key = "Thanks" }` and the client pops it on the authored **`OutroLabel`**, using the same pop/hold/fade the welcome card uses. There were two: a thanks card and a `FreeRoam` card naming how long the canyon stayed open afterwards. Free roam is gone, so that card, its `seconds` field and the `formatDuration` helper went with it — a card promising time the server does not give is worse than no card. `showWelcome` became `showNotice(rig, text, hold)` for the pair, and the rig — PopScale, UIStroke, UIShadow and their rest transparencies — is resolved per label by `noticeRig` rather than being five module locals bound to the welcome; both outlive the cut, because the welcome still needs them. Three things are load-bearing. The **authored welcome text is captured at boot** into `WELCOME_TEXT`. The **EXIT TRAINING button deliberately stays up** — the player is not teleported for another 5.0s and leaving early has to keep working — so only `Complete` takes it down. And the **fade token is shared across both labels**: a card arriving has to cancel whatever the previous one still has queued, and it hides the other label outright rather than fading it, since both are anchored on the same point and would otherwise overlap. The card gets the ordinary completion cue, because finishing the lesson is something the player did. `delay` on the payload is the beat that lets the finished instruction line clear the screen first, and a `"__sync"` resend deliberately carries `key` but not `delay` — it has nothing to clear.

2f. **The outro's hold is 9.1s and it is meant to OUTLIVE the run** (2026-08-12). Pop 0.3 + 9.1 + fade 0.6 is 10s on screen, while TutorialDirector teleports at `STEP_BEAT + OUTRO_HOLD` = **5.0s** (7.4 until 2026-09-09, when the server's hold was cut 5.4 → 3.0 because the card was sitting there five seconds after it had been read) — so the card is never seen to fade, because the teleport takes it away first. **This hold did not move with it**, and deliberately: it is a ceiling, not a match, so cutting the server's wait simply widens the margin. **The inversion is the point and the two constants must not be matched.** The old two-card version needed the server's gap to *cover* this animation so the second card landed on an empty screen; this one needs the animation to *outlast* the server's wait, because a card that finishes first leaves an empty canyon as the last thing the tutorial ever shows. Keep this hold above the server's, not equal to it.
2e. **`OutroLabel` is the one label in this GUI that is NOT `TextScaled`**, and that is the whole design of it: **the card is a single line, on every device, always.**
   - **A second authored label rather than resizing `WelcomeLabel`:** the welcome has to keep the size it has always had, and the outro needs different text behaviour entirely — see below. Authoring it keeps the geometry in the Explorer instead of computing it here.
   - **`TextScaled` cannot be told not to wrap.** It turns `TextWrapped` on itself, and clearing `TextWrapped` clears `TextScaled` with it (the banner note in item 11 is the other half of the same trap). So under `TextScaled` a long line breaks in two whenever the box is not wide enough — and a *short* line does not, which, while there were two cards, also rendered them at different sizes, since a one-row line is bound by the box **width** and a two-row one by its **height** (measured 37px against 26px on a phone). One root cause, two symptoms. A first pass tried to hide it by forcing the thanks card onto two rows so both were height-bound; that matched them at the cost of both cards wrapping, which is worse and was rejected on sight.
   - **So: `TextScaled = false`, `TextWrapped = false`, `AutomaticSize.X`, and an explicit `TextSize` solved from the viewport** (`applyOutroTextSize`). The line is budgeted at `OUTRO_LINE_FRACTION` (0.62) of the screen width, with a floor of 14 so a narrow window gets a small line rather than a broken one.
   - **`OUTRO_CHAR_WIDTH` is measured, and it was RE-measured on 2026-08-12 — 0.255 → 0.285.** The old value was correct for the free-roam line and wrong for the one that ships: that line was mixed case, capitals are wider, and the all-caps "THANK YOU FOR PLAYING THE TUTORIAL" runs ~12% past it, at 0.284 px per character per point read off `AbsoluteSize` on a simulated iPad at 44pt. **Change the copy to mixed case and this wants re-measuring**, or the budget is guarding a width the card does not have. It is measured against `#text`, the BYTE length, so it must keep being used against `#text` — a multi-byte glyph like an em-dash costs three and the constant absorbs that.
   - **The CEILING is bucketed per device, and it is the number that actually renders** (2026-08-12). With a 34-byte line the budget solves to `width × 0.051`, which clears every ceiling at any viewport wider than ~860 — so **every phone, tablet and monitor lands on its ceiling, never on the budget**, and the budget's remaining job is only the guarantee that the line cannot wrap or overflow. A single ceiling of 30 was therefore rendering identical text everywhere; and since Roblox viewport units are points and a phone and a tablet are within a unit an inch of each other (~131 against ~132), identical text meant the same *physical* size on a 6in phone as on a 13in iPad — heavy and crowding on the phone, lost in the middle of the tablet. Now `OUTRO_TEXT_MAX_PHONE` 24 / `_TABLET` 44 / `_PC` 30, chosen by `outroTextMax()` off the file's existing `TABLET_MIN_SHORT_EDGE` (600) short-edge test, so the game keeps one definition of "tablet". Measured plates: 30-44% of viewport width on phones, 39-55% on tablets, 19-57% on PC — the widest anywhere is 57% against the 62% budget, so nothing wraps.
   - **`AutomaticSize.X` hugs the line, so the plate needs authored `UIPadding` or it ends flush with the first and last glyph.** The step label never has this problem because its box is the width of the banner and its text is centred inside — the margin is a side effect of the plate being far wider than the line. An auto-sized box has no such slack, and the first cut of this shipped a shadow cropped exactly to the text, which read as a bug. The padding is `OUTRO_PAD_RATIO` (1.2) × the solved text size, re-applied with it, so the card keeps its proportions at every size. It is also counted **inside** the width budget rather than added after: the card the player sees is the plate, so it is the plate that has to fit the fraction of the screen.
   - **The bucket is read at the moment the card is shown, and on the SHORT edge so it is rotation-proof.** It needs none of `applyTouchLayout`'s re-apply-on-viewport-change machinery, because `ViewportSize`'s (1, 1) opening frames are minutes gone by the time a run reaches the outro — which is why there is still deliberately no outro entry in that function even though the card is bucketed now.
   - **The outro holds 9.1s against the welcome's 2.4** — see item 2f, which is where that number comes from and why it is not the server's.
2c. **The banner starts BLANK, not empty.** A `UIShadow` is a plate the size of its box and ignores the text entirely, and a `UIStroke` on a TextLabel is not governed by `TextTransparency` either — so an empty StepLabel still drew a dark bar across the screen (under the welcome card), and the green ✓ left an outline of itself sitting in the banner permanently. Boot therefore sets the text, its stroke and the shadow to fully transparent, and `setStepText` fades them back in with the first line. **The ✓ (`ConfirmTick`) was deleted outright on 2026-08-09** — the chime, the banner pulse and the warm flash already read as "done", and a fourth element in a 104px banner that also holds the instruction, the count and the keycap was one glyph too many.
3. Fires `"__sync"` up `TutorialProgress` the moment its `TutorialStep` listener connects (added 2026-08-09): the server's step-1 push fires within a second of the session starting, almost always before this script's WaitForChild chain finishes, and a push with no listener is dropped — the lesson opened to a blank screen until step 2's push arrived. The server answers with the current step payload.
4. Reports client-observed gates over `TutorialProgress` — **none since 2026-09-09**, three until 2026-08-16 and two until the panel steps were cut. `report`, `reportedSteps` and the remote are deliberately left standing, matching the director's now-empty `CLIENT_GATED`: an empty gate set refuses every report on its own, and a future step that has to watch something only this side can see gets both halves back rather than rebuilt. The three that existed:
   - **Move is gone.** It was both halves of getting around — a cumulative 60-degree camera sweep on RenderStepped AND sprint actually activating, observed as HunterMovement's `buff:Sprint` layer through `HunterMovement.Explain(character)`. Step 1 is now the walk to checkpoint 1 and the server polls the player's position against the pad, so `startMoveGate` and this script's whole require of `HunterMovement` went with it. Sprint survives in the copy, taught on the way rather than demanded.
   - **FreeMouse is gone** (2026-09-09). It watched `ReworkedSettingsGui.Backdrop` `Visible` flipping true — the panel opening, not the Alt press, since Alt was only the means — and went with its step.
   - **Stats is gone** (2026-09-09), on the same signal for the same reason: `StatMenuGui.Backdrop` `Visible` flipping true, the panel's real state rather than the TAB keypress or the button's `Activated`.
5. Step 3's touch copy falls back to the "Tap the red button to fire" variant when the `AutoFireEnabled` player attribute is explicitly `false` (absent means ON). Both touch variants and the PC line name the **reload** as well as the trigger ("LEFT CLICK to fire — PRESS R to reload"): the cylinder holds six and does not refill itself, so a player who empties it into the dummies and stands there clicking is the likeliest way to fail a step that only asks for three shots.
5a. **`findHudTile` matches the HUD on STRUCTURE, via `HunterIntroTags.findHosts`** — a top-level *enabled* ScreenGui whose Canvas holds both an AbilityFrame and an UltimateFrame. It used to match the names `ScreenGui` / `HunterGuiMobile`, which are the names of the **templates** inside `StarterGui.ReworkedHunterGui`; the live HUD is a clone named per hunter (`TemplateGameGui` for Dust), and StarterGui's own copy of the design folder sits inside a Folder rather than at PlayerGui's top level. So the lookup returned nil every time and steps 4/5/7 ran with no tile cue at all — never noticed until the breathe was added on top of it and also failed to appear (2026-08-09).
6. The rim glow (decision 20): clones `ReplicatedStorage.TutorialGlow` (24 UIShadows) onto the current step's target — SPRINT button (touch, step 2), the Q/E/F ability tiles in the live HUD clone (steps 4/5/7, both platforms), STATS button (touch, step 10). Global-ZIndexBehavior hosts get the glow parented inside at one ZIndex below plus an opaque-while-lit body (restored on clear — the UltimateReadyFX translucency trap); tiles take the authored ReadyGlow slot pattern (child at ZIndex 0). A missing target is a step without a cue, never an error.
6a. **The breathe** (2026-08-09): on the Dash / Fan / Ult steps the whole tile the step is asking for pulses 1 → 1.22 → 1 on a 0.45s reversing tween, on top of the rim glow. The ability steps deliberately raise **no keycap**: they carried `Q`/`E`/`F` caps for one round and the tile is the better teacher — it is where the ability lives, it is what goes on cooldown, and on touch it is the button being pressed, while a cap in the banner taught the key and pointed nowhere. It drives the tile's authored **`BumpScale`** — the same UIScale HunterHudBump uses, because only one UIScale per GuiObject takes effect and the whole tile is what has to move. It breathed the `Ability` icon alone first, on its own UIScale one level down (which composes cleanly and never touches HunterHudBump), and that read as the picture moving inside a static frame rather than the button pulsing. The shared-property overlap lasts a fraction of a second, on the press that ends the step: the bump's peak is a multiplier on the resting scale it read at construction, and this always hands the scale back at 1. `clearGlow` clears it, so `attachBreathe` is called AFTER `attachGlow` on the same tile.
6c. **On the ult step the tile does not light until the charge is actually full** (2026-08-16). The glow and the breathe are the loudest thing on the HUD and they were arriving with the step line, a second or more before the director's `ChargeService:Set` landed — so the cue said "press F" while pressing F did nothing. `lightTile` is therefore deferred behind Dust's own `HighNoonChargeUpdate` and fires the first time `charge >= maxCharge`; every other step lights immediately. Two things about the wiring: the remote is resolved **lazily with `FindFirstChild` at use time**, never a top-level `WaitForChild`, because `Hunters.Dust.Remotes` holds only HighNoonRequest/Visual/Shot in the Explorer and the rest are created at server boot by `RevolverRemoteSetup` from Dust's manifest — a boot-time wait would resolve nothing in edit mode and yield the whole cue behind it live. And `lightTile` carries a `tileLit` guard because **`attachBreathe` is additive**: a second breathe on one `BumpScale` cancels the first's tween, whose Cancelled handler re-arms both, and the two ping-pong forever (`attachGlow` already refuses a re-light on its own).
6b. **On touch the combat buttons the step is asking for expand, not just glow** (`cueMobileButton`, 2026-08-10): SPRINT on step 2, FIRE and RELOAD on step 3, STATS on step 10, and FIRE again on the ult step. Each takes the rim glow plus a breathe on its own authored `BreatheScale` — on the **button**, not on the `Icon` inside it, which was tried first and read as the picture moving inside a static frame rather than the button pulsing.
   - **The button is re-anchored to its centre for the cue** and handed back afterwards. The cluster buttons are authored at anchor `(1,1)`, and a `UIScale` pivots at the anchor point, so SPRINT grew up-and-left only — into the ability row rather than outward like the HUD tiles do. The swap is exact rather than approximate: the anchor point is the one point a scale leaves fixed, so the resting rect is recoverable to 0.000px on restore.
   - Growing the whole button is also what forced `restingRect` / `restingSize` into HunterMobileSprintButton and HunterMobileClusterLayout. Both measure `AbsoluteSize`, which includes the `UIScale`, so the pulsing button dragged the ability row and the cluster around with it. That is fixed on the layout side rather than by shrinking the cue.
   - The ult step arms FIRE only **after** the ultimate actually starts, by listening for Dust's `HighNoonVisual` `"Start"` — a player has to press F, hold aim until the skulls appear and *then* fire, and lighting the trigger up front teaches the wrong order.
7. **The key art row** (`setKeyCap`, PC only — `icons` is nil under `isTouch`). A row of rendered keycap images sits above the line: `Move` shows the WASD cluster + SHIFT, `Shoot` MOUSE1 + R, `Dash`/`Fan`/`Ult` Q/E/F. (ALT and TAB left with the panel steps on 2026-09-09.) Two icons at most, and the two-icon steps are the two whose line names two keys — the reload is taught beside the trigger because an empty cylinder with no auto-reload is the likeliest way to stall a step that asks for three shots, and the cluster sits before SHIFT in the order the sentence reads them. Each fades in and pulses on its own `PulseScale`; on the three ability steps it uses the HUD tile's own curve and peak (`BREATHE_INFO`, 1.22) instead of the calmer cap pulse, and starts in the same frame, so the key in the banner and the tile it points at move as one thing.
7b. **Icon sizes are measured, never eyeballed.** Every asset is a glyph floating in a much larger transparent canvas and the canvases agree on neither aspect nor how much of themselves the glyph fills (Q fills 63% of its height, TAB 49%), so a common box renders TAB visibly smaller than Q while being nominally identical. Each entry carries an `aspect` and a `fill` read off `EditableImage:ReadPixelsBuffer`, and the loader solves `height = ICON_GLYPH_PX / fill` once — retuning the whole set is one number (40, up from 16 → 28 across 2026-08-10) rather than eight pixel pairs that can disagree.
   - **WASD is the one entry whose `fill` is not the whole glyph, and it cannot be.** It is a two-row cluster (measured bands 313px and 320px in a 1024 canvas), so filling on the full 638px glyph would render the *cluster* at 40px and each of its keys at 20 — half the height of the SHIFT key beside it, which is the exact mismatch the measured-fill scheme exists to prevent. The unit of visual weight is a **key**, not an asset, so its fill is one key row (0.3125) and the box comes out 128x128.
   - ALT turned out to be drawn on the same canvas as TAB **to the pixel** (1023x682, exactly 3:2). TAB's recorded aspect was corrected 1.4999 → 1.5000 in the same pass so two identical assets cannot render a pixel apart.
7c. **The row grows for the cluster and shrinks for short windows.** The authored `KeyIconRow` (300x84, centred 45px above the banner) is sized for single keys — TAB and ALT are the tallest that fit, at 81. A 128 cluster does not, so the row grows **upward from its authored bottom edge**, and the branch is skipped entirely at or below 84, which is what keeps every single-key step rendering exactly where it always did. It then scales back down when the viewport is too short to hold it: 128 needs a 703px-tall window to clear EXIT TRAINING while the rest of the lesson works down to ~450, so without the clamp the cluster would sit on the exit button for anyone not full screen. The whole row scales together (cluster and SHIFT keep per-key parity on the way down) and the floor is the authored 84.
7d. **`KEYCAPS`, the text-cap table, is now empty.** ALT was the last entry and was text only because no ALT art existed; it moved into `ICONS` on 2026-08-10. The table and its branch are kept rather than deleted because they are the escape hatch, not dead weight — the icon path needs an `ICON_ASSETS` entry to draw anything, so a step naming a key with no art would otherwise show an empty slot. A text cap keeps its verb (`HOLD ALT`, never a bare `ALT`): a lone glyph beside a sentence reads as part of the sentence rather than as the key to press.
7a. **Two lines retire themselves** (`AUTO_FADE_AFTER` / `scheduleAutoFade`). A step listed there fades its own copy and key art out on the ordinary `fadeOutStepText` after a hold, measured from the moment **that step's own line pops** — after the lead-in, where there is one — not from the step starting. Both entries are long steps with nothing gated on the banner, which is what makes the line safe to take away: they are finished by shooting something, not by reading.
   - `LiveFire` (3.0s) — the last step is a fight, not an instruction, and the line was sitting over the shooting for the whole thing. This is the case the mechanism was built for. It briefly mattered more, while the step ended on a kill and the line had to be gone before that kill could happen; the step is a plain 30-second stopwatch again since 2026-08-12, so the fade is back to being about the sightline rather than about timing.
   - `Bounty` (4.0s, up from 3.0 on request) — the bounty marker is a world billboard over the target's head and the banner is a bar across the top; on a phone the two sit at the same height and the bar cuts the marker in half, which is exactly what the line is telling the player to look at. Three seconds cleared the marker but was not quite enough reading time first.
   Cancelled in `onStep` alongside the lead-in thread, and re-checked against `currentStepId` when it fires, so a step the player finished early — whose line has already faded and been replaced — cannot take the *next* step's line down with it.
8. Closes the settings and stats panels **when the lesson ends**, if either backdrop is still visible (`_G.CloseSettingsMenu`, a close SettingsController exports for this, and `_G.ToggleStatMenu` used as a setter). Until 2026-09-09 this was a step-to-step handoff — settings into step 10, stats into step 11 — because those steps opened the panels; no step opens either one now, but both stay reachable through the run from live HUD, and what the close still prevents is a dimmed panel left standing underneath the outro card. Both now run on Complete. Neither goes near `Backdrop.Visible` directly — writing it would leave the owning script's `isOpen` and `DisplayOrder` believing the panel is still up.
8a. **Points the camera on every lesson spawn** (`faceLessonSpawn`, added 2026-08-09, generalised to the bowl's area pads 2026-08-16). Each pad's authored `Stand` — resolved through `MapConfig.Instances.Tutorial` → `Areas` — carries the direction the lesson wants when placed there, and SpawnService writes the Stand's whole CFrame onto the character, rotation included. **That rotation never reaches the player:** under `LockFirstPerson` the PlayerModule camera owns the yaw and turns the character to match the *camera* every frame, so the authored facing is gone by the first render and the player is left looking wherever the camera was pointing when it attached — the world origin, where a Redwater character is loaded before it is teleported (the place has no SpawnLocation). The camera is a client object, so this is the only side that can fix it. It waits for the character to arrive within 12 studs of ANY Stand (polling, 3s cap) before writing — which Stand is the server's business, since respawns follow the player's current area — then writes twice a frame apart, because the camera module derives each frame's rotation from the camera's *current* CFrame and discards a write that lands on its initialisation frame; and levels the pitch — a spawn facing wants the horizon. The Stands are re-resolved with `FindFirstChild` on every poll, never cached and never a long `WaitForChild`: the pads' contents **stream by position**, and the one the player was just placed on is always replicated because they are standing on it, while a `WaitForChild` timeout would suppress the infinite-yield warning without ending the wait. Bound to `CharacterAdded` as well as the first character: the lesson respawns instantly, so a death would otherwise leave the player facing wherever they fell.
8b. **The step payload's `face` field** (2026-08-16). The ult setup plants the player on the area-2 pad server-side and ships the exact look vector on the payload; `faceFromPayload` applies it on receipt — at the step boundary, before the beat, while the finished line is still clearing — through the same arrival-wait and twice-a-frame-apart write, but **keeps the pitch**: the firing line sits ~16 studs above the pad, and a levelled look would put all six targets above the crosshair. A `"__sync"` resend carries `face` too, so a reconnecting client standing on the pad still gets pointed.
8c. **`pin` holds the camera there** (2026-08-16). The ult step is a demonstration — planted, pointed at six targets, two buttons to press — so its payload sets `pin` alongside `face` and the client binds a RenderStep at `Camera + 1` that rewrites `CFrame` to `lookAt(position, position + direction)` every frame, rather than pointing once and letting the player look away. **The same write repeated is not the same thing as the one-shot**: `faceFromPayload` fights the PlayerModule for one frame and hands the yaw back, which is correct for a spawn facing and useless for a hold. Bound above `Enum.RenderPriority.Camera` because the module writes the camera at that priority and the last write in a frame wins; nothing in Dust's own client code writes `camera.CFrame`, so there is no third party in the fight. The movement half of the freeze is the server's (`HunterMovement` override) — this is only the look. Unbound on any payload without `pin`, and on teardown.
8d. **The route guide** (`startGuide` / `updateGuide`). A payload carrying `pad` draws a row of ground-seated chevrons from the player to that pad, and the client's whole rule is that — a payload with a pad has a route, whether it is a `Travel` push or step 1's own `arriveAt`. Two `Beam` draws per chevron off a down-pointing arrow texture, so **nothing physical is put in the world**: a `Beam` maps its texture's Y axis ALONG the beam, which is why each arrow's tail attachment is the one nearer the player — verified by rendering it rather than reasoned about, because backwards every arrow in the lesson points the player home. `TextureMode.Stretch` fits exactly one chevron per beam, so `GUIDE_WIDTH` and `GUIDE_LENGTH` (5 and 5) are the arrow's aspect ratio and pulling them apart smears it. Each chevron re-seats on a downward raycast only when it has drifted `GUIDE_RESEAT` (0.35 studs) — the row is anchored to the pad, so walking straight at it barely moves any of them, and it is strafing that rotates the line and re-seats the near end. `GUIDE_MAX` (24) is there so a runaway leg cannot flood the client, and the near chevron fades across `GUIDE_FADE` rather than blinking off as it crosses `GUIDE_START_GAP`.
   - **`via` makes it a polyline, and one leg needs it.** The straight line from checkpoint 1 to checkpoint 2 crosses a 17-stud step in the terrain at its midpoint (measured 13.0 → 30.9 between t=0.4 and t=0.5) and so points the arrows at a face nobody climbs, while the ramp the player actually takes runs ~34 studs east. `getViaPosition` resolves the server's waypoint NAME, `routeSegments` splits the route in two and `routePoint` walks the polyline; the waypoint drops out of the route once the player is past it on a plane test **and** further than `GUIDE_VIA_RADIUS` (10 studs) from it, so nothing points backwards after the corner. Only the CP1→CP2 leg has one — CP2→CP3 measured clean without it.
9. EXIT TRAINING: shows the cloned confirm dialog (`STAY` / `LEAVE`); LEAVE fires the existing `LeaveToLobbyRequest` remote — no second exit path. The button sits at the **top centre of the screen** since 2026-08-09, which makes it a sibling of the banner rather than a child, so the `Complete` branch has to hide it explicitly — it no longer goes away when the banner does.

10. **A completed step flashes the healing vignette** — the same green screen-edge pulse a health pack gives, via the shared [[HealingVignette]] module (2026-08-10). Fired from the step-complete cue alongside the chime and the banner flash. It is the module rather than a copy specifically so the two can overlap: picking up a health pack during a step is a real collision, and the module's token hands the frames to whichever fired last instead of letting two sequences fight.
11. **The touch layout is applied at runtime, and PC comes out of it untouched.** One `TutorialGui` serves both platforms; the structure stays authored and only a handful of rects move, all inside `if isTouch`.
    - The banner widens to 0.92 (from the authored 0.58) and the step label's box shrinks to 26px. **The fix is the box, not the text**: touch copy names buttons where PC names keys, so the line broke in two and `TextScaled` shrank both halves. `TextScaled` takes the largest font whose laid-out text fits, so a short box prices any two-line rendering at half height and the engine picks the single line — but only if the banner is wide enough for that line to exist. **Do not reach for `TextWrapped = false`**: Roblox couples the two, clearing it silently clears `TextScaled`, and the label drops to its authored `TextSize` of 8 with no warning.
    - The banner moves out of the sightline, tucked under EXIT TRAINING. Its top is **derived** — exit bottom plus a 16px gap — rather than pinned, so it follows the button when the button changes height instead of being crowded by it.
    - **Tablets take the authored sizes back** (short edge ≥ 600, the same split `TelemetryClient` uses to bucket the platform, so the game has one definition of "tablet"). 26px is a phone number: Roblox viewport units are points and a 13in iPad is ~132 units/inch against an A06's ~131, so the same box is the same *physical* text on a screen three times the size. Tablets get the authored 46px label and the authored 132x44 exit button; phones keep 26 and 104x32. 46 is a ceiling as much as a target — a 56px box lets the longest line wrap to two 28px lines on a phone, *smaller* than the 26px single line it renders today.
    - Re-applied on `ViewportSize` change rather than set once, because `ViewportSize` reads (1, 1) for the first frames of a client's life and a tablet measured in that window would classify as a phone for the whole lesson. Testing the **short** edge makes it rotation-proof.
11a. **The intro card is in that layout too, added 2026-08-12** — it is authored in viewport *fractions*, which is the same failure the step label had. 0.125 of a 1080p screen is a 135px card and a 39px line; 0.125 of a 360-tall phone is a 45px card and a 13px line, with the logo landing on top of EXIT TRAINING. Four things move, and PC keeps every authored value.
    - **The card takes a pixel HEIGHT** (84 phone / 128 tablet) and keeps a scale **width** (0.62 / 0.5). Height in pixels is what makes the copy legible; width in scale is what keeps the gold rule — authored at 1.2× the card — the same rule everywhere instead of a full-bleed line on a phone and a short dash on a tablet. Everything inside the card is authored in scale against it, so the whole composition follows from that one height.
    - **The copy's box is widened past its own parent** (1.5× the card on a phone, 1.4× on a tablet). At the card's own width the 44-character sentence does not fit, `TextScaled` wraps it to two lines and prices both at half size — the identical trap the banner hit, and the fix is the box, not the text.
    - **The logo shrinks 25% on phones only, with its BOTTOM pinned** to where it was authored (it is drawn sitting on the gold rule, and a logo re-centred on a smaller box floats off it). A card sized until the sentence is readable carries a logo at 40% of a phone's screen height — twice what the same card renders on PC. The authored rect is captured at boot, because this function re-runs on every viewport change and a shrink applied to the current size would compound. Tablets are exempt: at 128 the authored 1.6 already lands at ~20% of an iPad's height, which is what PC renders.
    - **The copy's shadow plate is pulled back to the RULE's width** (`syncCopyPlate`). The plate is the size of the *box*, and the box is deliberately over-wide, so untouched it runs to 93% of a phone's screen against the rule's 74% and the rule reads as floating inside a bar nobody drew. `UIShadow.Spread` measures its scale component against the element's own size, so this is one derived number and **no `AbsoluteSize` read** — which matters, because `AbsoluteSize` is NaN until the first layout pass and this runs well before one. The authored `Spread` (0.1) is the value the same formula produces on PC, so the Explorer shows what ships.
    - The card's Y is **derived from EXIT TRAINING**, like the banner's. It is measured to the top of the **logo**, not the top of the frame: the logo is anchored at its centre and sized 1.6 card-heights, so it reaches well above the box that positions it, and both anchor points are read rather than assumed so re-drawing the card in the Explorer needs no constant edited to match.
    - Verified by measurement and capture at windowed PC, Galaxy A06, iPhone 7, Galaxy Tab A8 and iPad Pro 13in — logo clears the button by 11px on all of them and the sentence stays on one line.
