# SettingsController

Populates and drives the reworked settings panel and owns its open/close, plus Redwater's Return-to-Bounty flow and the PC Alt-to-unlock-mouse mechanic. Single-script replacement for the legacy `SettingsGui` (which split this across `SettingsPanelClient` + `SettingsButtonClient`), mirroring Bounty's `ReworkedSettingsGui.Canvas.Background.Content` layout.

## What it does
1. **Open/close:** the `ReworkedSettingsGui` ScreenGui stays `Enabled`; the panel is shown/hidden by toggling `Canvas.Visible` + `Backdrop.Visible`. A top-right gear `SettingsButton` (44x44) toggles it; `Canvas.Background.CloseFrame.Button` closes it. `DisplayOrder` is raised to **100001** only while open and dropped to 0 when closed so the gear tucks under full-screen teleport / round overlays instead of poking through
1a. **The open value has to clear the death-time UI, not just the HUD.** It was 50 — enough for the gameplay HUD (StatMenu 20, PostGame 25, DailyQuest 40, BountyList 41, PingFPSGui 100) but *under* KillCamGui's 9500 and SwapHunterGui's 9600, so the panel opened behind the hunter-swap cards. That overlap is not hypothetical: behavior 16 exists precisely because settings can be opened while down, and the swap panel occupies the centre-left band the settings `Canvas` reaches into. 100001 also clears the feeds (CombatFeed 8500, QuestToast 8600, KillFeed 9000) and the damage overlays (SlowVignette 99998, DamageVignette 99999, DamageDirection / HealthPackPickup 100000) so combat feedback can't tint the panel. It stays **below** the full-screen match-transition layer (Top3 900000, VictoryShowcase 950000, round + cinematic fades 999999, MatchComplete 1000001) and below VersionTagController's 1000002 — a menu must not draw over a round transition, and the version tag is documented to sit above everything. Consequence of the jump: the bottom-left ping/FPS chip now renders *under* the panel's backdrop instead of over it, which only dims it (the chip and the centred panel don't overlap)
1b. **`_G.CloseSettingsMenu` is the one external close**, exported for TutorialClient (the onboarding lesson shuts the panel when the stats step starts). It is `closeOnLifeChange` itself, so it inherits the `isOpen` guard that keeps it off the Return-to-Bounty state's raised `DisplayOrder`, and it is a close rather than a toggle — nothing outside this script opens settings.

2. **Gear reveal:** unlike Bounty (which gates the gear on the lobby intro), Redwater reveals it immediately on load — the imported button is authored `Visible = false` and this sets it visible at startup
2a. **Inset space:** the ScreenGui is `ScreenInsets = None` + `IgnoreGuiInset = true` as of 2026-07-30, moved off `DeviceSafeInsets`. `SettingsButton` is pinned `{1, -10}` at anchor `(1, 0)`, so it sits 10px off the edge of whatever space the ScreenGui is in — that used to mean 10px off the *device-safe* edge, which on an iPhone 16 Pro Max in landscape put it 72px from the physical right edge, because Roblox reserves a 62px band on both short edges even though only one carries the cutout. It now sits 10px from the physical edge, and on a phone with no cutout nothing moves. `StatMenuGui` (MobileStatsButton) and `QuestCompleteNotification` were moved in the same pass and must stay in this space — QuestToastClient measures the button cluster in pixels off the same edge, and that clamp mismeasures silently if the panel and the buttons it dodges sit in different inset spaces
3. **Backdrop:** an oversized full-screen `Active` `Backdrop` frame behind the panel dims the scene, blocks click-through (so a click can't fall through to `RevolverInput` and fire a shot), and dismisses settings when the press lands **outside** the panel's absolute rect (`pointInPanel` guard). The outside-only check is deliberate: non-`Active` panel frames — notably the sliders — let a mouse-down fall through to the backdrop, so an unconditional close would let a slider drag dismiss the panel. This single backdrop replaces the legacy `SettingsBackdrop`
4. **PC mouse-lock (Alt):** Redwater locks the cursor to center for first-person aiming, so the top-right gear is unreachable until the cursor is freed. Holding `LeftAlt`/`RightAlt` frees it; releasing re-locks and force-closes the panel. Skipped entirely on touch devices, which tap the gear directly. Neither `InputBegan` nor `InputEnded` is gated on `processed` — a missed release can't leave the mouse stuck unlocked, and a press reported as game-processed can't silently swallow the unlock; `InputBegan` instead skips only while a TextBox is focused (the code box) via `GetFocusedTextBox`
   - **Mechanism (2026-08-11, third implementation):** the authored `AltUnlockModal` TextButton (1×1px, transparent, `Modal = true`, direct child of the ScreenGui) is made `Visible` for the duration of the hold. A visible Modal GuiButton is an engine rule — "the mouse will not be locked unless the right mouse button is down" — so the cursor comes free **without touching `CameraType`, `Player.CameraMode` or `MouseBehavior` at all**. The gameplay lock the hunter clients assert (`LockFirstPerson` + `LockCenter`) stays in force for the whole hold, merely suppressed while the button is visible; hiding the button on release is the re-lock. The camera stops turning during the hold because an unlocked mouse feeds no deltas (right-drag still turns it, per the same engine rule) — the rotation freeze the previous implementation built by hand
   - **Why a third implementation:** both shipped attempts (Hotfix 8 and its hardening) freed the cursor by fighting the camera scripts for ownership — snapshot four properties on press, drop `CameraMode` to `Classic`, take the camera `Scriptable`, re-assert `Default` every render step, then restore or re-derive it all on release, with dedicated death/respawn hand-off paths. Both still lost the first hold of a live session to the engine's own first-person mouse lock, which no `MouseBehavior` write can override while it is engaged. The Modal rule sits above that lock inside the engine itself, behaves identically in Studio and live, and holds no state that a death, respawn, round change or camera swap could invalidate — nothing is restored, so nothing can be restored wrongly, and the stranded-`Scriptable`-camera failure (open item #28) is unrepresentable
   - **The cursor icon is the one thing still managed:** gameplay keeps `MouseIconEnabled` false, so the hold re-shows it every render step (`AltUnlockCursorIcon` bind at `RenderPriority.Last` — per frame, not once, because a hunter client's `setGameplayMouseLocked(true)` can land mid-hold on respawn / round start and write it false again). On release it is set by **rule**, never from a snapshot: hidden if this client is a living player in a live round (`isAliveInRound`), shown otherwise — kill cam, intermission and victory showcase all leave the cursor free and re-assert their own input state anyway. Snapshots taken in one life and restored into another were exactly the old #26/#28 failure, so none is taken at all
4a. **Losing window focus ends the hold** (`WindowFocusReleased`). Alt-tabbing is a press whose release Roblox never delivers, which latched `isMouseUnlocked` true — the next press then early-returned and did nothing, and only its release re-locked, i.e. Alt appeared to need pressing twice
5. Generic slider driver (`bindSlider`) reused for mouse sensitivity, crosshair size, and all four volume sliders — one drag/release model. `setSliderVisual` insets the fill's leading edge by half the handle width so the golden bar never overhangs the track ends, then places the round handle a few px left of that tip (`HANDLE_LEFT_NUDGE`) so it reads as sitting on the end of the bar rather than poking a half handle-width past it
6. **Camera:** mouse sensitivity slider maps `[0.05, 1]` to `UserInputService.MouseDeltaSensitivity`; loads saved value via `MouseSensitivityRequest`/`Response`, saves on release via `SetMouseSensitivity`
7. **Crosshair color:** 8 preset swatches (colors + `SwatchIndex` attributes baked into the GUI); clicking sets `Player:SetAttribute("CrosshairColor", Color3)` which `HitMarkScript` observes and repaints live. Selection ring toggled via `UIStroke.Thickness`. Swatches are sized as a scale fraction of the row (not fixed px) so all 8 always fit within the panel on any screen
8. **Crosshair size:** slider maps 0-1 fraction to `[0.5, 2.5]` multiplier; sets `Player:SetAttribute("CrosshairSize", number)` (also read by `HitMarkScript`)
9. **HUD Show Ping / FPS:** creates a `PingFPSGui` `ScreenGui` (DisplayOrder 100) with a bottom-left `TextLabel`; `RunService.Heartbeat` sampler averages FPS over 0.5s windows and reads `GetNetworkPing`. Reuses a surviving overlay (`ResetOnSpawn = false`) so it isn't duplicated on reclone
9a. **The chip measures its text rather than predicting it** (ported from Bounty 2026-07-30; this place had been left on the original build). `AutomaticSize.X` plus symmetric `UIPadding` makes the dark plate exactly as wide as the current string — the old fixed 140×22 box was a guess at the widest `PING nnn | FPS nnn`, so the readout ran past its own backing. Text scales off a 720p baseline clamped to `[12, 20]`, with plate height and side padding derived from it, re-fitting on `Camera.ViewportSize` changes. One `buildPingFpsLabel` / `sizePingFpsLabel` pair serves both the startup path and `ensurePingFpsOverlay`'s rebuild
9b. **Touch devices take a further ×0.65 cut with a lower floor of 7** (`PING_FPS_MOBILE_TEXT_SCALE` / `PING_FPS_MOBILE_MIN_TEXT_SIZE`). The 720p baseline alone never reached any phone — `12 × 400/720` is 6.7, so every handset clamped back up to the floor of 12 and a 360-tall Galaxy drew the identical chip to a 720p monitor, the same "floor sets phone sizes" bug VersionTagController was tuned out of twice. Every phone goes text 12 → 7 and plate 113×22 → 68×13 (a 64% cut in area); tablets scale genuinely (iPad 10in 14 → 9, 13in 17 → 11); desktop is unchanged at every height because the gate is `TouchEnabled`, not viewport size
9b-i. **Tuned down twice — and the floor, not the multiplier, is the knob that moves phones.** The first pass (×0.8, floor 9) was a real reduction on paper (12 → 9, plate 22 → 17) and still came back from a device test as "did not get smaller" — 3px off a 12px stamp is a change visible in a diff, not in the hand. Because every phone clamps to the floor, the multiplier only ever shapes **tablets**: dropping 0.8 to 0.65 without also lowering the floor would have changed nothing on a phone. 7 matches the version tag's floor for the same reason; raise it first if the readout comes back unreadable
9b-ii. **`TouchEnabled` is read inside `pingFpsTextSize`, and a late flip is explicitly re-run.** Studio's device emulator flips the property a beat *after* client scripts start, so without a re-run the chip is built at the desktop size and stays there — indistinguishable from the mobile cut never having been applied. The other resizes fire only on viewport / probe changes and a late flip is neither, so a `GetPropertyChangedSignal("TouchEnabled")` connection (in a `pcall`) plus one `task.defer` pass cover it
9c. **The 8px left gap is conditional on the screen actually having a cutout to clear.** `PingFPSGui` sets `IgnoreGuiInset = true`, the legacy spelling of `ScreenInsets.DeviceSafeInsets` (verified by read-back). On a notched handset in landscape Roblox reserves a device-safe band on *both* short edges though only one carries the cutout — 62px on an iPhone 16 Pro Max — so the chip rendered 70px in from the physical edge; with no cutout that reserve is 0 and the same 8px was a plain margin against bare glass. `sizePingFpsLabel` measures the band live from two probe ScreenGuis (`PingFPSInsetProbeNone` / `PingFPSInsetProbeDevice`) and drops the gap to 0 when it reads 0. X only — the Y delta between those probes is the topbar offset, and reading it is the mistake that shipped twice in VersionTagController. Also touch-gated: a monitor likewise reports no inset, but edge space is only scarce on a handset, so desktop keeps its 8px. Both probes are named into RoundClientController's `ALWAYS_ENABLED_GUIS`, because a disabled ScreenGui stops laying out and reports `AbsolutePosition` 0; the reading is additionally sticky (only a `> 0` delta overwrites it)
9d. **`applyShowPingFPS` stamps a `ShowPingFPS` attribute on the GUI, and that is what makes the overlay survive a round boundary.** RoundClientController snapshots each ScreenGui's `Enabled` for intermission and restores it at round start; this chip is built `Enabled = false` and only switched on when `PlayerSettingsResponse` lands, so a player joining during intermission had the pre-settings `false` snapshotted before the payload arrived, and the snapshot's `== nil` guard meant the real value never replaced it — the chip then stayed dark for the whole match for anyone who had the setting turned on. The attribute is the player's actual preference at all times and RoundClientController restores from it by name instead, so the order the two land in stops mattering. The attribute key is duplicated in that script; changing it here alone silently reintroduces the bug
9e. The `ViewportSize` listener is **rebound, not stacked** — `CurrentCamera` is replaced outright by kill cams, spectating, the intermission idle camera and every respawn, which happens many times a match in this place, so the previous connection is dropped before a new one is made
9f. **HUD Ability Descriptions:** a three-mode segmented row (`ON` / `DEFAULT` / `OFF`) under the HUD header controlling HunterAbilityIntroClient's ability tags, persisted as the `AbilityDescriptions` setting. Published to that client as a **player attribute**, the same way `CrosshairColor` / `CrosshairSize` reach `HitMarkScript`, so neither script needs a remote of its own. (Plain text, not a wikilink — an attribute write is not a code-level connection.) The row is **two lines** — label above, three full-width buttons below — because a fixed-width three-button control does not fit: at a 705x338 viewport the row is 165px wide and a 176px control drove the label to a negative width and made it vanish. The active button is painted with the same `TOGGLE_ON_COLOR` the boolean toggles use, and the whole row is cloned from `ShowPingFPSRow` at build time so its font, colours and corner radius cannot drift from the row above it
9g. Unlike every other control, this one is **not** given a safe default before the server responds. `HunterAbilityIntroClient` treats a missing attribute as "show nothing", and seeding an optimistic `"Default"` would flash the tags at a player who has turned them **Off**; the fallback instead runs after the request retries are exhausted, and is idempotent if the payload already landed
9h. **INPUT Auto Fire (2026-08-07):** a boolean toggle row (`AutoFireRow`, cloned in Studio from `ShowPingFPSRow`) under its own `INPUTHeader`, between the CROSSHAIR and HUD groups, persisted as the `AutoFireEnabled` setting (default **true**). `applyAutoFire` publishes the value as an `AutoFireEnabled` **player attribute** (plain text, not a wikilink — an attribute write is not a code-level connection), which each hunter client's MobileAutoFireController reads live, so there is no join-order handshake; the controllers treat an **absent** attribute as ON, because the attribute only exists once the settings payload lands and absent-means-off would kill auto-fire for every player's first seconds in a match. The row and its header are hidden on non-touch devices (`UIS.TouchEnabled`, re-polled for ~12s because Studio's device emulator flips the property late — the same reason HunterMobileFireButton polls); the `UIListLayout` collapses the invisible rows so no gap is left. Seeded `applyAutoFire(true)` alongside the other defaults and re-applied from the `PlayerSettingsResponse` payload
10. **Graphics Shadows:** toggles `Lighting.GlobalShadows`; default PC = on, mobile = off (detected via `TouchEnabled`, used only if the server returns nil)
11. **Audio:** four sliders drive `SoundService.SFX/Music/HitSound.Volume` with a master multiplier applied on top of each group (SoundService has no `Volume` property, so master is faked as `master * perGroup`, written client-local so each player mixes their own)
12. **Codes:** a `CodeTextBox` + `RedeemButton` fire `RedeemCodeRequest`; `FeedbackLabel` shows the success/failure message from `RedeemCodeResponse`
13. **Return to Bounty:** a red `LeaveButton` pinned to the panel's bottom edge (outside the `Content` scroll, which is shortened to make room) opens the `LeaveConfirmFrame`; confirming hides the panel (keeping `DisplayOrder` elevated so `ReturningLabel` shows above the HUD) and fires `LeaveToLobbyRequest`; cancelling closes the confirm. Closing the panel also hides the confirm frame
13a. **The confirm popup was rebuilt in the game's own style 2026-08-05** and its shape changed: `LeaveConfirmFrame` is now the **full-screen scrim** (black @ 0.5, `Active = true`) and the card itself is a `Dialog` child, so the buttons are looked up at `LeaveConfirmFrame.Dialog.ConfirmButtonRow` rather than directly under the frame. The script still toggles only `LeaveConfirmFrame.Visible`, and `Active` makes the confirm genuinely modal — previously the settings panel behind it stayed live, so a stray press could drag a volume slider through an open confirm. The old popup was a grey `GothamSSm` box at fixed `TextSize` with `AnchorPoint (0, 0)` against `Position (0.5, 0.5)`, which pinned its **top-left corner** to screen centre and hung it down-and-right across the crosshair swatches. It is now a dark-brown panel with the house gold-rope `UIStroke`, a header plaque straddling the top edge, a gold hairline rule and amber `STAY` / deep-red `LEAVE` buttons. Sizing is a fraction of viewport **height** via `UIAspectRatioConstraint` (1.8, `DominantAxis = Height`) with the dialog height floored at 190px — the button row carries its own 44px touch floor, and below a 190px dialog that floor grew the row upward into the body text. Button labels are deliberately equal length: `TextScaled` shrinks the longer of two labels, so the previous `STAY IN MATCH` / `RETURN TO BOUNTY` pair rendered at two different sizes and the long one wrapped on a phone. See `UI_STYLE_GUIDE.md` §5.5 for the full recipe
14. **Load flow:** applies safe defaults immediately, then fires `PlayerSettingsRequest` (retry x5, 1s apart) until `PlayerSettingsResponse` arrives — server also pushes proactively on `PlayerAdded`
15. **Save flow:** every control fires `SetPlayerSetting(key, value)` on release/click (sensitivity uses its own dedicated remote pair)
16. **Auto-close on death and respawn:** the ScreenGui is `ResetOnSpawn = false` (the gear must survive respawns), so nothing used to hide an open panel when the player died — it stayed up through the kill cam and over the hunter-swap cards, and once a hunter client re-locked the cursor on respawn there was no way to click it away. A `Humanoid.Died` watcher (re-armed on every `CharacterAdded`) closes it, and `CharacterAdded` itself closes it again — the cursor is already free during the kill cam because KillCamClient claims the camera on the death frame, so settings can legitimately be opened while down, and that panel would otherwise survive into a mouse-locked life. Both paths are guarded on `isOpen` so they never disturb the Return-to-Bounty state, which closes the panel by hand while deliberately keeping `DisplayOrder` raised for `ReturningLabel`. (Since the 2026-08-11 Modal reimplementation the Alt hold has no life-cycle connections of its own — it holds no camera or cursor state a life change could invalidate — so this pair is the only life-cycle wiring in the script)
17. **Responsive scaling:** a `PanelScale` `UIScale` (created under `Canvas` at startup) scales the whole panel uniformly toward a 1280×720 baseline — `math.min(viewport.X/1280, viewport.Y/720)` clamped to `[0.9, 1]`, re-applied on `Camera.ViewportSize` changes — so the panel's fixed-pixel controls (text, sliders, swatches, padding) stay proportional and don't overflow on small / mobile viewports. Desktop (≥ baseline) is unchanged at scale 1.0; the mobile floor is `0.9` (raised from an earlier `0.6`) so the panel renders ~50% larger and stays legible on phones instead of shrinking to a hard-to-read size. The panel's decorative `UIStroke`s (outer outline, header box, and the inner content-border rectangle) render proportional to this `UIScale`, so each stroke's authored thickness is divided by the current scale on every update — keeping its on-screen weight constant so the inner rectangle stays fully drawn instead of thinning to just its top edge on phones
